## What this branch is — VERSION ONE (owner's brief, 2026-11-05 22:10 EST)
The base, ground-level scene-creation setup. THE SCENE contains
exactly three things and nothing else:
- THE VOID — a vast, dark, relaxing emptiness. It is the page's own
  background (a radial gradient on the viewports). There is NO
  backdrop plane, no wall, no stage, no curtains.
- THE GRID GROUND — infinite. A wireframe grid floor in the manner
  of 3D software: ONE transparent plane (12000×12000, a single
  shader draw — the WebGL rider in the phone-hardening law below
  carries its size) carrying only the two-axis grid at the 92-unit
  measuring scale — no fill, no sheet, nothing beneath it; the
  void's own gradient shows through it. The lines end at per-tier
  distance windows whose ends are the tiers' own analytic 3:1
  crossings (Class N), dissolving across the last stretch by
  screen-door so no fragment ever paints below the floor — no
  edge, no silhouette, no rectangle, and no sub-floor paint at
  any distance. The grid renders from BOTH sides
  (no backface culling on the plane; the solids keep theirs, which
  is load-bearing): the floor exists across the director's full
  pitch range and the camera's full tilt range, from above and
  from below alike (convergence fix, 2026-11-06). (Sizes are governed by the
  phone-hardening law below and its WebGL rider.)
- NO CANDLE — REMOVED 2026-11-06 (owner's ruling, 12:44 EST: "I
  don't want any placeholder. I don't want any candle — just an
  empty space, a void, at most a ground floor." This supersedes his
  2026-11-05 "the candle stays" ruling.) The lamp is gone from the
  build entirely — stem, halo, core, its pool on the ground, the
  warm screen wash, and the camera's keep-out disc around its seat.
  Nothing in the scene depicts a light source; the shading is
  ambient, in the materials.
- THE CAMERA — an object in the world, not a viewpoint: a dark paper
  camera (plinth, body, lens barrel with blue glass, viewfinder,
  dial) standing on the ground. Its position, heading, tilt and lens
  are state — moved by hand (below) or by the designer's instruction.

## The two views and the picture-in-picture
- The world is built ONCE — one scene from one builder; both
  views are render passes over it (the corner is a further pass
  over the same scene, the gizmo a pass over its own mini-scene),
  and one state drives them all, so the views can never disagree
  about the scene. (Fix round 8 wording, E-1: the CSS era built
  the world twice, once per view; the migration record's addendum
  carries the correction.)
- MAIN VIEW shows the chosen role: DIRECTOR (the free working view —
  six numbers: yaw, pitch, roll, panX, panY, dolly; they HOLD where
  left, no spring) or CAMERA (the shot, seen through the camera's
  eye — a first-person view that itself moves, below).
- THE CORNER (PiP, top-left) always shows the OTHER eye, live, with
  a tag naming it (CAMERA or DIRECTOR — the CAMERA tag also
  carries its lens length, labelled LENS, the same readout the
  main camera view shows in its chip). On short screens (height
  ≤480px — landscape phones) the corner takes the small 178×112
  size, the same as narrow portrait, so it never covers half the
  scene. While the stage is empty the corner is hidden and measures
  zero; it is refit to its real box in the same pass that brings it
  back (applyRoles calls fit), so a resize during the empty state
  cannot leave it scaled for a box it never had. Top-left because the bottom
  of the screen belongs to the ground action, the hint, and the
  camera's usual seat. A clean TAP on the corner swaps the views.
- Ranges (owner's full-freedom ruling, 2026-11-05 23:57 EST — the
  complete standard navigation set, researched and documented in
  assets/plan/navigation-research.md): director yaw CONTINUOUS
  (unclamped, full circle and beyond; home flights wrap to the
  nearest equivalent), pitch −89°..+89°, roll ±180° (two-finger
  twist), pan ±1600/±1000, dolly −6000/+1000 (apparent size ≈21×
  at the near end; the +1000 cap is the perspective ceiling — past
  1050 the viewed centre crosses the eye plane and inverts).
  Camera object:
  x/z −3000..3000, heading the full circle, tilt −70°..+70°,
  roll ±180°, focal 240..1700 (its view's perspective, in world
  units). Home: yaw 0, pitch −6, roll 0, pan 0/6, dolly 60; camera
  at (742, 150), heading −107.3° (aimed at the stage's centre point
  CENTRE (430, 53)), tilt 6°, focal 800.
- THE EYE RIG: the world shifts by (500 − cam.x, 320 − EYE, −cam.z)
  so the eye sits at the rotation centre, then the rig applies
  perspective(focal) rotateZ(roll) rotateX(−tilt)
  rotateY(180° − heading) — the exact inverse of the camera's
  placement. Perspective lives inside the rig transform strings, in
  world units, never in a viewport property, so both views agree
  on field of view at any size.

## Moving the camera object (the manual mechanics)
- In DIRECTOR view, a drag that starts ON the camera object moves
  it across the ground (screen deltas un-projected through the
  director's yaw/pitch and apparent scale). "On the camera" means
  its drawn faces OR its GRAB PADS: two crossed invisible planes
  (front 134×89, side 118×89 — the drawn body's measured union,
  118 wide × 73 tall × 102 deep, plus 8 units a side and centred
  on that union; the original
  236×180 pads reached ~70 units past the body and captured drags
  aimed at empty space, shrunk in the 2026-11-06 navigation
  upgrade and re-measured to the silhouette itself in convergence
  fix round 3; the contact shadow is an instrument print and is NOT
  part of the hit set) in the camera's group — the pick proxy every 3D tool
  uses, because the drawn object at working distance is a ~40px
  target no finger can hit reliably (owner's verdict, 2026-11-06).
  While a drag holds the camera, a pale RING shows on the ground
  around its feet. (The lamp keep-out disc that once guarded the
  camera's moves is gone with the lamp — the camera is the only
  object, so nothing stands for it to collide with.) A double-tap
  ON the camera object FOCUSES it: the director flies so the camera
  sits centred, close (pan AND dolly solved from the rig's own
  rotation chain — the object seats at a fixed depth of 500 in
  front of the perspective eye). The flight is solved from the
  camera's CURRENT position (what the director sees, not its
  targets) and clamped to the flight's own working bounds — pan
  ±4700 and dolly −6000..5200, derived from the seating envelope
  (the rotated offset to the farthest seat is at most ~4613 long,
  so no solved component can exceed it) — so the visit centres
  exactly at any seat in the camera's range; the director's
  gesture clamps are untouched law for gestures — and after a
  flight lands a pan/dolly target beyond them, gesture writes
  follow the NO-SNAP rule (convergence fix, 2026-11-06): the
  effective bound is the wider of the law bound and the current
  target on the side approached, so movement back toward the
  legal range is always free, movement further out is capped at
  the current value, and the first gesture after a far visit
  moves by its own input, never snapping the target to the wall.
  RESET STAGE writes the director's yaw LITERALLY to 0
  (convergence fix round 3): flyHome's nearest-equivalent wrap
  lands on an equivalent, not on 0 itself (777° → 720°), and the reset's
  contract is field-for-field identity with the boot state.
  The test-view YAW value wraps by true modulo at the numParam
  door like heading (convergence fix round 3): gesture yaw in
  use stays continuous and unclamped, but a finite-but-huge
  admitted yaw (1e308) could never be wrapped home — flyHome's
  step is absorbed by a float that large — so no URL value can
  strand the home flight. A double-tap on empty space flies
  home. A double-tap is two genuine taps inside ONE tap geometry
  — TAP_DIST 40px and TAP_WINDOW 300ms, the same pair feeding the
  double-tap action, the one-hand-zoom arming and the tap test —
  and the two landing surfaces run SEPARATE tap chains
  (convergence fix, 2026-11-06): a tap ON the camera object seeds
  only the camera chain, which only a second on-camera tap can
  complete (the focus visit), and a tap on empty space seeds only
  the general chain — neither chain seeds or completes the other,
  so a camera tap can still never hijack the next drag or arm an
  unasked home flight (the navigation upgrade's N1 cure stands),
  and a MODIFIED tap (Shift / right button) is invisible to both
  chains: it can neither seed, complete, nor break either one.
  An elapsed interval counts only when it is NON-NEGATIVE as well
  as inside its window (fix round 9, W3 Seat 1 D-1): every
  elapsed-time window test in the file — the two chain tests, the
  tap test, the one-hand-zoom arming and the corner's swap test —
  carries its >= 0 leg, so a backwards clock satisfies no window;
  the >= 200ms hold-proof was already fail-safe in that direction
  (a backwards step can only delay it), and the frame loop's
  elapsed step is clamped at zero by the same root, reading a
  backwards step as an ordinary frame. The census of every
  elapsed comparison in the file stands in the fix-round-9
  record.
  Invisibility is enforced at RELEASE as well as at seeding
  (convergence fix round 2, 2026-11-06): a non-primary release
  (right or middle button) returns before any chain bookkeeping,
  so it cannot wipe a chain it was never allowed to touch; a
  genuine primary-button drag still breaks both chains, and a
  cancelled pointer still counts as a genuine non-tap.
  The DOOR admits exactly TWO buttons (convergence fix round 5,
  V2-1): the primary (0) and the documented pan modification (the
  right button, 2). Every other value — the middle button (1), the
  X1/X2 side buttons (3, 4), and any button a future device adds —
  is refused at the door AS A CLASS: never registered — no
  pointer-map entry, no capture, no mode decision, no chain
  contact — and its release reaches nothing, returning at the
  pointer-up membership guard. (Round 4's guard enumerated the
  middle button alone, curing round 3's leftover — the mode
  decision resolving a middle press to a look, so a middle drag
  orbited the director and looked in the camera view; verification
  round 2 then walked the side buttons straight past the
  enumeration: a button-3 drag orbited, and on the camera it
  grabbed.) The right button and Shift remain the documented pan
  modification: they register, and are invisible to the chains
  only. DECIDE-ONCE ROUTING (convergence fix round 5, V2-2): the
  main move routing reads the DOOR'S RECORD — downMod, written
  once at the drag's landing — plus the Shift key, and NEVER the
  live button mask: a primary button chorded into a right-button
  pan once flipped the mask to 3, failed the routing's live
  equality test, and converted the pan into an orbit mid-flight,
  against the pointer-down's decide-once law. Shift stays the one
  live input, by design: it is the documented modification
  itself, consulted identically at the door and at the routing —
  pressing Shift mid-drag converts a look into a pan and
  releasing it returns to the look — while a drag that LANDED
  modified (downMod) stays a pan for its whole life, even if
  Shift is released mid-drag. Under a chord, the gesture belongs
  to the pointer, not to a button: the first release of either
  admitted button ends it (a non-primary release leaves both
  chains untouched), and the still-held button's later moves are
  inert, its release a guarded stray. DECIDE-ONCE COMPLETED (convergence fix round 6,
  V3-1 + V3-2): round 5 converted the ROUTING to the door's record; two
  consumers still read the live event, and round 6 converted both.
  (1) THE RELEASE RESOLUTION (V3-1): endPointer's chain-invisibility
  return now fires when the release is non-primary OR the gesture was
  decided modified (downMod) — a right-button pan drag chorded by the
  primary and ended by the primary's release first carried a primary
  release event, fell past the event-only test into the resolution,
  and disarmed both chains; a chain seeded before a chord now
  completes after it in BOTH release orders, for both chains.
  (2) THE CAMERA VIEW (V3-2): the modification means PAN IN BOTH
  VIEWS (designer decision, Archer) — a modified drag in the camera
  view spends itself on that view's pan-equivalent, the camera's own
  strafe (along its right vector) and fly (along its look), at the
  SET.pan gain through the focal-aware scale and the no-snap clamp,
  aim untouched; before, the camera-view look branch never read
  downMod at all, so the two admitted modified presses silently
  looked at full primary gain. THE CENSUS THAT PROVES THE CLASS:
  every remaining read of a live event property (e.button /
  e.buttons / e.shiftKey) in the move/up/cancel handlers of both
  surfaces is enumerated in the fix-round-6 record with its
  disposition — zero unconverted consumers; the survivors are the
  door itself (which WRITES the record), Shift read live at the
  routing (the documented modification itself, consulted
  identically at the door and at the routing, in both views now),
  the release's own button (which classifies the ending release,
  never the gesture), and the keyboard's Shift step (a key
  handler, its own documented input). e.buttons is read NOWHERE. The tap chains are BORN DISARMED (same
  round): both last-tap stamps initialise to −Infinity, and
  every clearing write — role-boundary cancellation, a
  completed double-tap, the non-tap resolution — writes
  −Infinity, never 0; a stamp of 0 was a live timestamp for the
  first TAP_WINDOW after the time origin, so a clock starting
  near zero (the file's own synthetic driving surface) could
  arm the one-hand zoom on a tap that never happened.
  The one-hand zoom (double-tap-hold slide) arms only after the
  second press is held nearly still for 200ms near the first tap.
- TWO-FINGER CHANNEL LAW: a two-finger gesture locks to ONE
  dominant channel — zoom, pan or roll — classified from its NET
  displacement from the landing (never a sum of absolute per-frame
  steps: jitter that displaces nothing locks nothing), with a 4°
  deadzone under which twist never counts as roll. A plain pinch
  never rolls or pans by bleed. The director's pan is screen-space:
  its deltas are divided by the fit scale, so the pan tracks the
  finger at any view size, like moveCam and the camera strafe.
  THREE-FINGER SLIDE zooms the camera's lens in BOTH views (the
  lens belongs to the object); the focal length is always named —
  in the corner tag while directing, in a chip in the camera view.
- Dragging INSIDE THE CORNER drives whichever eye the corner shows:
  one finger aims it (the camera's heading/tilt when the corner is
  the shot — aim by what you see in the corner, that is its
  purpose; the director's orbit when the corner is the director)
  through the same full ranges as the main view. Wheel OR PINCH
  over the corner zooms that eye: the camera's lens (focal) or the
  director's dolly. The corner keeps its own pointer map and its
  own active counter; a TAP that swaps the views is strict — one
  finger, under 8px of travel, under 300ms — so a fine aim nudge
  can never swap by accident. AIM AT CENTRE re-aims the camera at
  the stage's CENTRE from wherever it stands.
- ROLE BOUNDARY (navigation upgrade, 2026-11-06): every role
  application — view pills, corner swap, FIND CAMERA, RESET
  STAGE, ADD CAMERA, the test hook — first CANCELS the live
  gesture WHEN THE APPLIED LAYOUT ACTUALLY CHANGES (the role or
  the camera's presence; convergence fix, 2026-11-06): a
  re-application that changes nothing — re-tapping the lit pill,
  FIND CAMERA while already directing (the focus still fires),
  the hook set to the current role — cancels nothing and leaves
  a live drag untouched; RESET STAGE forces the cancellation
  always, because it replaces state wholesale. On a genuine
  change: both pointer maps emptied, capture released per
  pointer, mode / channel lock / both tap chains wiped, the
  activity counters recomputed from the maps (the frame loop
  also recomputes them every frame, so they can never stick).
  Gesture state earned in one role never fires in another, and
  the camera-move branch additionally refuses to run outside
  the director view or with no camera present. Pointer-up
  handlers on both surfaces are membership-guarded (an up for
  an unknown pointer changes nothing); capture is attempted
  only after the pointer enters its map, in its own protected
  step, and released as it leaves. A resize during a live
  gesture reseeds the gesture's baselines from the fingers'
  stored positions — and only a fit whose MEASURED inputs
  changed (the two view scales, the orientation) may trigger
  that reseed (convergence fix round 2): applyRoles refits on
  every pass, so without the measured gate a no-op
  re-application (lit-pill re-tap, same-role hook call, FIND
  CAMERA while already directing) silently cleared a live
  pinch's channel lock and the pinch reclassified on its next
  move; a genuine resize reseeds exactly as it always has. Test-view URL values are clamped to their
  law ranges, and clamp() maps every input to its NEAREST legal
  value (+Infinity to the high bound, −Infinity and NaN to the
  low bound), while heading-type values wrap by true modulo into
  (−180, 180] for any magnitude, so no path can poison or strand
  state (convergence fix, 2026-11-06).
- Main-view gestures, DIRECTOR: 1-finger drag = orbit (yaw
  continuous, pitch −89..+89), shift/right-drag = pan, 2-finger
  drag = pan, pinch/wheel = dolly, 2-finger TWIST = roll.
- Main-view gestures, CAMERA (first-person): 1-finger drag = look
  in place (heading full circle, tilt −70..+70), shift/right-drag =
  pan the camera itself (strafe sideways + fly forward/back — the
  modification means pan in both views, round 6), pinch/wheel = fly
  forward/back along the look, 2-finger drag = strafe sideways +
  fly on the vertical, 2-finger twist = roll the shot, double-tap
  = level the roll and re-aim at the centre.
- The frame loop chases every number toward its target with a
  time-based constant (k = 1 − e^(−dt/τ); τ from the Smoothing
  setting; τ tightens to 0.05 s while a pointer is down) and snaps
  the last 0.001 exactly onto the target, so at rest every current
  EQUALS its target and the page writes no styles (a pure
  exponential approach never arrives — round 5, 2026-11-06).
  Angular targets that wrap (heading, both rolls) are re-wrapped
  at write time and chased by wrapped deltas with the same snap,
  so turns always go the short way; the director's yaw is the one
  continuous exception, and its home flight wraps explicitly.
  THE WRAP IS ONE FUNCTION (convergence fix round 5, V2-3):
  wrap180 is true modulo at the function — it lands in
  [−180, 180) for any finite magnitude — and every angle wrap in
  the file runs through it (14 call sites: aimCentre, flyHome,
  the heading/roll gesture writes, the pinch classifier, the
  corner aim, and the frame loop's six seam-chase sites, whose
  inline copies of the old shifted formula were retired into
  calls, so no copy of the fragile formula survives to be fed an
  unbounded input by a future edit). The two test-view doors
  keep their own inline true-modulo variant by design: it lands
  in (−180, 180], the half-open side their door contract
  states. The old formula silently left its range below −540°
  (JavaScript's % keeps the dividend's sign) — that is how
  flyHome, the one unbounded caller, once spun the long way
  home from any yaw past a turn and a half.
  Resize fits are queued one per frame (rotation bursts cost one
  fit, not one per event); applyRoles and init still fit directly.
  The hint's fade clock is one engine-scope timer: a press cancels
  a pending fade, and any pass that rewrites the hint's text
  (applyRoles) restores it to full strength and restarts the
  clock.

## THE SEED + THE SPLIT (owner's orders, 2026-11-06)
- THE SEED is the empty-stage state: void + wireframe grid, NO
  objects — stored as a SEED constant in Layer 2. RESET STAGE
  applies it — and homes the director's view to the checkpoint's
  opening view (targets set home, currents snapped onto them),
  and normalises the absent camera's numbers to the boot values
  (CAM_HOME, level roll, the 800 lens, currents snapped onto
  targets), so the reset reproduces the checkpoint exactly —
  view and hook-visible state alike, field for field, whichever
  path reaches it (convergence fix, 2026-11-06); ADD CAMERA (view bar, seed state only) brings the
  camera back AT its home — it enters there, its currents snapped
  to the home state, never gliding in from wherever it was when
  removed — and every camera-writing gesture channel (the
  three-finger lens slide, the corner's aim and lens writers) is
  present-guarded, so a gesture on the empty stage cannot mutate
  the absent camera. The corner and camera controls leave
  the screen while the stage is empty. Industry seeds keep a
  camera (Unity/Blender); this one deliberately does not — the
  seed is the space itself. The seed FILE (assets/seed/
  stage-seed.html) is GENERATED from stage.html by
  assets/plan/make-seed.py (one injected flag block) after every
  stage.html change — never hand-edited, so the fork can't drift.
  Since the migration the press also writes a byte-identical
  shadow copy of the vendored bundle at
  assets/seed/assets/three-vendor.js — the path the seed's own
  script tag resolves from its folder — so the seed keeps its
  byte equality (stage.html + the block, nothing else) and still
  runs standalone from a bare file:// open.
  The injected flag BLOCK is four lines, 294 bytes: a three-line
  generated comment naming the source sha, plus the
  window.__STAGE_SEED__ script line (convergence fix round 3 —
  the block was measured; the earlier one-line description in
  this file and in the press's docstring is corrected).
- THE SPLIT: stage.html's script stands in three bannered layers —
  LAYER 1 ASSETS (the visual layer: builders + scene), LAYER 2
  STATE (every number, incl. CENTRE, CAM_HOME, SEED), LAYER 3
  MECHANICS (the engine: rigs, loop, controls — consumes state and
  element handles only). No mechanics above the Layer 3 banner, no
  scene code below it; the mechanics block is the lift-out engine
  for future two-file forks.
- BOOT ORDER: the frame loop starts at the END of init(), and the
  control wiring runs in isolated groups — no frame ever renders
  an unwired state, and one failed binding cannot kill the rest.
  The isolation is TOTAL (convergence fix round 3): the grain
  builder is guarded (a refused 2d context leaves a plain void
  and a working script), the frame loop schedules its next frame
  BEFORE its body runs (a throw costs one frame, never the
  loop), init's pre-wiring segment (fit, seed boot, first role
  application) is guarded so the wiring groups still register,
  and a tab-hide or window blur cancels any live gesture through
  the role boundary's own cancellation — no ghost fingers.

## Phone-hardening law (2026-11-05 diagnosis — the camera in pieces)
No element in the scene may exceed 4096px on either axis (phone GPU
texture caps; the 30000×20000 far plane and the 6000×4400 ground
were the best-fit cause of the camera disintegrating on the owner's
Android while desktop rendered fine — duplicated across two worlds
at DPR ~3). Solids are built face-by-face directly in their
parent's space (solid()) — no per-part preserve-3d wrapper level —
and no will-change on the rig. Coordinates stay within ±3600 so
mobile float precision is never strained — with one named
exception: the focus flight's solved pan targets work to their
own ±4700 envelope (derived from the seating range in the
manual-mechanics section above). Those are flight TARGETS only,
never scene geometry; every element's own coordinates stay
inside ±3600 (convergence fix round 3 — the blanket sentence
here previously read as if no number in the file could exceed
±3600, which the flight envelope already contradicted).

WEBGL RIDER (fix round 8, 2026-11-06, E-2): the caps above are
CSS-era law — they governed DOM elements and textures, and the
disintegration they answered belonged to the CSS 3D compositor
the migration retired. The WebGL render layer holds exactly two
oversize geometries, both single-draw shader surfaces carrying
no texture at all: the grid plane (12000×12000 world units, its
paint windowed by the Class N distance cuts) and the dome
(radius 9000, a gradient shader). Their sizes are set by the
cutoff distances and the far plane, not by any texture cap, and
they are the standing exception, named. The 4096px / ±3600 caps
STAND for everything else — any textured surface, any
per-element geometry, any future scene element — and a new
oversize shader geometry joins this rider by name or does not
ship.

## PERCEPTION (owner's phone-pass verdict, 2026-11-06 — classes N, O, P)
Classes A–M verify mechanics, documents and words. None of them could
SEE the stage: on sha d9e5b47c they all read zero while the grid
composited to 1.40:1 against the void and no orientation cue existed —
the owner's phone pass scored the build ~10/100 and failed it. These
three classes close that hole, permanently. The industry basis
(Blender / Unity / Unreal / three.js / SketchUp / WCAG §1.4.11) is
researched and named in records-20261105-stage3d/
stage3d-perception-research.md.

N. RENDERED LEGIBILITY. Every reference-layer paint (grid tiers, axis
   lines, origin mark) is audited on its COMPOSITED colour: paint
   colour at paint alpha, over the void colour adjacent — alpha
   itself is never distance-scaled inside a tier's window: each
   tier paints at its full alpha or not at all, its distance
   window ending at the tier's own analytic 3:1 crossing, the
   last 350 units a screen-door dissolution that holds surviving
   fragments at full alpha (fix round 8, N-3 — a smooth fade
   passes through every sub-floor alpha on its way to zero and
   was retired for exactly that). The floor is 3:1 (WCAG 2.1
   §1.4.11 — graphical objects required to understand the
   content) inside the windows, in both views, at shipped
   defaults. The census is COMPUTED from the paint values in
   the bytes AND measured on the rendered frame by the pixel
   hook, at p95 and p99 against the region median in the named
   regions (full, centre, ground, horizon): true WCAG — sRGB
   linearised before the luminance weights — the hook reading
   the page's own drawing buffer, its viewport seated in CSS
   pixels, on a settled state (fix round 8, N-1/N-2: the
   hook's three unit cures — formula, viewport, target — stand
   in its reason in stage.html). A nominal colour
   is not a composited one (the 2026-11-06 failure: hue 12.17:1
   solid, α 0.16 → 1.40:1 composited, 1.00:1 past the mask's zero),
   and a screenshot opinion is not a census.
O. ORIENTATION — THE GLANCE TESTS. From a still frame and from the
   live view, a first-time user must be able to answer, from visible
   cues alone: which way is up · where is the horizon · which way am
   I facing · where is the origin. Every cue carrying an answer is
   inventoried and its presence verified in the bytes: grid hierarchy
   (major/minor) · axis lines through the origin, colour-coded and
   sign-readable · a world-anchored horizon (the void's bright band
   tracks pitch; a screen-fixed vignette is NOT a horizon) · a
   heading readout in the chrome · an orientation gizmo in the
   chrome · the origin mark — the ground's own ring where it
   stands in frame, and its edge marker where it does not (fix
   round 8, O-3: a ring-and-dot seated on the frame's edge along
   the origin's true bearing, so the origin is locatable from
   every director frame — in frame, or as a bearing). A glance question with no cue is a
   defect of this class, counted like any other.
P. SCORING GATE. Every delivery face scores the build on five
   perception dimensions, each 0–100 with its evidence type named
   (census / glance test / inventory): reference visibility ·
   orientation legibility · scale legibility · state legibility ·
   control discoverability. The face's front score is the WEAKEST
   dimension, never an average. A build whose weakest dimension is
   unmeasured, or below the owner's accept bar (>50), is never
   presented as passing — whatever classes A–M read. Certification
   requires N and O at zero defective, the mechanics classes
   re-verified on the same bytes, AND the weakest dimension above
   the bar on the owner's phone pass.

Q. FORM FIDELITY — THE OWNER'S WORDS ARE THE SPEC (owner ruling by
   catch, 2026-11-08 ~13:09 EST: the location DROPDOWN, ordered by
   name three times on 2026-11-07 — 18:22, 22:51, 23:22 — shipped
   as a shelf of chips plus menus buried in a panel; the owner
   found the substitution himself, twice). Every surface's FORM
   (dropdown, button, menu, panel, pill) is specified by the
   owner's own words in the record; a form word is an order, not
   an approximation of the model it carries.
   · BRIEF RULE: every build brief carries an OWNER'S WORDS block —
     for each surface the build touches, the quoted directive
     (with its date) that names the surface's form. A surface with
     no quoted word and no owner-approved form decision does not
     build.
   · TRANSLATION RULE: planning may translate the owner's MODEL
     into architecture; it may never translate his FORM words into
     different forms. A design that wants a different form puts
     the form choice to the owner BEFORE it builds; silence never
     adopts it.
   · VERIFY SEAT: for every surface, the record names the quoted
     word it implements and the rendered evidence (the control's
     actual kind, seen in the served page at phone size). A
     mismatch — any form other than the named one — is a class-Q
     defect, counted like any other, and BLOCKS delivery. The
     check is mechanical: the quote either matches the rendered
     control's kind or it does not.
   · PATTERN THIS GATE EXISTS FOR: when the owner's specification
     and the builder's design diverge, the builder's layer ships
     its own form and reports the model's success (the caption
     plate after the word-animation reference, 2026-10-23; the
     derived framing held against his hand framing, 2026-11-07;
     the shelf for the dropdown, 2026-11-08). Class Q is the
     mechanical catch for that pattern: quoted word vs rendered
     kind, every surface, every delivery.

R. CLARIFY BEFORE BUILD — A DIVERGENCE IS A QUESTION, NOT A
   DECISION (owner ruling by catch, 2026-11-08 ~13:22 EST; it
   mechanizes his standing order of 2026-10-29 — "if his ask is
   not 100% clear, I must ask clarifying questions before
   building; no questions asked means it was 100% clear" — which
   stood ten days without a gate, and its companion of
   2026-10-31: a conflict between the owner's words and
   Archer's plan is raised the same turn, never silently
   decided). The instance: the screenplay plan WROTE the
   divergence down ("What B rejects: the drop-down…") — both
   versions in one document — chose its own, and spent a build,
   five suites, the owner's catch, and a rebuild, where a
   one-line question would have cost seconds.
   · TRIGGER: any point in Observe or Plan where (a) the
     build's chosen form, scope, or reading diverges from the
     owner's words in context, or (b) his words admit two
     readings the build must choose between. A divergence the
     planner can see is a divergence that must be asked. Doubt
     is the trigger, not the exception.
   · THE QUESTION IS THE CHEAP INSTRUMENT: price both sides —
     a clarifying question costs one line in the owner's
     action-list format, comparative (a CHOICE between the two
     concrete forms or readings, never a yes/no), riding the
     next message or TLDR unless it blocks the build in
     flight, when it goes immediately. Pricing the cost of
     asking (his attention) without pricing the cost of NOT
     asking (build + suites + catch + rebuild) is the defect
     this class exists to kill.
   · CARVE-OUT — the designer rule stands: divergences INSIDE
     the craft (numbers, seats, timings, quality) are the
     designer's to decide silently. This gate fires only on
     the owner's own stated forms, scopes, and readings of his
     requests — the ground where he is the only authority.
   · RECORD: every build's Plan carries a DIVERGENCE LOG —
     divergences found · asked · silently decided. For
     owner-word divergences, silently decided must be 0; the
     Verify form seat checks the log against the brief's
     OWNER'S WORDS block (class Q).
   · FAILURE-COST LINE (owner, 2026-11-08 ~13:36 EST): every
     interpretive choice in a Plan is priced BEFORE Execute —
     if this reading is wrong: what restarts, what usage
     burns, does the work start over. The standing pre-action
     judgment priced the cost of asking and never the cost of
     being wrong; this line prices being wrong. A choice
     whose failure cost dwarfs the cost of asking (one line)
     and whose reading is not pinned to the owner's verbatim
     words is ASKED, not decided — the trigger above, priced.
     Worked example on the record: the drop-down reading —
     asking ≈ one line; wrong ≈ a surface built, five suites
     run against it, the owner's catch, and a rebuild inside
     the assembly build.
   · EXPECTATION STATEMENT — THE DIFF IS THE ALIGNMENT
     SURFACE (owner, 2026-11-08 ~13:24 EST, strengthening this
     class in the same hour it was written): the goal is to
     match the owner's expectation; matching requires
     understanding it; understanding is proved only by STATING
     it. Before Execute spends on any build that interprets an
     owner request, the build's face prints MY UNDERSTANDING
     of his expectation in his terms and his format (what he
     will see and get — forms, scope, behaviour) BEFORE the
     spend it describes, so the diff between his expectation
     and my understanding is visible to the only judge of it —
     him — while correcting it is still cheap. This fires even
     when NO divergence is noticed: class R's trigger catches
     the divergences the planner sees; the statement catches
     the ones he cannot see because he believes he is aligned.
     A statement buried in a plan file is not delivered; it
     rides the chat face in his reading format.
   · BETTER-CHANGE LICENCE: a change to his ask is permitted
     only when it is better BY HIS EXPECTATION (it serves his
     stated objective better) — and it is never self-adopting.
     It is presented as the diff (his ask · the proposed
     change · why it is better) and ships only on his word;
     his silence never adopts a change. A silent flip of any
     kind breaks the alignment contract, because his
     expectation stays pointed at what he asked and the
     delivery is judged against it; a silent flip into a
     WORSE version is, in the owner's own classification
     (2026-11-08), a direct act of sabotage.
