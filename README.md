## What the piece was

A 9-line teaser script, "A Book That Speaks Without Its Author":
around 1440, printing let a book be copied exactly, hundreds of
times, with no scribe and no author in the room. Its Phase 0 brief
credited Bi Sheng (~1041–48) and Korea's *Jikji* (1377) before
Gutenberg's system; its structure was Problem–Agitation–Solution,
declared before drafting; its own Phase 1 gate self-graded PASS —
and the owner's ear failed it anyway. That gate-vs-ear contradiction
is carried openly in this package (see
`render/verification.json`): it is the rejection's most reusable
lesson. The full rejected document — brief, declaration, gate,
scene/intent log, weak points and the 9 lines (121 mechanical
tokens / 118 spoken words) — is the evidence file at
`transcript/rejected-script-draft.md`, byte-identical to its
workspace source and unedited.
