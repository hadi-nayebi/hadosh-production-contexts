## Reviewer grading anchors: 0–10

Use whole-number grades. Enter a grade only with the reviewed build identity, viewing size, timecodes, concrete observations, and remaining defects. A source-only check or a still-image check cannot establish a final motion or audiovisual grade.

| Grade | Observable anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | The required treatment is absent, or cannot be viewed or heard. |
| 1 | The treatment exists but does not communicate its intended purpose. |
| 2 | Major inaccuracies, broken behavior or unreadability prevent understanding. |
| 3 | Some intent is recognizable; most of the passage needs substantial reconstruction. |
| 4 | Parts work, but material omissions or conflicting visual information remain. |
| 5 | The treatment is functional and understandable, with obvious unfinished craft or reliability issues. |
| 6 | The treatment is coherent and usable; noticeable weaknesses still interrupt the experience. |
| 7 | The treatment is strong and consistent, with limited defects that do not overturn its meaning. |
| 8 | The treatment is very strong across the full part; only small, documented refinements remain. |
| 9 | The treatment is exceptionally clear, deliberate and consistent; the required evidence shows no known material defect. |
| 10 | The treatment is exemplary throughout the part, including its hardest transitions; every applicable criterion is supported by final viewing evidence and independent review. |

These grades are judgments of quality, not ratio-scale measurements. A change from one grade to another does not establish “10× better.” Competition readiness and the requested improvement remain review outcomes, not labels awarded by the production code or this document.

## Observable criteria for the ten dimensions

### 1. Storytelling

**Pass:** A viewer can explain how scale leads to useful work, how the origin explains the capability, and why external work leads to the invitation. The river sentence returns with a purpose. Each passage adds a cause, consequence, comparison or question.

**Fail:** Images merely decorate speech; a new motif displaces the story without explanation; the ending is disconnected; later parts provide labels without developed ideas.

**Evidence:** Watch the whole final film, then record a plain-language account of each part and its connection to the next. Identify the least understandable passage in each part and cite its timecode.

### 2. Pacing

**Pass:** The opening sentence registers before shrinking; the full title is readable near two seconds; comparisons receive reading time; concrete operations have a visible input and result; reflective holds are intentional. Visual emphasis follows the actual recorded words.

**Fail:** Clauses outrun their images; words flash too quickly; explanation depends on long decorative loops; later parts rush to compensate for the opening; the recording is forced into the provisional 143-second timeline.

**Evidence:** Review final audio and motion together at normal speed. Inspect word anchors, reading holds and the recorded intervals throughout all four parts. Record where attention is lost or a pause is needed to understand.

### 3. Visual coherence

**Pass:** Amber stock, blue processing, language tiles, relationships and work objects retain their meanings. Carried objects can be followed into their next role. Scene transitions have an identifiable purpose.

**Fail:** Objects change identity unexpectedly; a generic dissolve hides a missing causal connection; old and new scenes collide; unrelated icons replace the promised continuity.

**Evidence:** Inspect adjoining boundary frames and replay every scene and part boundary. Track the opening sentence, marked repeated-processing subgroup, search request, learned context, model and external work result.

### 4. Composition

**Pass:** Every frame has a clear primary subject, purposeful negative space, readable hierarchy and sufficient scale. Desktop and portrait staging preserve the same explanation without crowding or shrinking everything.

**Fail:** Important diagrams are tiny or faint; labels collide; elements crop; several foreground actions compete; a portrait layout loses the operation that was clear on desktop.

**Evidence:** Inspect all interval samples on desktop and every passage at its intended portrait size. Open originals for concerns found on contact sheets. Check the same relationships during movement.

### 5. Typography

**Pass:** Words, quantities, qualifiers and units remain readable during entry and at rest. The complete title resolves; the recurring sentence remains exact; changing words retain stable positions where continuity requires them. Labels support the image without reproducing the full narration.

**Fail:** Type overlaps or clips; changing word widths shift an unrelated prefix; low contrast conceals key information; only the numeral resolves; full narration captions replace visual explanation.

**Evidence:** Inspect the fastest opening checkpoints, all quantity labels, changing-word transitions and the end-card transition at intended size. Check exact spelling and the relationship between a label and its object.

### 6. Motion quality

**Pass:** Trajectories, acceleration, group behavior and entrances are deliberate. The same words become marks and letterforms; operations visibly transform inputs into results. Later parts receive comparable choreography. Pause freezes the intended state.

**Fail:** Jitter, abrupt unrelated replacements, constant bobbing or ornamental movement substitute for explanation; transitions create illegible piles; only the opening has developed motion.

**Evidence:** Watch every motion-beat group continuously, including starts, exits and handoffs. Review frame delivery as well as render cost. Still images and low drawing time alone do not prove smooth motion.

### 7. Clarity of explanation

**Pass:** The image distinguishes a reported monthly processing count from an estimated text stock, uses a common count unit, marks approximation, and shows repeated processing without consuming the source. Prediction, attention and external tool work remain distinct.

**Fail:** The image implies unique new text, measured productivity for every token, an exact empirical ratio from unlike quantities, AI beginning in 2017, measured attention weights where the example is illustrative, or direct model execution without external tools.

**Evidence:** Compare the viewer's likely inference with the exact narration and the cited source boundaries in the production review. Check arithmetic and geometric units. Ask a viewer to explain the operation without relying on its heading.

### 8. Narration and sound

**Pass:** One fresh, consistent performance preserves all 25 locked lines. Pronunciation, emphasis, breathing, sound design, mix level and scene timing work together. Audio and image remain aligned during play, pause and seeking.

**Fail:** Old narration is substituted; text is omitted or rewritten; inconsistent takes distract; sound masks speech; motion is timed to an absent or provisional voice; a silent build is called the final film.

**Evidence:** Listen to the complete final take, compare it word for word with the locked script, inspect the final cue map and test actual audio behavior. Fresh narration, sound design and final alignment are currently absent.

### 9. Technical playback

**Pass:** The HTML opens directly into the film. Play, pause, seek, chapters, restart, end state, fullscreen and keyboard access behave correctly in actual use. Controls appear when paused. Seeking produces the expected deterministic frame without accumulating playback loops.

**Fail:** A dashboard is the entry surface; assets fail silently; typed or selected seeks return to the previous value; restart duplicates loops; controls obscure playback; audio or verification is claimed when absent.

**Evidence:** Record actual browser interactions, build identity, sizes and errors. Test rapid control changes and end-state recovery. Keep source checks, still-frame checks and actual behavior results separate.

### 10. Balanced completeness

**Pass:** All four parts, 25 prose passages and 85 planned intervals have developed visual actions, checked transitions and final sound. The weakest later section receives the same care as the opening. The ending resolves the argument.

**Fail:** A polished opening hides storyboard-level later sections, missing actions, placeholder assets, incomplete sound, an underdeveloped invitation or unresolved material defects.

**Evidence:** Map every interval to actual evidence. Review each part independently, then compare the complete old and new films using the same ten dimensions. Record the weakest passage in each part and what was done about it.

## Weakest-part completeness rule

A film-wide average cannot compensate for a missing or materially failing part. For each dimension, review P1, P2, P3 and P4 separately; the lowest supported part assessment governs that dimension's readiness. An UNRATED part keeps final acceptance open.

A part cannot be called complete while it has a missing required action, an unresolved material defect, absent narration or sound, or an unverified necessary interaction. The more developed opening earns no exemption for the remaining parts.

Final acceptance requires a complete audiovisual viewing, an interval and transition review, actual playback evidence, and a documented comparison with the baseline. No numerical improvement, award outcome or competition acceptance has been assigned.
