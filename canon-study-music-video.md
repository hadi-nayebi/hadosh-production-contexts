# Canon study — "Who Owns the Toaster?" music video (watched 2026-10-28, browser study)

Source: Academy project page https://hadi-nayebi.github.io/projects/who-owns-your-toaster.html → video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fg2cV8sh3kY ("Who Owns the Toaster? | A Music Video Co-Created with AI", Let's Build It Together, 3:58, created/directed by Hadi Nayebi). Watched in full by scrubbed checkpoints; six timestamped frames described in the study report. This digest records the video's visual GRAMMAR for inspiration — not its lyric text, and not layers to copy.

## What it does, layer by layer (condensed)
- Form: glossy AI-assisted still illustrations joined by slow zooms/pans/dissolves; one protagonist (bearded man, headphones) in ONE recurring warm room as the anchor across eras — calendar and device changes mark time (1996 CRT → smartphone → flat screen).
- The harness made visible: small pale document icons and teal "{}" code tiles float in the air BETWEEN the user and the screen, linked by thin dotted lines — files living in the user's space, inspectable and keepable.
- The Digital Cortex in two registers: (a) functional — a dotted file-web radiating from the man's head/eyes toward the screen, captioned "Digital cortex — Your instructions, retained"; (b) emblematic — the end card: a dark faceless profile silhouette whose head dissolves into a glowing constellation of files and nodes. The emblem is the video's most iconic image.
- The chorus poster-word: in the night-sky passages, ONE huge cream serif word at a time fills the centre over a landscape of floating glass panels — the argument turned into a poster. (The direct ancestor of our word layer.)
- Palette: deep navy/black cosmic world; warm amber/wood domestic world; teal-cyan + pale yellow for file/node highlights. Cold digital vs warm ownership, deliberately contrasted.
- Motifs: documents dominate; dates/calendars as timeline; brand/interface history as shorthand. NO directory/folder structure is ever shown — files form a loose constellation, not a hierarchy.

## What to be inspired by (adopt the grammar, not the layers)
1. The emblematic cortex end-card: faceless thinker, head dissolving into the file-web — the Academy's signature image; our style should carry a refined version of this grammar (canon: files as nodes around the head).
2. Files in the user's space, between person and machine — the harness as a visible, ownable layer, never a hidden program or a glowing brain.
3. The poster-word: one large serif word, alone, as a visual hook — our word events at their calmest should still aspire to this poster clarity.
4. The recurring-place time machine: one space, changed by time — a low-cost, legible continuity device.
5. Palette kinship: our scenes palette (ink-navy/gold/cream) is a descendant of this video's cosmic/domestic contrast — record the lineage honestly; it was inherited via the trunk v2, and the derivation is NOW documented here.

## What to do better (the study's named weaknesses → our answers)
1. Slideshow stillness → our scene switches on the cadence rule + words in motion.
2. Three competing text layers (lyrics + labels + auto-captions) → ONE designed word layer; no caption band, no reliance on platform captions.
3. Ownership asserted, never demonstrated (files float but are never opened/edited/locked/moved) → show one concrete act per film where the story allows: a file changed, a rule refused, the layer carried elsewhere.
4. No directory structure → our information-keeping motifs add real branching/hierarchy (directories, index systems) to the constellation.
5. Generic AI gloss (plastic faces/hands) → our painterly authored illustration; faces kept out of close-up.
6. Repetition of one room/framing → our 33-scene arc varies place while the style holds.
