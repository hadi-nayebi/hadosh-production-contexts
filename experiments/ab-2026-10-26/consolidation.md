## §2 — Owner text rule recorded here (2026-10-26 ~13:24 ET, immediate)

**The word "lives" (L-I-V-E-S) is BANNED in any text destined
for synthesis** — the voices pronounce it as the plural of
"life," not the verb, mixing the meaning. Replace per context
(sits / runs / stays / is kept / exists) or restructure the line.
Application in this phase: the settled phase-11 text carries the
banned word twice (its L3 and L8 — quoted here as evidence, the
only place it may still appear); both candidates were written
under the ban from the first draft and machine-scanned before
this phase closed: **zero occurrences in either candidate**
(the shared L3 fix uses "sits"; neither candidate uses the verb
"live" either, so no near-miss rides into synthesis).

## §4 — Weighing against the objective

The objective (phase 8): "Make every AI user in the 1.2 billion
see a renter in the mirror — and ask who owns their layer." The
message: "You use it every week. You own none of it."

- **The mirror and the question** are carried identically by
  both (front half + vanishing test). No difference.
- **The naming's subtlety** — the owner's word was "subtle."
  V1's appositive is a naming and nothing more; the claim waits
  one beat. A cold viewer takes in ONE new idea per line, which
  is this lane's established rhythm (two parts first, then the
  opening — canon's presentation order). V2 asks the viewer to
  take the gloss, the name, and the possessive in one breath;
  its editor pass flags the load honestly. Advantage: **V1**.
- **Ownership's force** — V2's "and it is still yours," placed
  after a performed swap, is the single strongest ownership
  beat in either text; V1's claim is cleaner but cooler, and
  its swap remains a stated capability. Advantage: **V2**.
- **The destination** — the funnel this piece feeds assigns the
  close to the installed question and the bridge card (phase 9's
  end_referral). V1 ends its narration on the message and lets
  the relay ride as the bridge into that card; V2 ends on the
  invitation itself, spending the final line's force that the
  payoff just earned. For a piece whose job is the mirror and
  the question, advantage: **V1**.
- **The demonstration question** — V2 demonstrates (move it,
  swap the model, it persists). Demonstration is the funnel
  LONG-FORM's job ('Who Owns the Toaster?' is literally the
  worked proof); this short's job is to install the question
  that sends the viewer there. V2 pulls a piece of the
  long-form's work into the short. Advantage: **V1**, on lane
  discipline — recorded as a judgment, not a fact.
