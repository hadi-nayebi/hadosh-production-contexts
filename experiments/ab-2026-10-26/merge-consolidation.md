## §2 — The double-claim test (a merge, not a stack)

Three lines touch ownership: L8's "makes it yours" (inside the
naming description, inherited from the settled text), L9's
"belongs to you — not to them," and L11's "it is still yours."
Checked as a stack, they would be a defect. They are not a
stack: each performs a different speech act in the argument's
order — the naming line DESCRIBES (what makes it yours is the
layer), the claim line ASSERTS (the layer is yours, not
theirs), the proof line CONFIRMS under test (after the model
changes, it is still yours). V2's own design already ran this
two-step (its fused "your harness" + "still yours"); the merge
makes the assertion explicit between them because its naming
stays neutral. No other redundancy survives the editor pass
(§3): the locality idea appears once as instruction (L10) and
once as the relay's content (L13 — the Academy teaches the
how; a reference, not a restatement).
