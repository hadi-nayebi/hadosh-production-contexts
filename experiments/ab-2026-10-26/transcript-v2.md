## Editor pass (demanding-human-editor simulation, this candidate)

- L8 fuses two new ideas — the NAME and the CLAIM — into one
  beat ("— your harness"). It is the boldest line in either
  candidate and the riskiest: a cold viewer must absorb the
  gloss ("the layer around it"), the name, and the possessive
  in a single breath. The editor records the risk rather than
  smoothing it away: this fusion is exactly what the A/B exists
  to test at the owner's ear.
- L9 is the piece's only imperative. The register shift is
  deliberate (the alternative as an instruction, not a
  description), and it is placed where the architecture turns
  from diagnosis to remedy; an editor who rejects imperative
  turns in this lane rejects this candidate's spine.
- L10's "tomorrow" makes the swap concrete and near; "and it is
  still yours" lands the ownership payoff AFTER the proof —
  the strongest single beat in the back half of either text.
- L12 is the longest line in the piece (18 spoken words) and
  carries the owner's own terms ("control and transparency")
  un-paraphrased. It spends the final line's energy on the
  invitation; whether the piece should end on its verdict
  (V1) or its invitation (this candidate) is the structural
  question the A/B puts to the owner.
- The front half (L1–L7) is identical to V1's, including the
  text-rule fix at L3: the candidates diverge only where their
  designs diverge. The comparison is honest — the A/B tests the
  back half, not two different openings.
