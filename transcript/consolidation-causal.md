## §1 — The phase-8 pressure test, in full

Applied to phase 1's three decided fields (source / constraint /
substance):

- **Objective** — P1 value: "Show 1.2 billion weekly users rent
  AI access while owning none of the layer around it."
  SOURCE: fail in form — its recorded derivation opened the first
  draft's script doc and checked its statement.
  CONSTRAINT: fail — "Show X rents Y" is satisfied by any accurate
  third-person explainer, including one the viewer files as news
  about other people; the funnel purpose (this report feeds "Who
  Owns the Toaster?") requires the viewer to locate themselves in
  the number and leave carrying the ownership question, which the
  recorded value nowhere requires. SUBSTANCE: pass — the fact is
  the lane's founding sourced fact (OpenAI DevDay, 2026-09-29:
  1.2 billion weekly ChatGPT users) and the rent/own read is
  canon §4's ownership thesis applied to it.
  **Verdict: substance genuine, form reverse-fitted → re-derived**
  in viewer-change form (phase 8, metadata.json).
- **Message** — P1 defined it as the first draft's own closing
  line ("Adoption is total. Ownership is zero."). A payoff line
  defining the message is the reverse direction whatever the
  line's quality. **Re-derived** from the phase-8 objective
  (phase 8, metadata.json).
- **Audience** — P1's value was inferred from the first draft's
  exhibits. Under the re-derived objective ("make every AI user
  in the 1.2 billion see a renter in the mirror") this audience
  is *required*, not merely evidenced. **Value stands; derivation
  upgraded** from artifact-inference to prescription-requirement.

## §4 — The settled text + editor pass

The settled transcript is `transcript/transcript.md` (10 lines,
81 spoken words — machine-counted; the consolidation's first
hand-count of 83 was caught and corrected in this phase, see
the count note in transcript.md). Editor pass (simulated demanding human
short-form editor, applied to the draft before settling):

1. **One idea?** Yes — rent vs own, nothing else taught. The
   layer is named only as far as the payoff needs.
2. **First 3 seconds:** the fact itself, monumental on screen,
   mirror by line 2. No label, no throat-clearing. Pass.
3. **L3's "their chat box":** "their" = the provider's. Kept —
   the alternative ("a chat box") loses the owner, and the owner
   is the argument. The scene shows a generic box (no brand) so
   the generalisation is visual as well as verbal.
4. **L4's fragments:** three full-stop fragments risk sing-song
   delivery. Kept deliberately — they are inventory ticks, the
   punctuation IS the delivery instruction (each item lands as
   its object lands), and the pause map spaces them as beats,
   not as a list read aloud.
5. **L5's conditional past in L6** ("It was all rented") —
   grammatically the hypothetical's answer; reads as verdict.
   Kept.
6. **Navigation:** 0% of narration. The bridge is card-only.
   Pass.
7. **The fact:** L1 is precise to the source; the source line
   rides the number on screen with date and event. Pass.
8. **What the editor rejected:** an earlier drafting impulse to
   close L9 with "…and nobody can take it away" — rejected:
   unearned guarantee, and canon promises ownership of the
   layer, not immunity. The line ends at "your own machine";
   L10 does the closing work.
