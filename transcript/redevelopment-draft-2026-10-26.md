## §1 — The governing direction (the owner's own definition, 2026-10-26)

Connect prompt to the concept of **direction**: a prompt *has* a
direction. As the context is being produced by the agent — continued
and compacted — prompting is essentially giving **new directions**;
we shift the context in a particular direction by the intermediate
prompts. A prompt does not happen just at the beginning — it is
**accumulated across the job**. Capture all the prompts during a
session of a job and create a chained / end summary — a chain
summary — and you have a **mega prompt**: the prompt accumulated
throughout the work. In his words:

> "the prompt is not just one sentence, but it's all the directions
> that the user tries to re-steer the context in a different
> direction — that's more close to what I would describe prompt."

Two canon flags open since 2026-10-23 are resolved by this ruling
(recorded in `metadata.json`): `direction_wording` — "direction" is
the **structural** sense, and it is the piece's concept (the
provisional everyday-sense-only restriction, DR-3, is superseded);
`prompt_instructions_canon` — the canon meaning of "prompt" is
direction + accumulation. What the ruling does **not** settle: the
prompt↔Instructions boundary — the rejected text's L6 framing
("a prompt lives once") is false under his definition and is gone;
the new draft does not depend on the boundary.

The catalogue's "direction" thread is leaned on consciously: WD-01's
delivered line calls a token "the smallest piece of text that gets
one [a direction]". This piece now teaches where directions come
from: the user's prompts, accumulated.

## §2 — The draft (8 lines, 128 spoken words)

Beat map: (1) the flat picture, named fast → (2) his turn: a prompt
is a direction → (3) a performed steering moment → (4) the session
reality: context produced, continued, compacted; every prompt
re-steers → (5) accumulation → (6) the landing: chain the session's
prompts into one summary — the mega prompt — and the bookend: the
real prompt was never the first sentence.

- **L1** (14 words) — Most people think a prompt is the first sentence you type into the chat.
- **L2** (13 words) — It is not. A prompt is a direction — it points the context somewhere.
- **L3** (13 words) — Say: build me a page. That is one direction — the work starts moving.
- **L4** (12 words) — Then you steer again: warmer. Shorter. For my mother, not for engineers.
- **L5** (21 words) — Meanwhile the agent keeps producing the context — continuing it, compacting it — and every prompt you add bends it a new way.
- **L6** (19 words) — So a prompt does not happen once, at the start. It accumulates — direction after direction, across the whole job.
- **L7** (17 words) — Take every prompt from a session, chain them into one summary, and you hold the mega prompt.
- **L8** (19 words) — That is what a prompt really is: not one sentence — all the directions you gave to re-steer the work.
