## Phase 0 — mini study brief (sourced facts; uncertainty flagged)

1. **⚠️ THE DATE IS NOT KNOWN — and the script says so.** The
   origin of spoken language is one of the unsettled questions
   in human evolution. Published estimates range widely — from
   ~50,000 years ago to well over 150,000, with some researchers
   arguing for gradual emergence alongside early *Homo sapiens*
   (~300,000 years old, Jebel Irhoud fossils, Hublin et al.,
   *Nature*, 2017). The honest anchor is "more than a hundred
   thousand years ago," and L1 admits the uncertainty inside the
   piece itself ("nobody knows when") instead of faking a date.
   This follows the same convention Ep.2's brief adopted.
2. **What IS safe to claim:** before speech, an individual's
   knowledge could only be transferred by showing — pointing,
   demonstrating, letting another watch (exactly Ep.1's
   behavioural channel). Speech is the first channel that moves
   *detailed, unseen* information — a route, a plan, a warning
   about a place nobody present has visited — between minds at
   the speed of sound, to many hearers at once.
3. **⚠️ FLAGGED — animal calls are a partial counterexample.**
   Alarm calls and other animal signals also travel faster than
   a body. What they cannot carry is *new, detailed* information:
   a route with turns, a plan for tomorrow. The script's claim
   is scoped to information that can be *composed* — but the
   words "information" alone (L7) are slightly wider than this
   defence. Named in Weak points, not buried.

## Narrative declaration (N-04 / N-06 / N-03 — written BEFORE the draft)

- **Structure: And–But–Therefore** (clarity — the series spine,
  kept as the CONTROL structure so this teaser's tested variable
  is its opening device, not its structure). AND: speech lets
  information jump mind to mind in one breath (L4–L5). BUT: a
  spoken word dies the moment it is said, if nobody remembers
  it (L6). THEREFORE: the first voice was the first road
  information ever travelled (L8).
- **Intended feeling:** a held breath, then release — the wonder
  of speed arriving in a world that had only ever moved at
  walking pace.
- **Villain (N-06), named: The word that dies as it is spoken.**
  Consequence stack: (1) speech leaves no trace — the moment
  the sound ends, the information exists only in memory, the
  most fragile storage Ep.1 already indicted; (2) every hearer
  remembers a slightly different version, and there is no
  original to check against — errors begin at the speed of
  sound too. No dead solutions inside this world: gesture and
  showing (L3) are not failed solutions, they are the *only*
  predecessors — stated here per N-06.
- **The ONE most vivid detail, chosen on purpose (N-03):** one
  breath carrying a whole hunting route into ten minds at once
  (L4). Speed you can feel in your own chest.
- **Jargon push (one deliberate push only):** none enters. The
  calculated push in this piece is the admitted uncertainty in
  L1 — a "we don't know" placed in line 1, testing whether
  honesty itself can be the hook (see Variation).

## Script draft (9 lines, 120 words — counted mechanically)

1. More than a hundred thousand years ago — nobody knows when — a human first spoke a thought out loud.
2. Before speech, what you knew travelled only as far as your feet.
3. You could point. You could show. That was all.
4. Then one breath carried a whole hunting route into ten minds at once.
5. Knowledge now outran the fastest runner alive.
6. But a spoken word dies the moment it is said — if nobody remembers it.
7. Say it plainly: speech let information jump from mind to mind, in a single breath.
8. The first voice was the first road information ever travelled.
9. [Bridge, after the close — T-16] One chapter of how information evolved on Earth. Subscribe, like, and follow Earth One — the rest is coming.

**Word count / runtime:** 120 words total, counted mechanically
(whitespace split, em-dashes count as tokens — the conservative
count). At Ep.1 i7's MEASURED pace (0.45 s/word incl. pauses):
speech ≈ 54 s. With a teaser container (1.6 s opening card,
2.5 s end card, normal gaps and payoff hold) → **≈ 63 s total**,
inside the ≤65 s teaser target. ⚠️ Keeping Ep.1's full 3.4 s
end card lands ≈ 64–65 s — still inside, but with no margin;
the container choice is named here, not discovered at build.

## Delivery variation tested (vs Ep.1 control + Ep.2–4)
1. **The performed-voice open:** Ep.1 opens with a question,
   Ep.2 with a performed *story*. This teaser opens by
   performing *speech itself* — L1's line is delivered as the
   first spoken thought (narration slows, one voice, the
   listeners' scene) — the medium demonstrates the message in
   the first five seconds. The control structure (ABT) is kept
   identical to Ep.1 so the opening device is the isolated
   variable.
2. **Honesty as the hook:** the only script in the batch that
   puts an admitted "nobody knows when" in line 1. Tests
   whether declared uncertainty holds attention as well as a
   confident date (Ep.3's 40,000 years, TEASER-PRINT's 1440).
3. **Runtime discipline:** 120 words, the tightest script in
   the batch — same compression test as TEASER-PRINT, run on
   the control structure instead of PAS, so the two teasers
   together separate "shorter" from "different structure."

## Honest weak points
1. **L1's "first spoke" is a dramatisation of something that
   was almost certainly gradual.** There was no single first
   sentence. The line is defensible as a scene, not as history —
   the uncertainty clause carries the honesty, but a strict
   reviewer could still call the scene itself a false precision.
   Named here; if Hadi prefers, L1 can become "somewhere in
   that deep past, humans began speaking whole thoughts out
   loud" at the cost of the hook's single figure.
2. **The claim is wider than its best defence.** Animal alarm
   calls already travelled faster than a body (Phase 0, fact 3).
   The script's real claim is about *composed, detailed*
   information — L4's "whole hunting route" carries that scope,
   but L7's bare word "information" does not repeat it. The
   first critique round should test whether L7 needs "whole
   routes and plans" inside it.
3. **Territory overlap with Ep.2.** Speech (this teaser) and
   story (Ep.2) are one breath apart in the arc: this piece is
   about SPEED, Ep.2 about BACKUP, and L6 deliberately hands
   the weakness to Ep.2 — but a cold viewer who meets both may
   read them as the same chapter twice. The slate should not
   schedule them adjacently.
4. **The villain is a property, not an actor.** "The word that
   dies as it is spoken" is nameable and stacked, but — like
   TEASER-PRINT's drifting copy — it has no face. Across the
   two teasers that is now a pattern: the batch's villains are
   weakening (Ep.1's death had a body). Flagged as a batch-level
   observation, not hidden per-piece.
