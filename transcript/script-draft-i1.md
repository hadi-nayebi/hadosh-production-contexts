## Phase 1 Narrative Gate — declared before drafting (N-04)

- **Structure: Beat-sheet / object hunt** (Save the Cat skeleton,
  compressed to definition size: opening image → the hunt for the
  thing itself → reveal → finale restatement). Chosen over
  And–But–Therefore because this piece's job is *locating* a thing
  the viewer has never seen, not contrasting two states.
- **Intended feeling:** the curtain pull — "the smart thing was
  never the glass I tap; it was behind it the whole time."
- **Misconception / villain (N-06, extends S-01): The Window
  Illusion** — the belief that the app / chat window IS the AI.
  Consequence stack: (1) the viewer credits the window for the
  intelligence, so (2) a bad answer makes them hunt for a "smarter
  app" — the dead solution, named in L5: swapping apps that run the
  same model changes the door, not the cook; (3) they never learn
  the one fact that reorganises everything else in this series:
  the model is a separate thing that apps merely reach.
- **Chosen vivid detail (N-03, tyranny rule):** a cook who trained
  for years, standing behind a restaurant door. The door is what
  you knock on; the cook is what answers. Deliberately the most
  picturable image in the piece — see the anthropomorphism risk in
  the weak points, and its mitigation at L6.
- **Orientation log (N-02):** Where — at your phone, looking at a
  chat window (L1 presumes exactly that scene). Who — you, the
  person tapping the glass. Vibe — backstage reveal. What to
  watch — not the window; what is *behind* it. All four ride the
  L1–L2 beat; no preamble.
- **First-line cold test (N-01):** L1 read cold — (a) "What is this
  about?" → "what the model actually is / where it is" ✓ graspable.
  (b) "Intrigued?" → yes: the viewer uses a model daily and is told
  they have never seen it; the payoff (L3: a trained pattern, not a
  program-with-a-face) is not the predictable "it's servers somewhere"
  answer.
- **Bookend (N-08):** plant L1 "you have never actually seen the
  model" → return L7, the unseen thing finally named in definition
  form. Removal test: without L7 the piece ends on "the window
  itself knows nothing" — a jab at the app, not a definition of the
  model. FAIL without it → structural. Twist: the invisible thing
  turns out to be a *pattern*, not a hidden machine room.

## Essence + learning sentence

- **Essence (≤8 words):** A model is the trained pattern, not app.
- **The one thing the viewer learns (T-17):** "The model is not the
  app or the chat window — it is the pattern that was trained on
  huge amounts of text, and it is the thing that actually continues
  your words; apps are just ways of reaching it."
- **Viewer restatement target:** "The app is the door. The model is
  the cook."

## Deliberate variation tested (batch plan: one variation per WD piece)

- **Example domain: a food/restaurant analogy ladder** (door → cook
  → training years) carries the whole definition, where WD-01 used a
  performed counting demonstration and WD-02 a contrast pair. Tests
  whether an analogy-carried definition retains as well as the
  lane's performed demonstrations — the lane's riskiest open
  question, isolated in this one piece.

## T-17 audit + cold restatement

- (a) One concrete example carried through: the restaurant — door
  and cook introduced L4, both cashed in L5, and the cook's skill
  (continuing words) demonstrated in L6 ✓. (b) Mechanism in plain
  words: "learned which words tend to follow which" / "the model
  continues them" ✓. (c) Anchor: **billions of words** read in
  training ✓ (order-of-magnitude, true of modern LLMs generally —
  see canon notes). (d) Close translates: L7 is the plain
  definition; the cook is retired before the close so the metaphor
  does not replace the payoff ✓.
- **Cold restatement (written from the script alone):** "The model
  isn't the app I use. It's a pattern that was trained on billions
  of words to predict what comes next. The app is just a door to
  it, and the same model can sit behind lots of apps." → matches
  the learning sentence ✓.

## Canon check (K-01) + FLAGS

- **Model = LLM, one slot:** the script says "the model" throughout
  and never presents "model" and "LLM" as two things — canon §3
  ledger ✓. It also never calls the model an "agent" (canon §5,
  published claim 1: a model on its own is not the agent) ✓.
- **Continuer doctrine:** L6–L7 state the Academy mechanism exactly
  — the window routes words in, the model continues them ✓.
- **One model, many apps** (L5) is the electricity lens in plain
  clothes (one source, many devices — canon §1/§4) without naming
  electricity inside a definition short ✓.
- **⚠️ CANON FLAG (minor) — "training" mechanics are not in canon.**
  Canon locks what the model *is* in the system, not how training
  works. L3's "read billions of words and learned which words tend
  to follow which" is standard, uncontroversial mechanism and is
  the brief's own framing ("the trained pattern itself") — but no
  frozen source states the billions figure as a *series* claim. It
  is used as an order-of-magnitude anchor, not a specification; if
  Hadi wants zero unanchored numbers in definitions, L3 can drop
  "billions of" and keep the mechanism ("it read text, and learned
  which words tend to follow which") at a small anchor cost.

## Honest weak points

1. **The cook anthropomorphises.** A cook has intentions; a pattern
   has none. Mitigation is built in (L6: the model *continues
   words*, a mechanical act; L7 retires the cook before the close),
   but the tyranny rule cuts both ways: the cook is the most vivid
   detail, so for some viewers "the model is a little chef" may be
   the durable takeaway. This is the variation's stated risk.
2. L2's "hides" also implies intent. Accepted for rhythm; the
   build's visuals must show a curtain/window reveal, never a
   character peeking — flagged here so it isn't discovered at
   frame QC.
3. The beat-sheet is the loosest structure in the batch; if the
   hunt beat (L1–L2) runs long in TTS, the piece feels like two
   corrections before any content. L3 must land by ~40% (S-03).
