## Phase 1 Narrative Gate — declared before drafting (N-04)

- **Structure: And–But–Therefore (clarity).** AND: the model answers
  from what is in front of it. BUT: what is in front of it is only
  *right now* — yesterday is not on the desk. THEREFORE: forgetting
  is not a flaw in the model; it is the shape of context itself.
- **Intended feeling:** relief — "it's not broken, and it's not
  ignoring me; the paper fell off the desk."
- **Misconception / villain (N-06, extends S-01): The Rude Robot** —
  the belief that the AI *should* remember like a person, so
  forgetting reads as rudeness or brokenness. Consequence stack:
  (1) the viewer re-explains from zero every session, growing more
  annoyed each time; (2) the dead solution: they start pasting
  *everything* into every chat — which stacks the desk until the
  important papers are the ones pushed off; (3) they never build
  the durable fix (files the harness can re-supply) because they
  never learned there was a desk at all.
- **Chosen vivid detail (N-03, tyranny rule):** the oldest paper
  sliding off the edge of the desk. One motion, fully picturable,
  and it *is* the mechanism — the detail and the lesson are the
  same object.
- **Orientation log (N-02):** Where — inside a chat, the morning
  after (L1's "yesterday" sets it). Who — you, the returning user.
  Vibe — mild frustration, answered calmly. What to watch — not
  what the model knows; what it can *see*. All four in the L1–L2
  beat; no preamble.
- **First-line cold test (N-01):** L1 read cold — (a) "What is this
  about?" → "why the AI forgot what I said" ✓ graspable. (b)
  "Intrigued?" → yes: "it is not being rude" kills the predictable
  explanation and leaves a gap — if not rudeness, what? The payoff
  (L4–L5: it can only read what is physically in front of it, and
  papers slide off) is a mechanism, not the predictable "AI has no
  memory" slogan.
- **Bookend (N-08):** plant L1 "what you told it yesterday" →
  return L2/L7 in the piece's own terms: yesterday is not in the
  "right now." Removal test: without L7 the piece ends on the
  harness replacing papers — a tip about setups, not the
  definition. FAIL without it → structural. Twist: "and nothing
  else" — the close adds the hard edge the opening only implied.

## Essence + learning sentence

- **Essence (≤8 words):** Context: everything the model can see right now.
- **The one thing the viewer learns (T-17):** "Context is everything
  in front of the model at this moment — this chat, its
  instructions, any files it was handed — and the model can use
  nothing else, which is why it 'forgets': what is no longer in
  front of it no longer exists for it."
- **Viewer restatement target:** "It only sees the papers on the
  desk. Mine slid off."

## Deliberate variation tested (batch plan: one variation per WD piece)

- **Hook form: the empathy reframe** — a viewer complaint voiced
  verbatim ("it forgot what I told it") and answered with a
  character correction ("not rude") instead of a definition
  question (WD-01), a contrast pair (WD-02), or a hunt (WD-03).
  Tests whether an emotional-reframe hook out-retains the lane's

## T-17 audit + cold restatement

- (a) One concrete example carried through: the desk — introduced
  L4, the forgetting performed on it L5, the fix performed on it
  L6, the close restated over it L7 ✓. (b) Mechanism in plain
  words: "can only read the papers lying on the desk" ✓. (c)
  Anchor: yesterday vs right now — a one-day scale the viewer has
  personally lived; this lane's anchor here is temporal, not
  numeric, deliberately (the number for this topic — window size —
  varies by model and would date the piece; see weak points).
  (d) Close translates: L7 is the plain definition with its hard
  edge ("and nothing else"), not a desk repetition ✓.
- **Cold restatement (written from the script alone):** "Context is
  just what's in front of the model right now — the chat, its
  instructions, files it was given. It forgets because old stuff
  falls out of that view, like papers sliding off a desk. Files
  keep things so they can be put back." → matches the learning
  sentence ✓ — and the restatement now contains BOTH halves the
  topic needs (the window *and* the forgetting).

## Canon check (K-01) + FLAGS

- **Active vs durable context (canon §3, "context"):** exact match.
  The narration's context = canon's *active context* (the material
  in one model call — temporary); files = *durable context*; and
  "the harness composes the first from the second" is L6's entire
  job, in desk words ✓.
- **Files gloss discipline:** L6 says files *keep* things (the
  drawer) — storage form, never "what it remembers" as a function
  claim; Memory-the-function is not named in this piece ✓.
- **Instructions:** L3 lists "your instructions" as context content.
  Canon-consistent: instructions steer *through* context (§3), so
  their presence inside the active context is correct, and the
  script does not call them anything else ✓.
- **⚠️ CANON FLAG (minor) — "harness" is introduced here, glossed,
  not built.** L6 names the harness with a ≤6-word in-line gloss
  ("the layer around the model," T-04-compliant) because canon
  assigns the context-composing job to the harness and giving it
  to "the app" would contradict WD-03's demotion of the window.
  It is a named, glossed term, not an unbuilt concept dropped in
  (T-16) — but there is no WD piece for "harness" in the WD-02..05
  slate, so this is the lane's first formal use of its most
  important word. Hadi may prefer the NEXT bridge (below) to point
  at the Lessons lane's harness piece instead of WD-05; script
  works either way.
- Simplification, disclosed: "this whole chat" (L3) — real systems
  may trim or summarise history inside the window; the desk picture
  (papers slide off) already implies selection, so the plain claim
  and the mechanism do not conflict at high-school register.

## Honest weak points

1. The desk needs TWO props (desk + drawer) where WD-01's chunking
   machine needed one; the drawer plant (L4) and its transformation
   (L6) are the load-bearing subtlety — if a cold viewer misses
   that the drawer *changed meaning*, L6 reads as a contradiction
   of L4. The Chekhov table exists precisely to force the build to
   stage that transformation visually.
2. No numeric anchor (window sizes vary by model and date fast).
   The yesterday/today anchor is a deliberate trade: durability
   over precision. If a future model makes forgetting rare, this
   piece ages — accepted, and noted here rather than discovered
   later.
3. L6 is the densest line (harness intro + gloss + the fix in 20
   words). It is the line most likely to need a second clause at
   the critique rounds before build.
