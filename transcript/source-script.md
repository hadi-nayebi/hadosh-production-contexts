## 1. The fact (from Phase 0)

Pew Research Center, survey **February 17–23, 2026**, published June 17,
2026 ("Americans and AI 2026"): **44% of U.S. adults have used ChatGPT**
(18% in 2023); **61% of adults under 30**.
https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/

## 2. Narrative declaration (N-04 / N-06 — BEFORE drafting)

- **Structure: The Count and the Key** (lane structure, named): a number
  that keeps growing (L2–L3), then the thing the number never counts
  (L4–L5), then both in one breath (L6).
- **Intended feeling:** the turn from impressive to slightly hollow —
  "everyone's in… and nobody has anything to show for it that is
  theirs."
- **Villain (N-06), named: The Tab That Closes.** Consequence stack:
  (1) the session ends and the layer the user built inside it — their
  instructions, the memory they fed it, the files they made — stays on
  the company's side; (2) tomorrow's session starts from zero, so the
  user's growing skill at steering lives nowhere durable. Dead solution
  named by implication at L5: a longer chat history is not ownership —
  it is a longer tenancy.
- **The ONE vivid detail (N-03):** the key that was never handed over
  (L6) — the whole tenancy argument in one object.
- **Jargon:** "rented" is the lane's plain word for the gap; no term is
  used before the fact earns it.

## 3. The one thing the viewer learns (T-17)

"By 2026, 44% of American adults had used ChatGPT — almost all of it
inside one company's rented chat box, where nothing they shaped stays
theirs when the tab closes."

Essence (≤8 words): **Half tried it; nobody owns it.**

## 5. Retention reasoning — Decision Records (Q-0)

### DR-1 — THE CLICK (title + cover)
- **Choice:** Title: **"44% of America Has Used ChatGPT."** Cover
  line: **"HALF IN. NO KEY."** (4 words, S-09) over the dot-crowd
  counter visual inherited from SOA-01's template.
- **Alternatives considered:** "Almost Half the Country Uses AI" —
  rejected: it upgrades Pew's *ever-used ChatGPT* figure into a
  general AI-use claim the source does not make (Phase 0 flag 2).
  "The Generation That Walked In" — rejected: pretty, numberless;
  this lane's click IS the number.
- **Context it serves:** the lane sells a dated count; the cover adds
  the one thing the count hides (no key) so the tap promises the turn,
  not just the statistic.
- **What would change it:** the 48-h scoreboard (S-10) on SOA-01/02 —
  if number-led titles underperform fact+gap titles, the gap moves
  into the title.

### DR-2 — THE CONTINUE (first sentence + re-earn map)
- **Choice:** the fact lands at L2, inside five seconds; attention is
  re-earned at L3 (the number nearly tripled; a generation is in), and
  again at L4, where the piece stops counting people and starts
  counting what they keep — the question changes from "how many?" to
  "yours or theirs?", which is the question the close answers.
- **Alternatives considered:** opening on the under-30 figure (61%) —
  rejected: the bigger shock is generational but the smaller one is
  national; the lane reports the country first, the generation as the
  escalation.
- **What would change it:** retention dip measured at L4 on SOA-01's
  scoreboard would mean the turn needs to arrive one line earlier
  across the lane.

## 8. Honest weak points

1. "Almost none of them were handed a key" is the lane's rhetoric at
   the same register as SOA-01's "ownership is zero" — a small number
   of users do run their own harnesses. The word "almost" is the
   honesty device; it stays.
2. Ever-used (44%) is a wide, shallow measure — Phase 0 flag 2. A
   hostile commenter can say "tried once in 2024 counts." The script's
   defence is that it never claims more than the source claims.
