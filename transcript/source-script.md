## 5. Retention reasoning — Decision Records (Q-0) [NEW in i2]

### DR-1 — THE CLICK (title + cover)
- **Choice:** Title: **"What Is a Model?"** Cover: a chat window
  drawn as a restaurant door, closed; cover line: **"YOU'VE NEVER
  SEEN IT."** (4 words, S-09).
- **Alternatives considered:** cover showing the cook — rejected:
  the cook is the payoff; a cover that shows him spends the hunt
  before the tap. Cover line "IT'S NOT THE APP" — rejected: it
  states the correction, and corrections are the continue's job;
  the click's job is the itch (you use it daily, you've never
  seen it).
- **Context it serves:** the piece's hunt structure (beat-sheet /
  object hunt, declared in i1's gate) starts on the cover, not at
  L1.
- **What would change it:** if hunt-covers underperform
  definition-covers on WD-01/02 scoreboards, the door stays but
  the line becomes the question form.

### DR-2 — THE CONTINUE (first sentence + re-earn map)
- **Choice:** L1 makes a claim about the VIEWER (you have never
  seen it) — an itch, not a topic. L2 narrows the search to two
  familiar objects; L3 lands the definition by ~35% (S-03), early
  for this lane, because the piece's remaining runtime is spent
  *cashing* the definition (analogy L4, consequence L5, mechanism
  L6) rather than withholding it. The re-earn at L5 is the
  practical payoff: why swapping apps feels identical.
- **Alternatives considered:** withholding the definition to L5
  (full hunt) — rejected at i1's gate and re-affirmed: a
  definition lane that withholds its definition past the halfway
  mark trains viewers to leave before the product arrives.
- **What would change it:** a retention cliff between L2 and L3
  would mean the hunt needs a partial reveal at L2 (e.g. the
  door visual arrives earlier), not a later definition.
