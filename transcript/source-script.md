## Narration (127 words, 8 lines)

1. Look at this picture. On the left, a mind made of glowing pieces, standing apart from the person.
2. On the right, the same pieces, worn like a helmet. Same material. Two different futures.
3. Nothing is added. Nothing is taken away. Only the arrangement changes.
4. The pieces are an AI model, plus its instructions, its memory, its files, and its tools.
5. Built one way, they stand apart from you: powerful, generic, and owned by someone else.
6. Built the other way, they wrap around you: a second cortex that keeps your memory, your rules, your files.
7. The model is just raw intelligence. The harness is where your way of working lives.
8. So don't just use the mind standing apart. Wear the one that's built around you. Own your harness.

## The poster artwork

Hadi's own two-panel illustration (from his LinkedIn post screenshot). The raw upload
carried screenshot UI — a thumbs-up icon, a "3", and a comment bubble — over the
artwork's lower-right corner. Repair (from the raw art, not the earlier patched copy):
the icon pixels were detected by color signature, isolated by connected components
(so genuine chalk lines — jaw, collar, sweater folds — were never masked), removed by
diffusion inpainting, then the two icon areas had their low-frequency tone re-matched to
the surrounding art and chalk grain re-seeded; a mild corner vignette finishes it.
The right-panel close-ups use a tighter crop (helmet + face) that keeps the repaired
corner out of frame. Result verified at full size: no icons, no patch rectangle, no seam
lines; at most a faint tonal smudge in the extreme corner, at the level of the art's own
chalk mottling. All genuine linework (jaw, collar, fold lines) is untouched.

## Production notes / iterations

- Narration first (TTS speed 115, same voice as Lessons 1–5); every caption and cut
  placed on measured timings, never estimates.
- QA pass 1 (full-size frame per scene) found three defects: (1) the title lockup
  overlapped the poster's own "AGI" headings → opener rebuilt with the poster smaller,
  lower, and dimmed so the lockup sits in clean dark space; (2) the end handle sat on
  the artwork → moved to the dark band under the poster (printed once, exact handle);
  (3) the inherited poster repair (rectangular patch from the Lesson 2 production) was
  visible in the close-ups → full re-repair from the raw art (above).
- Poster-repair iterations: mirror/texture transplants and wide-mask inpainting were
  tried and rejected (visible seams / erased fold lines); the shipped version is
  narrow-mask diffusion inpaint + low-frequency tone match (softened on the sweater
  box to avoid a ring) + grain. Right-panel crop tightened as a final safeguard.
- One residual, disclosed: a very faint tonal smudge remains where the comment-bubble
  icon sat (extreme lower-right of the artwork). Invisible at normal viewing; only
  discernible in a 3× blow-up. Everything else on the checklist passes.
