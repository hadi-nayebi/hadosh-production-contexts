## Phase 0 — mini study brief (sourced facts; uncertainty flagged)

1. **Music is at least ~40,000 years old** — bone and ivory
   flutes from Geissenklösterle Cave, Germany, dated to roughly
   42,000–43,000 years ago (Conard et al., *Nature*, 2009;
   Higham et al., 2012). This gives the episode its honest time
   placement: songs belong to the same deep era as the cave
   marks of Ep.3 — the episodes run parallel, not in a false
   sequence (flagged in the weak points).
2. **Songs have carried practical knowledge across whole
   landscapes** — Aboriginal Australian songlines encode routes,
   water sources and law in sung verses tied to landmarks,
   maintained across generations (widely documented; popular
   synthesis in Lynne Kelly, *The Memory Code*, 2016 — ⚠️ a
   popular source, used for framing, not as primary evidence).
3. **Oral texts can survive millennia nearly word-perfect when
   carried in verse AND depended on** — the Rigveda was
   transmitted orally for roughly 3,000 years with elaborate
   error-checking recitation (composed ~1500–1200 BCE; the oral
   tradition is on UNESCO's Memory of the World register). The
   v2 qualification of L6 rests on the pattern across facts 2
   and 3: fidelity is a property of depended-on, checked song —
   not of song as such. Used in the brief; the script keeps
   its one example prehistoric.

  catalyst (plain words fail) → low point (knowledge dying in

  own life).
- **Intended feeling:** delight — the small shock of realising
  a song is a piece of technology.
- **Villain (N-06), named: Words without a beat** — plain spoken
  lists. Consequence stack: (1) a list spoken once is already
  out of order by nightfall — sequence is the first casualty;
  (2) one swapped word changes the route or the recipe — the
  error is silent, nobody can hear it; (3) each retelling drifts
  further, so the group's survival knowledge decays while
  everyone believes it is intact. The beat answers each: rhythm
  makes a wrong word *audible* (it breaks the line), which is
  an error-check built into the carrier.
- **The ONE most vivid detail, chosen on purpose (N-03):** the
  grandmother singing the road to the water — first verse, the
  big rock; second verse, the bent tree.
- **Jargon push (one, deliberate):** none new — the push here is
  conceptual, not lexical: "a song is a memory tool." Plain
  words, a pushed idea (audience rule respected by restraint).

## Script v2 (10 lines)

1. Forty thousand years ago, a grandmother sings the road to the water: first verse, the big rock; second verse, the bent tree.
2. The children sing it back. It sounds like a song. It is a map.
3. Plain words are slippery: say a list once, and by nightfall the order is wrong.
4. But a beat holds words in line. A wrong word breaks the rhythm — you can hear the mistake.
5. So what had to survive went into songs: where the water is, which plants heal, which plants kill.
6. Mouth to mouth, camp to camp, generation to generation — a song a whole group depends on arrives almost unchanged.
7. Rhythm is a memory tool. A song remembers what plain words forget.
8. The first archive was a body. Then a story, then a mark. And riding through them all, a song.
9. You still use this tool. You learned your letters by singing them.
10. [Bridge, after the close — T-16] One idea at a time, across the whole story of this planet — subscribe to Earth One.

## 5. Retention reasoning — Decision Records (Q-0)

### DR-E4-CLICK — title + cover
- **Choice:** Title: **"A Song Remembers What Words Forget."**
  Cover: the grandmother and children on the road between the
  big rock and the bent tree, cover line **"A SONG IS A MAP."**
  (4 words, S-09).
- **Alternatives considered:** "The First Memory Tool" —
  rejected: a priority claim again (Ep.3's lesson), and
  "tool" spoils L7's naming beat. "Singing Directions" —
  rejected: reads as a music-class topic, not a gap; nothing
  is at stake in it.
- **Context it serves:** the title is a small impossibility —
  songs are entertainment; remembering is what minds and
  books do. A cold viewer taps to resolve which side is
  wrong. The cover line is the piece's own first reveal (L2)
  promoted to packaging: it promises a reframe (song → map),
  and the piece pays it in the first ten seconds, which is
  what makes the promise honest rather than bait.
- **Why this one:** title and cover argue the same paradox
  from two sides (memory vs map) without repeating each
  other, and both are claims the script itself makes — the
  packaging cannot be accused of promising another video.
- **What would change it:** if the road frame crowds at
  180 px, fall back to the grandmother alone mid-song under
  the same cover line.

### DR-E4-CONTINUE — first sentence + re-earn map
- **Choice:** open on performance, not explanation: the
  grandmother is SINGING the road, and the first line hands
  the viewer the song's actual contents (two verses, two
  landmarks) so the viewer holds the evidence before the
  claim exists. The script then re-earns attention:
  - **L2 — the reveal, early:** "It is a map." The piece
    pays its first debt inside ten seconds — a fast first
    payoff that buys credit for the mechanism beats.
  - **L3 — the failure:** plain words lose their order by
    nightfall. The viewer has felt this (a list, a phone
    number) — recognition does the persuading.
  - **L4 — the mechanism, audible:** a wrong word BREAKS
    the rhythm; the error-check can be heard. The abstract
    claim (fidelity) becomes a sound.
  - **L5 — the stakes:** water, healing plants, poison
    plants. What rides in the songs is life and death, so
    L6's fidelity claim matters before it is made.
  - **L6 — the build, scoped:** mouth to mouth, camp to
    camp, generation to generation — and the claim arrives
    already qualified (depended-on songs), so a skeptical
    viewer is answered before they object.
  - **L7 — the statement:** the tool named in direct words,
    unannounced (de-signed).
  - **L8 — the bookend widened:** the song has been riding
    through body, story, and mark all along — the series
    line pays with a fourth member who was never absent.
  - **L9 — the twist lands on the viewer:** you learned
    your letters by singing them. Prehistory becomes the
    viewer's own childhood; the "so what" is personal.
- **Alternatives considered:** opening on the flutes (the
  Phase 0 fact) — rejected: an artifact is a topic, not a
  scene; nobody in it is doing anything the viewer needs.
  Opening on L3's failure — rejected: a problem with no
  carrier yet; the grandmother must exist before words can
  fail her.
- **Context it serves:** Hadi's retention law — every beat
  maps to a why above or is absent.
- **What would change it:** the 48-h scoreboard (S-10) — if
  viewers leave at L5's list, the cargo compresses; L2's

## Honest weak points (v2)
1. **Timeline honesty (carried):** Ep.4 shares Ep.3's era
   (~40,000 years ago). The series order is thematic, not
   strictly chronological — songs may be far older than cave
   marks, but the *evidence* (flutes) is the same age. The
   script never claims songs came after marks; the build's
   description should call it "the same age as the cave marks
   — a parallel road" (noted for the upload package, not
   smuggled into the narration).
2. **Runtime pace-dependent** (≈61–79 s projected); measured
   TTS timing decides, with L8's recap the designated cut.
3. **"Almost unchanged" is now scoped, not removed** — the
   qualification is one clause ("a song a whole group depends
   on"); a hostile reading can still call it strong, but it
   is now the claim the cited traditions actually support.
4. **The alphabet-song close (L9) crosses cultures unevenly** —
   it lands for English-speaking viewers; the channel's wider
   audience may have learned letters differently. It is the
   bookend twist, so replacing it costs the ending; flagged
   for Hadi's ear rather than pre-emptively cut (carried
   from v1, still open by design).
