## Settled teaching (unchanged from i1/i2 — the material)

**Essence:** A token is a chunk of text the machine counts.
**Viewer restatement:** "A token isn't a word — it's a piece of
text, often part of a word, that the machine reads, counts, bills,
and turns into numbers. Everything the model does is measured in
tokens."
**One teaching idea:** what a token is. Nothing else is taught.

## Stage-1 (content) decisions — what survived the restructure, and why

- **Kept verbatim:** the definition (L3 = i2 L2), the
  machine's-eye contrast (L4 first half = i2 L3), the live count
  (L5 = i2 L4's performed count). These are the piece's settled
  teaching; the owner's complaint was structure and style, not
  the definition. Rewriting them would churn settled content for
  no evidence.
- **Kept, compressed — the stakes (L6):** i2's L5 carried a
  question frame ("Why should you care? Because…") and four
  stakes (reads / writes / pay / hold). DECISION: keep all four
  stakes, drop the question frame. Reason: the stakes are what
  make the bookend's quadrillion *matter* (each one is read,
  written, billed, held — that is why a monthly quadrillion is a
  story and not a trivia figure); but the piece now has one
  question already (L2), and a second rhetorical question
  mid-piece spends the F3 think-beat's value twice. The four
  stakes survive as a single measured list.
- **Dropped — "about three-quarters of a word"** (i2 L4's second
  gloss on the 4-characters rule). Reason: two numeric glosses on
  one rule is one too many for a 9-word demonstration line to
  carry; the four-characters rule plus the performed count teach
  the size. The count itself proves the point better than the
  fraction states it.
- **Dropped — i2 L1 "Quick: what is a token?"** as the opener.
  The owner's structure replaces the cold question with the
  number; the definition question survives as L2, now *earned*
  by the hook instead of asked cold.
- **Dropped — i2 L6's closing restatement as a separate line**
  ("So that's a token: not a word — a chunk of text, counted by
  machines."). Its job — return & name (W-05) — is absorbed by
  the bookend: L8 returns to the number and names what the viewer
  now knows ("Now you know what one is"). A second restatement
  after the bookend would close the piece twice.
- **Structure:** bookend (hook number → question → definition →
  count → stakes → turn → the number again). Story-structure
  class: hook = sourced-fact monument; ending = return-to-hook.
- **Navigation budget:** 0 of 132 words point outside the piece
  (0% ≤ 10% cap). The silent NEXT bridge after the close is
  packaging, not narration (i2 practice, T-16: the pointer lives
  outside the teaching arc).

## Editor pass (demanding-human-editor simulation, 2026-10-26)

Run over the Stage-1 draft before synthesis. Findings and
dispositions:
1. *"Quadrillion" cold, with no anchor, is a comprehension risk
   in the first 3 seconds.* — Accepted as the owner's explicit
   design (the number's scale IS the excitement; L1's second
   sentence anchors it to Google + tokens + monthly within the
   same line). The scene mitigates: the numeral assembles as a
   monument with the word — sound-off, the scale reads.
2. *L2's "that there can be quadrillions of them" is an archaic
   construction.* — Kept deliberately: it is the owner's own
   phrasing for the entry question, and its slight formality
   slows the line down at exactly the joint where the think-beat
   lands. Flagged here so the keep is a decision, not an oversight.
3. *L7 repeats "think in" twice in one sentence.* — Kept: the
   parallel is the rhetoric (machines that think in LANGUAGE /
   think in TOKENS) and mirrors the owner's wording for the turn.
4. *"Google's machines" (L1) vs "the model" (L6) vs "machines"
   (L7) — inconsistent referents?* — Read as deliberate widening:
   Google's fleet (the fact) → any model (the stakes) → machines
   generally (the turn). The fact's scope note (all Google AI
   surfaces) is carried in this doc and on screen.
5. *Does L6's list slow the run to the turn?* — It is the
   piece's only list; its four beats are the stakes tiles' rhythm
   (fast, counted). Editor accepts: the turn lands harder after
   the cost is felt.
6. *Ending "Now you know what one is" — is "one" unambiguous?*
   — Yes by then: one token. The bookend's whole design makes
   "one" point at the single chunk defined in L3.
Verdict: PASS with the six dispositions above recorded.
