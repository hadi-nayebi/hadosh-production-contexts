## Phase 0 — mini study brief (carried from round 1; unchanged)
1. Anatomically modern humans ~300,000 years old (Jebel Irhoud,
   ~300 ka; Hublin et al., *Nature*, 2017) — "people" at 100,000
   years ago is safe.
2. Symbolic behaviour visible by ~100,000–75,000 years ago
   (Blombos engraved ochre ~75 ka; shell beads possibly ~100 ka).
   ⚠️ The origin date of spoken language is NOT settled (estimates
   ~50,000 to well over 150,000 years). The script therefore opens
   "By one hundred thousand years ago…" — a world-placement, never
   a claimed start date.
3. Oral traditions can preserve real events for thousands of years
   (Nunn & Reid, 2016 — debated; plausibility support only, not
   stated in the script).

   L1 opens "**By** one hundred thousand years ago" — the
   mitigation the draft itself offered. No start date is claimed
   anywhere; the Phase 0 uncertainty stays attached to the brief.

   Round 1: 156 words → ~81 s projected. Round 2 applied the
   draft's named merge (old L7+L8 → new L8+L9) and trims → 141

## The one thing the viewer learns (T-17)
"By about 100,000 years ago, people kept what they knew in stories
told out loud — and because a story is copied into everyone who
hears it, what one person knew could reach everyone listening and
survive their death — as long as it kept being retold."

Essence (≤8 words): **A story lives in many memories.**

## Script (12 lines, 141 words — LOCKED)

1. By one hundred thousand years ago, people kept their knowledge in stories.
2. A hunter tells of the river that runs when the others dry — where the water hides.
3. Nothing is written yet.
4. That night, the story is copied into every memory.
5. A story is information that copies itself by being heard.
6. Children retell it, and theirs. The hunter dies; his knowledge lives.
7. A body passed knowledge to a few. A story passes it to everyone.
8. But the story has an enemy: forgetting.
9. Tell it once, and it fades in every head at once — no original to check.
10. So say it plainly: the story was the first backup. One memory became many, hard to kill.
11. The first archive was a body. The second, a story everyone heard.
12. [Bridge, after the close — T-16] Chapter two of how information evolved on Earth. Subscribe, like, follow Earth One. (The end card carries "the rest of the story" in lettering — format v2.)

## Phase 1 Narrative Gate (QC v2.7, Section N) — on the LOCKED script

### N-04 Structure declaration (declared in round 1, before the draft)
- **Structure: And–But–Therefore** (clarity — the series spine).
  AND: a story copies itself into every memory that hears it
  (L4–L7). BUT: told once and never repeated, it fades in every
  head at once (L8–L9). THEREFORE: the story was the first backup
  (L10–L11).
- **Intended feeling:** warmth and safety around the fire first —
  then the quiet fear that the safety depends on retelling.

storage is not predictable from L1; payoff named — mechanism

### N-06 Concrete villain
**Forgetting — a story told only once.** Consequence stack:
(1) the teller dies or never repeats it → the copies start to
fade; (2) details slip in every head at the same time — no
original left to check against (L9); (3) the knowledge is gone
while people are still alive — the channel fails silently, unlike
Ep.1's visible death. No dead solutions: nothing else had been
tried in this world; stated per N-06. PASS.
