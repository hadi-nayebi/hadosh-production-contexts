## 1. Phase 0 — study brief (sourced; uncertainty flagged)

1. **The built statistic (sourced, iron-paired).** "It took
   about three years to print these 180 Bibles, while prior to
   the development of the printing press it would have taken
   three years for a scribe to produce just one Bible."
   — Barnebays, "The Testaments Transformed: Printing the
   Gutenberg Bible,"
   https://www.barnebys.co.uk/blog/the-testaments-transformed-printing-the-gutenberg-bible
   ⚠️ Run-length estimates vary by source (Apollo Magazine:
   180 in two years; New World Encyclopedia: one year; Book Riot
   / Sotheby's: the Bible project spans 1450–1455). The script
   uses the matched pair — same three years, 180 vs 1 — because
   it is the formulation where BOTH sides carry the same span;
   the range is recorded here, not hidden.
2. **The climax scale (sourced).** "More books were produced
   in fifty years (between 1450 and 1500) than over the
   previous thousand years." — Centre Culturel Irlandais,
   "The living book" exhibition,
   https://archives-en.centreculturelirlandais.com/digitized-collections/online-exhibitions/the-living-book
   The same estimate is cited in Tom Wheeler, *From Gutenberg
   to Google* (Brookings, 2019): "more books were printed in
   the first fifty years after Gutenberg's discovery than had
   been copied by all the scribes in Europe in the previous
   thousand years,"
   https://www.perlego.com/book/742353/from-gutenberg-to-google-the-history-of-our-future-pdf
3. **Corroborating totals.** University of Glasgow Incunabula
   Project: over 30,300 editions known from the 15th century;
   total books printed before 1501 estimated at **8–20
   million**,
   https://www.gla.ac.uk/news/archiveofnews/2015/february/headline_395752_en.html
4. **Credit order (carried from v1, unchanged — it is true).**
   Movable type is NOT Gutenberg's invention: Bi Sheng's clay
   type, China, ~1041–1048 (recorded by Shen Kuo); Korea
   printed with metal movable type — oldest surviving
   metal-type book, the *Jikji*, 1377. Gutenberg's
   contribution (~1440s, Mainz) was the *system*: durable
   metal type, oil-based ink, screw press. The script never
   calls him printing's inventor; China and Korea are named
   BEFORE he is.
5. **The opening's honesty flag.** "Identical" is claimed of
   the PRINTED PAGE — letter for letter, the printed text is
   the same across copies. Finished copies still differed:
   rubrication and illumination were added by hand, and rare
   stop-press corrections exist. The information claim (the
   printed text no longer depends on a copying hand) is exact;
   the flag lives here so the choice is deliberate.
6. **Time anchors.** Gutenberg Bible completed ~1455
   (workshop production 1450–1455). Bi Sheng ~1041–48 →
   "four centuries old" from 1455 is accurate (~410 years).

## 2. Narrative declaration (N-04 / N-06 / N-03 — BEFORE drafting)

- **Structure: The Impossible Object** (mystery-first).
  An object that cannot exist (L1) → why it cannot (L2) →
  the seed is older than the hero (L3, the surprise) → the
  repeating system (L4, the mechanism payoff) → the mirrored
  number (L5) → the thousand-year scale (L6) → what it means
  for a book and its author (L7). NOT chronological — v1's
  walk from workshop to shelf is exactly what died in
  critique (see §6).
- **Intended feeling:** bafflement first ("that can't be"),
  then the ache of the cost (three years of a life for one
  book), a jolt at L3, triumph at the numbers, and a quiet
  chill at the close: the author has left the room and the
  book doesn't care.
- **Villain (N-06), named: The Wandering Copy.** By hand,
  copies wander. Consequence stack: (1) drift is inherited —
  a skipped line, a wrong word becomes the next copy's
  truth, with no original to check against; (2) scarcity —
  at three years per Bible, books stay locked in
  monasteries and churches, one-of-one objects that die
  with their shelf. Dead solutions the audience already
  believes in: a steadier hand, more scribes — L2 kills the
  first in its first sentence ("No hand can do that"); the
  drift lives in the hand itself, so more hands drift in
  more directions.
- **The ONE vivid detail (N-03 tyranny rule):** the pair of
  Bibles lying open at the same page, identical — an object
  the viewer can hold in the mind's eye for the whole piece,
  and the object the close returns to, transformed.
- **Jargon:** none pushed. "System" (L4) is glossed in the
  same breath by its three parts; no term is used before it
  is shown working.
- **Signature ban (Hadi, 2026-10-23) applied at declaration:**
  this script contains NO "say it plainly," no member of the
  plain/simple family, and no stock transition. The N-09
  message (L7) is delivered in direct words WITHOUT being
  announced. Sentence shapes were varied on purpose (see
  the opener audit in §5).

## 4. The script (8 lines, 118 tokens counted mechanically —
whitespace split, em dash counted as a token; 109 spoken
words + 9 bridge words)

1. Mainz, around 1455. Two Bibles lie open side by side: every letter in its place, identical.
2. No hand can do that. By hand, copies wander: a skipped line, a wrong word, and one Bible cost a scribe three years.
3. The fix is four centuries old: Bi Sheng's clay letters in China; Korea's, cast in metal.
4. Gutenberg's Mainz system repeats: metal letters, oil ink, a screw press.
5. Three years, 180 Bibles from his presses. Three years, one Bible from a scribe.
6. By 1500, print outnumbers a thousand years of hand-copying.
7. The book no longer needs its author in the room. It speaks without him — the same, everywhere it lands.
8. [Bridge, after the close — T-16] One chapter of how information evolved. Follow Earth One.

**Runtime:** 109 spoken words × 0.45 s/word (Ep.1 i7's
MEASURED pace) ≈ 49 s speech; + teaser container (1.6 s
opening card, 2.5 s end card, gaps, payoff hold) → **≈ 58 s
total**, inside the ≤65 s teaser ceiling with slack v1 did
not have.

## 5. Retention reasoning — Decision Records (Q-0)

*Why would anyone tap this, and why would they stay? Each
answer below is a Decision Record: if a beat stops serving
its why, the beat is cut or fixed — the why is not
decoration.*

### DR-1 — THE CLICK (title + cover)
- **Choice:** Title: **"Two Identical Bibles."** Cover frame:
  the two Bibles lying open side by side (the L1 object),
  cover line: **"EXACTLY THE SAME."** (3 words, S-09).
- **Alternatives considered:** v1's title, "A Book That Speaks
  Without Its Author" — rejected: it hands over the payoff
  (the viewer already knows the ending; nothing is owed).
  "180 Bibles in Three Years" — rejected as the title: a
  number reads as a fact to file, not a mystery to open;
  the number earns more discovered inside, at L5.
- **Context it serves:** a cold viewer in a feed taps on an
  information gap, not on a topic. "Two identical Bibles" is
  a contradiction most viewers have never noticed: books
  used to be unique objects, so sameness is the anomaly.
  The cover shows the proof object itself, not the machine —
  the machine is the answer, and answers don't get taps.
- **Why this one:** the title states the impossibility in
  three words and withholds everything else — how, who,
  since when. Every withheld piece is a line of the script.
- **What would change it:** if the L1 frame can't be made
  legible at 180 px (two small books), fall back to a single
  open Bible page under the same cover line.

### DR-2 — THE CONTINUE (first sentence + re-earn map)
- **Choice:** the first sentence promises an explanation the
  viewer cannot supply themselves: an object dated ~1455
  that the world before it could not produce. The script
  then re-earns attention at named beats instead of spending
  the opening's credit in one walk:
  - **L2 — it gets worse:** the impossibility survives its
    obvious explanation (a very good hand) AND carries a
    human cost (three years of one life). Curiosity converts
    to stakes.
  - **L3 — the inversion:** the viewer who settled into
    "Gutenberg invents printing" is wrong-footed inside
    25 seconds — the fix is four centuries old and not
    European. A viewer who almost left now has a new
    question: then what did Gutenberg actually do?
  - **L4 — the debt is paid:** the system, named by its
    parts. The L1 question closes here; the piece could
    end — and doesn't, which is the next hook.
  - **L5 — the shareable number:** mirrored arithmetic
    (three years / three years) makes the fold impossible
    to misread and easy to repeat to someone else — the
    retention beat most likely to be quoted.
  - **L6 — final escalation:** the scale jumps from one
    workshop to a thousand years, in nine words. Nothing
    after it needs to top it; the close spends it.
  - **L7 — the meaning lands on a person:** not output, not
    history — an author, absent, outspoken by his own book.
- **Alternatives considered:** opening on Gutenberg at work
  (v1's L1) — rejected: it announces a topic ("the printing
  story") instead of opening a debt. Opening on the scribe's
  three years — rejected: a cost with no mystery attached
  is a fact, and facts don't hold the first five seconds
  the way an impossibility does.
- **Context it serves:** Hadi's rule (2026-10-23): the job
  must rationalize the tap, the first sentence, and every
  continuation. A beat that serves no why above was cut in
  critique (§6): v1's "thousand rooms" rhetoric, the shelf
  bookend, and the workshop walk all died on this record.
- **What would change it:** Studio's 48-h scoreboard (S-10)
  on this piece — if viewers leave at L3, the inversion is
  mistimed, not wrong; it would move earlier, not out.

### DR-3 — The Google-token cross-reference: considered, REJECTED
- **Choice:** no modern token-processing comparison in this

- **Why:** the piece already stacks two scale beats (L5,

## 9. Honest weak points

1. **L1's "identical" is true of the printed page, not the
   finished object** (§1.5): hand rubrication still made
   each physical copy one of a kind. A pedant viewer can
   land this punch. Accepted because the piece's claim is
   about information, not binding — but it is the first
   line a hostile comment will quote.
2. **"Three years" is stated flat in L5; the sources give a
   range** (one to five years for the run; §1.1). The
   matched-pair formulation (same span both sides) is the
   widely repeated one and the arithmetic survives any
   value in the range — but the hedge "about" was cut for
   the mirror's rhythm, and that trade is recorded here,
   not buried.
3. **The transmission question is opened and not paid.**
   L3's surprise implies "then why Mainz, why then?" —
   historically murky (no proven line from Korean metal
   type to Gutenberg's workshop). The piece pays HOW, not
   the route. If a viewer asks it in comments, the honest
   answer is "nobody knows" — which the long-form series
   can carry; a 58-second teaser cannot.
4. **Nobody is on screen.** The scribe is a cost, Gutenberg
   a name, the author an absence. The object carries the
   human interest — a deliberate bet (DR-1/DR-2), and the
   opposite bet from v1's workshop figure. If Hadi wants a
   face, L2's scribe is where it would be bought.
5. **L3 compresses two inventions and skips a third.**
   Clay type (China), metal type (Korea) — and woodblock
   printing, older than both, is absent entirely. The
   piece teaches the movable-type lineage only; a viewer
   could leave thinking printing began with movable type.
   Named here; fixing it costs a line the budget spent
   elsewhere.
