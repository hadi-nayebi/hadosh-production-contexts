## 1. The fact (from Phase 0, refreshed 2026-10-24)

Gallup Workforce Panel, **Q2 2026 — surveyed May 6–20, 2026**
(22,573 U.S. employees, ±0.9 pp); headline numbers published by
Gallup July 28, 2026 (*Front Page*): **52% of U.S. employees use AI
in their role at least a few times a year** (21% in Q2 2023; 45% in
Q3 2025); **only 15% use it daily**; 30% use it a few times a week
or more; 47% say their organization has integrated AI tools.
https://www.gallup.com/workplace/704225/rising-adoption-spurs-workforce-changes.aspx
(Q1 2026 wave, same series — 50% / 13% daily, surveyed Feb 4–19,
2026); Q2 2026 confirmed in Gallup's *Front Page*, July 28, 2026,
and Gallup's AI adoption hub
(https://www.gallup.com/workplace/650153/ai-adoption.aspx).

## 2. Narrative declaration (N-04 / N-06 — BEFORE drafting)

- **Structure: The Count and the Shallowness** (lane structure,
  varied): the headline number (L2), its speed (L3), then the same
  survey read a second time, closer (L4) — the piece's wow is that
  the deflating number was inside the impressive one all along.
- **Intended feeling:** a headline deflating in the hand — "half the
  office… a few times a year."
- **Villain (N-06), named: The Occasional Dip.** Consequence stack:
  (1) a few visits a year never compound into a practice — no
  instructions tuned, no memory accumulated, no workflow owned;
  (2) because the tool is the employer's, the little skill that does
  form is stored in the employer's setup and vanishes with the job.
  Dead solution named at L5 by negation: more access is not the
  missing piece — access already arrived.
- **The ONE vivid detail (N-03):** the whole office flipping in
  thirty-six months (L3) — adoption as a single motion.
- **Jargon:** none. "Practice" (L6) is the piece's plain word for the
  durable layer, defined by contrast inside the piece.

## 3. The one thing the viewer learns (T-17)

"By mid-2026, 52% of U.S. employees had used AI at work — but only
15% used it daily, almost all of it inside tools their employer
rents, so adoption is broad and ownership is nearly zero."

Essence (≤8 words): **The office adopted; the worker rents.**

## 5. Retention reasoning — Decision Records (Q-0)

### DR-1 — THE CLICK (title + cover)
- **Choice:** Title: **"52% of Employees Now Use AI at Work."**
  Cover line: **"ONLY 15% DAILY."** (3 words, S-09).
- **Alternatives considered:** title = "The Office Flipped" —
  rejected: this lane's tap is the number; the flip is the continue,
  not the click. Cover "HALF THE OFFICE" — rejected: it repeats the
  title's job instead of promising the turn.
- **Context it serves:** the cover carries the *second* number — the
  one the piece exists to reveal — so the packaging itself opens the
  gap the narration closes.
- **What would change it:** if SOA-02's scoreboard shows gap-covers
  underperforming number-covers, the cover reverts to "52% AT WORK"
  and the turn lives only in the narration.

### DR-2 — THE CONTINUE (first sentence + re-earn map)
- **Choice:** L2 pays the title immediately (no tease debt); L3
  re-earns with speed (21% → 52% in three years); L4 opens the real
  loop — "look closer" is a promise the next line keeps — and L5
  converts the shallowness into the ownership question the close
  answers.
- **Alternatives considered:** leading with the 15% — rejected: the
  deflation only lands if the impressive number lands first; order
  IS the argument.
- **What would change it:** a retention dip at L3 on the 48-h
  scoreboard would move the second read up to L3 and demote the
  climb to packaging.

## 8. Honest weak points

1. Gallup's 52% bar ("a few times a year") is low enough that a
   sceptic can call L2's framing generous. The piece's defence is
   structural: L4 makes the lowness the subject. If Hadi wants the
   bar stated inside L2 itself, the line grows by five words
   ("…at least a few times a year") and L3's rhythm pays for it —
   his call at the lane gate (#29, Option B), not smuggled.
2. "A practice reached almost nobody" — 15% daily use is not nobody;
   the line's "almost" carries that honestly, at the lane's register.
3. L4's "the rest dip in a few times a year" is the bar's shorthand:
   of the 85% who are not daily users, 15 points use AI a few times
   a week. Carried from i1 (whose gate accepted it) and re-logged
   here rather than silently kept.
4. The dateline now uses the survey month (May 2026); i1 used the
   publication month (December 2025). If the lane prefers one
   convention, the alternative dateline is "July 2026" — flagged in
   the refresh job file for the #29 sitting.
