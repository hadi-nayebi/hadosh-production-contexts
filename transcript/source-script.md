## 1. Phase 0 — study brief (sourced; uncertainty flagged)

1. **⚠️ THE DATE IS NOT KNOWN — and the script says so, in
   line 1.** The origin of spoken language is unsettled.
   Published positions: linguistic *capacity* was present in
   the Homo sapiens population **at least 135,000 years ago**
   (genomic divergence argument — every later population has
   full language, so the capacity predates the first split),
   with widespread social use proposed at ~100,000 years ago:
   Miyagawa, DeSalle, Nóbrega, Nitschke, Okumura & Tattersall,
   "Linguistic capacity was present in the Homo sapiens
   population 135 thousand years ago," *Frontiers in
   Psychology* 16:1503900, published **March 11, 2025**,
   https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1503900. Other
   researchers argue for much earlier or gradual emergence
   (Dediu & Levinson 2018; Everett's Homo erectus case). The
   script's anchor — "over a hundred thousand years ago —
   nobody knows when" — sits inside EVERY published range and
   admits the dispute inside the piece itself, per this
   lane's honesty convention (Ep.2's brief set the precedent).
2. **The built statistic (sourced, iron-paired).** Sound in
   air at 20 °C travels at ≈ **343 metres per second** —
   about **one kilometre in three seconds**. (Standard
   reference value: "Speed of sound," Wikipedia, citing the
   20 °C dry-air figure of 343 m/s, 1 km in 2.92 s,
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_speed.) A human on
   foot needs **minutes** for the same kilometre — even a
   strong runner is above two minutes; an ordinary messenger
   far above. The pair (three seconds vs minutes) is the
   piece's mirrored number: same ground, two carriers.
3. **What IS safe to claim:** before speech, an individual's
   knowledge could only move by showing — pointing,
   demonstrating, letting another watch (Ep.1's behavioural
   channel, already taught). Speech is the first channel that
   moves *composed, detailed* information — a route with
   turns, a plan for tomorrow — between minds at the speed
   of sound, to many hearers at once.
4. **⚠️ FLAGGED — animal calls are a partial counterexample**
   (carried from v1, still true). Alarm calls also travel
   faster than a body. What they cannot carry is *new,
   detailed* information. v1's defence lived in its brief
   while its close said bare "information" — **v2 fixes this
   in the narration itself:** L4's cargo is "a whole hunting
   route — every turn of it," so the scope is performed
   before the plain statement (L7) generalises it. See change
   log, item 5.

## 2. Narrative declaration (N-04 / N-06 / N-03 — BEFORE drafting)

- **Structure: And–But–Therefore, re-engined as a speed
  trial.** AND: a warning/information suddenly moves at
  sound speed (L3–L5, with the sourced pair as the stopwatch).
  BUT: the word dies as it is spoken (L6). THEREFORE: the
  first voice was infrastructure — the first road (L8). The
  ABT control structure is KEPT from v1 on purpose (the
  batch's tested variable is the opening device + the number,
  not the skeleton) — and the reason is logged here so the
  repetition is a decision, not a habit.
- **Intended feeling:** a held breath released at speed —
  the wonder of a world that only ever moved at walking pace
  discovering three-seconds-per-kilometre.
- **Villain (N-06), named: The word that dies as it is
  spoken.** Consequence stack: (1) speech leaves no trace —
  when the sound ends, the information exists only in
  memory, the fragile storage Ep.1 already indicted;
  (2) every hearer remembers a slightly different version,
  and there is no original to check against — from the very
  first day, information could now be wrong at the speed of
  sound. Predecessors (not dead solutions — the only ones
  there were): point and show (L2), honoured as the channel
  speech outran, not mocked.
- **The ONE vivid detail (N-03 tyranny rule):** one breath
  carrying a whole hunting route into ten minds at once
  (L4). Speed you can feel in your own chest — kept from v1;
  it earned its place and the tyranny rule protects the
  strongest detail, not novelty.
- **Signature ban applied at declaration:** NO "say it
  plainly" anywhere (v1's L7 carried it — stripped); NO
  "Subscribe, like, and follow" formula (v1's L9 — stripped);
  the bridge is written fresh for this piece (§4, L9). The
  N-09 message (L7) is stated in direct words, unannounced.

## 4. The script (9 lines, 134 tokens counted mechanically —
whitespace split, em dash counted as a token; 118 spoken
words + 16 bridge words)

1. Over a hundred thousand years ago — nobody knows when — a warning began outrunning the animal it warned about.
2. Before speech, what you knew went only as far as your feet. You could point. You could show. That was all.
3. Sound crosses a kilometre in about three seconds. Feet need minutes for the same ground.
4. Then one breath carried a whole hunting route — every turn of it — into ten minds at once.
5. Knowledge had outrun the fastest runner alive.
6. But a spoken word dies the moment it is said, if nobody remembers it.
7. Speech let information jump from mind to mind in a single breath.
8. The first voice was the first road information ever travelled.
9. [Bridge, after the close — T-16] That road runs through marks, stories, and print. Follow Earth One for the rest of it.

MEASURED pace) ≈ 53 s speech; + teaser container (1.6 s
opening card, 2.5 s end card, gaps, payoff hold) → **≈ 57–58 s
total**, inside the ≤65 s teaser ceiling with margin v1 did
not have (v1: ≈63–65 s, no slack).

## 5. Retention reasoning — Decision Records (Q-0)

### DR-1 — THE CLICK (title + cover)
- **Choice:** Title: **"Faster Than Feet."** Cover frame:
  the fire scene at the moment of L4 — one speaker, the ring
  of listeners, route landmarks faint beyond the firelight —
  cover line: **"ONE BREATH. TEN MINDS."** (4 words, S-09).

### DR-2 — THE CONTINUE (first sentence + re-earn map)
- **Choice:** L1 opens a debt in animal terms — a warning
  outrunning the animal it warned about is a small
  impossibility a cold viewer cannot complete (warnings used
  to BE the animal arriving). The script re-earns at named
  beats:
  - **L3 — the stopwatch:** the sourced pair converts the
    opening's poetry into a measurable fact inside ten
    seconds; a viewer who doubted L1 is handed arithmetic.
  - **L4 — the cargo:** speed alone is a physics fact; the
    route makes it information — the series' actual subject
    arrives, and "ten minds at once" is the shareable image.
  - **L5 — the race called:** five words of verdict; the
    cheapest possible re-earn, placed where a scroll-away
    is most likely.
  - **L6 — the reversal:** the triumph is taxed in the same
    breath it was won; the dying word opens the loop Ep.2
    closes — the viewer now needs the series, not just this
    piece.
- **Alternatives considered:** v1's opening (a human first
  speaking, performed) — retained in SPIRIT (the medium
  still demonstrates the message when the narration
  performs L1) but the line itself was rewritten because
  v1's L1 spent the whole hook on the event and left the
  speed — the piece's actual claim — to be discovered at
  L4. v2's L1 leads with the claim's strangest form.
- **What would change it:** Studio's 48-h scoreboard (S-10)

## 9. Honest weak points

1. **L1's scene is still a dramatisation.** Speech almost
   certainly emerged gradually; "a warning began outrunning
   the animal" stages a transition as a moment. v2's
   wording is softer than v1's "a human first spoke" (a
   process verb, "began," no single first sentence claimed)
   — but the scene shape itself remains. Named, deliberate:
   the uncertainty clause + "began" are the honesty devices;
   if Hadi wants the gradualism stated, it costs the hook's
   single image and the trade is his call.
2. **"Feet need minutes" is a range wearing a point's
   clothes.** True for every human who has ever lived (the
   fastest kilometre ever run is over two minutes), but the
   line's confidence is rhythmic, not statistical. The
   Phase 0 carries the exact figures; the narration carries
   the honest bound (nobody does it in seconds).
3. **Territory overlap with Ep.2** (carried from v1):
   mitigated by scheduling (never adjacent) and by subject
   discipline (this piece = SPEED, Ep.2 = BACKUP; L6 is the
   only shared door, and it is a hand-off, not a repeat).
4. **The villain still has no face.** "The word that dies
   as it is spoken" is nameable and stacked, but across the
   teaser batch the villains are properties, not actors
   (TEASER-PRINT v2's Wandering Copy is the same species).
   Logged as a batch-level observation for Hadi's taste
   call — not fixable inside one script without breaking
   the batch's parallel design.
