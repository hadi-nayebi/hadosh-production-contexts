## The one thing the viewer learns (T-17) — unchanged
"Before writing, information lived in living bodies, in two
ways: in the body itself — patterns like a spotted coat,
passed to the young — and in behavior — what the young learned
by watching. If it was not passed on, it was lost."

Essence (≤8 words): **Life stored information in bodies first.**

## Script (11 performance lines, 204 spoken words — counted mechanically)

*Performance lines: line 9 is v4's line 7 split at its own
sentence boundary so the sting gets its own beat — no words
changed (R5).*

1. Writing is only five and a half thousand years old. So
   where did information live before that?
2. Can you guess where it used to live?
3. Inside living bodies. And I mean that directly: a body was
   the storage.
4. Bodies carried information two ways. In the body itself —
   and in behavior.
5. First, in the body. A coat of spots hides an animal from a
   lion. That pattern is information — how to stay alive. When
   the animal had young, the pattern was copied into a new
   body.
6. Second, in behavior. A calf learns the way to the water by
   following its mother. No gene carries that route. It is
   copied by watching, and by walking it, again and again.
7. Copy after copy, both ways carried it for millions of
   years.
8. But there was one fatal rule: die before you pass it on —
   before you have young, before the young have watched you —
   and your information dies with you.
9. There was no backup.
10. The first archive on Earth was not paper or stone. It was
    a living body — and the copying was having children, and
    watching, and learning.
11. The story goes on from here — subscribe to Earth One, and
    take the rest of the walk with us.

**Intra-line beats (F7 — engine-owned, set by punctuation,
recorded here because punctuation is a pause decision):**
- L3, at the colon ("…directly: | a body was the storage") —
  target ~400 ms: the plain statement steps forward out of its
  own sentence.
- L5, at the dash ("That pattern is information — | how to
  stay alive") — target ~300 ms: the concept key gets a
  half-beat of isolation.
- L8, at both dashes — engine beats ~200–400 ms each: the
  nested "before" clauses must stay parseable at first
  hearing.
- L10, at the dash ("It was a living body — | and the
  copying was…") — target ~300 ms before the closing list.

inter-line gaps (vs the shipped build's flat ~4,500 ms across
the same span), no two adjacent gaps the same size unless
their functions are the same.
