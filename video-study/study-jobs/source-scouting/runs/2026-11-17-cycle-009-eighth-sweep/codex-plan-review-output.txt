The queue never silently empties: at any moment, `../QUEUE.md` shows either (a) ≥3 live candidates, each with specimen identity (creator, platform, URL), a one-line learn-reason naming the production surface it feeds, and its entry source; or (b) a dated empty-queue finding in this job's latest run record explaining the drought and what was searched. A candidate whose reason could apply to any video ("good editing") has not been scouted — it has been listed.

## Scope — in / out
- IN: candidate discovery (web/social search, owner-send intake, creator tracking), queue entry + ordering, backlog promotion/retirement with reasons, adjacency mapping to production's next builds.
- OUT: digesting (video-digestion), reception verdicts (reception-analysis — a candidate's view counts are intake data, not a reception finding), contacting creators, any outward action in the owner's name.

- Grain: one cycle = one scouting sweep producing 0–5 queue entries + a queue state report. Quality of reasons over count.

### T01 — Pixel-icon cold open with a stat line (0–13 s)
- **Mechanics:** on black, a white pixel-art icon builds at center in sequence — globe (2 s), ear (4 s), CC badge (6 s), film camera (8 s), couch-and-screen (10 s), seated figure with a question mark (12 s) — each held under a single small-caps caption line at the bottom ("MORE THAN 430 MILLION PEOPLE WITH DISABLING HEARING LOSS" · "RELY ON CAPTIONS TO WATCH MOVIES AND SHOWS" · "HOWEVER, THE CURRENT CAPTIONING SYSTEM HAS SEVERAL SHORTCOMINGS"). One icon at a time; no competing element; the argument advances by icon substitution at a steady ~2 s beat.
- **Why it works:** the pixel register reads as "caption-era technology" before a word of argument lands, and the single-icon discipline makes the stat sequence legible at glance speed.
- **Evidence:** ev/t002-globe-430m.jpg (the globe icon over the 430-million stat line).

### T02 — Numbered shortcomings, signed over conventional captions (13–22 s)
- **Mechanics:** three ASL signers appear in turn against a grey studio ground, each carrying one numbered display line in large white type — "#1 NOT TIMED" (~13–15 s), "#2 DON'T CONVEY EMOTIONS" (~16–19 s), "#3 DON'T IDENTIFY SPEAKER" (~20–22 s) — while a CONVENTIONAL caption bar at the bottom states the same fault in plain form ("CAPTIONS ARE NOT TIMED WITH THE SPOKEN WORD." / "THEY DON'T CONVEY TONE OR EMOTION." / "AND THEY DON'T IDENTIFY WHO IS SPEAKING."). The defect list is enumerated, not narrated.
- **Why it works:** the packet's whole argument is priced in three numbered, timestampable claims — and the film exhibits the old system's flat bar underneath each claim, so the "before" state is on screen the entire time.
- **Evidence:** ev/t014-problem1-not-timed.jpg · ev/t017-problem2-emotions.jpg · ev/t020-problem3-speaker.jpg (each shows its numbered line over its signer, conventional bar below).

### T03 — "1971": the giant pixel numeral as a stagnation stake (22–27 s)

### T06 — Word-by-word spoken-state highlight sweep (38–48 s)
- **Mechanics:** on black, a full caption line sits centered in white; as the voice speaks, each word flips from white to yellow AT ITS SPOKEN ONSET, left to right, so the line's spoken prefix is yellow and its unspoken remainder stays white: "Caption with Intention uses" → "so captions are synchronized" → "precisely as each word is spoken." (at 44 s "each" has just flipped; by 46 s the line is fully yellow). The flip is a color-state change per word; the line does not move. The change is not instantaneous: the 40–47 s clip shows a mid-word state at ~42 s ("dynamic" fully yellow, "text" split yellow/white mid-word, "animation" still white) — the spoken state sweeps through the word as it is spoken, at letter scale. - **Why it works:** progress through the line is visible at every instant — the reader always knows where the voice is, which is the exact failure T02's #1 named.

### T07 — Pre-lighting: the whole line visible before it is spoken (38–67 s, the demonstration sections throughout)
- **Mechanics (the trick):** caption lines in the demonstration sections are FULLY VISIBLE from the line's first frame — future words are already on screen in white (the unspoken state), and each word's spoken onset changes its state (color flip in T06; size/weight in T08). Anticipation is therefore a visibility state, not a reveal: nothing appears at onset; something CHANGES at onset. (The queue's learn-reason described this as words "pre-light in white before turning the speaker's color" — the frames confirm the white pre-state; what turns at onset in the narration demos is the spoken-state yellow, and in scene demos the speaker's color, T09.)
- **Why it works:** the reader can pre-read the whole line (no word ever ambushes), while the state change still marks the voice's position — anticipation and synchronization in one device.
