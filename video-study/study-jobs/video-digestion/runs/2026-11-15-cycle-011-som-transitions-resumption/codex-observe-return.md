## TAXONOMY / CONFUSABLES

What the catalogue will need to distinguish, on the fed record. The specimen is a **tutorial about transitions**, so the catalogue carries two different populations that must not be merged: (A) the six transitions taught, and (B) the tutorial's own teaching devices (definition card, difficulty ladder, countdown pre-announcement, one-variable-isolated demonstration, two-scene reuse). Population B belongs in technique entries only as the specimen's *presentation* techniques, separately labelled — conflating A and B is the single largest taxonomy risk in this packet. Source: Cycle-011 §1 structure record (0–81 s) and lesson-class list.

Confusable pairs the digest must use precise mechanics vocabulary for:

- **C1 — Match Cut vs Cut on Action.** Both cut during movement. Distinction: cut on action preserves the *same action's continuity* across the cut; match cut joins *different* subjects/scenes on a *shared anchor* (here: shape/position — the logo) and, in this specimen's mechanic, lands the cut at the anchor's *fastest movement* (queue head line; Cycle-011 §1 article note). Vocabulary the entries must keep separate: "action continuity" ≠ "anchor correspondence"; "cut at peak velocity" is the match-cut trick here, not a definition of cut on action.
- **C2 — Dissolve vs Morph.** Both pass through blended intermediate states. Distinction: a dissolve is an *opacity crossfade* between two scenes with no shape correspondence; a morph is a *shape/element interpolation* in which features of the outgoing element deform into the incoming one (Cycle-011 §1: "~53–59 s" morph demo; ladder card wording "element morph"). An entry that describes a morph as a "shaped dissolve" (or vice versa) collapses the pair the tutorial's Difficult tier depends on.
- **C3 — Dynamic/Infinite Zoom vs P1 T10 Recursive Zoom / tile dive.** This is the one confusable with a live library entry: Cycle-006 §1 already cites T10 as prior art to distinguish from. Distinction to state: the specimen's dynamic zoom is a *continuous scale move through a scene boundary* (Cycle-011 §2 names a "cursor-follow zoom" trick), whereas T10's recursive zoom dives into a *nested/repeated frame structure*. If the packet does not print this distinction, the style library risks registering a duplicate of T10 under a new name. *Prices Plan decision:* catalogue entry for the zoom technique must carry an explicit "distinguished from T10" line (see also G2 for P3/P4).
- **C4 — Hard Cut vs Cut on Action.** A hard cut is the zero-transition baseline (instantaneous scene replacement, no bridging device); cut on action is a hard cut *timed to an action phase*. The entry for hard cut must not borrow cut-on-action's timing rationale, and the tempo map must not count the baseline as if it demonstrated a timing trick.
- **C5 — Dissolve vs the static CTA end card's fade/hold (negative case).** Cycle-011 §1 records a 19 s static end card (62–81 s) and prices it as a pacing bad. It is not a seventh transition and must not be catalogued as one; its place is §5 bads / pacing, per Cycle-011's own pricing.
- **C6 — Countdown pre-announcement vs a transition.** The "X in 3 · 2 · 1" device (Cycle-011 §1, transcript device) *announces* each demonstration; it is not itself a transition between scenes in the taught sense. Catalogue it, if at all, in population B (teaching device), never as a seventh taught transition.

Mechanics vocabulary the digest must use precisely (each term is load-bearing in at least one entry): anchor shape · peak velocity / fastest movement · action continuity · opacity crossfade · shape interpolation · continuous scale move · shared anchor position. Source: queue head line + Cycle-011 §1. Any entry using "blend", "zoom", or "match" without one of these qualifiers is under-specified — *this last sentence is an opinion of this review, labelled as such per the guard charge; no external source is cited for it.*
