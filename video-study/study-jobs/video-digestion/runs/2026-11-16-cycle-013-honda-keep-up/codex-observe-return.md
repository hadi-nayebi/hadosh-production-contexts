## PRIOR-ART GAPS

**Finding 1 — [PRIOR-ART GAP · PLAN-BLOCKING] The Spritz precedent is missing from Observe, and it is the specimen's immediate technical context.**
Spritz launched its RSVP ("text streaming") reader in 2014, one year before this film; contemporary trade coverage of "Keep Up" itself explicitly connects the two — StopPress (2015-07-17): the ad "appears to have drawn inspiration from text streaming technology Spritz," which removes saccade time by presenting words at a fixed point. *UNVERIFIED as influence:* that ManvsMachine/W+K actually took Spritz as a source is asserted by no creator or agency statement found in this run — StopPress's "appears to have drawn" is a reviewer's inference. File it as contemporary-reception inference, never as production fact.

**Finding 2 — [PRIOR-ART GAP · MAJOR] The RSVP comprehension literature contradicts the transfer the consumer wants, and Observe does not price it.**
The named consumer (DLC repeat-after-me / word-animation containers) wants an accelerating onset curve for a *learning* surface. The reading research says the curve's ceiling is a comprehension cliff, not a free gain: Rayner, Schotter, Masson, Potter & Treiman (2016, *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*) — comprehension is comparable to normal reading near normal rates and falls as RSVP rate climbs, with little evidence that doubling/tripling rate preserves comprehension; Benedetto et al. (2015, *Computers in Human Behavior* 45:352–358) — Spritz-format RSVP *reduced* literal comprehension even at a nominal 250 wpm versus traditional reading, attributed to suppression of parafoveal preview and regressions, and reduced blink rate raised visual fatigue. Average silent non-fiction reading is ~238 wpm (Brysbaert 2019 meta-analysis, as summarised in the RSVP reviews cited). Observe prices "~500 wpm ceiling" purely as the film's craft fact. **Prices Plan decision:** the lessons/extraction framing — Plan must add a priced transfer limit (the brand film *wants* comprehension to fail at the ceiling; a learner container must cap below the comprehension-loss zone and needs dwell/regression affordances RSVP by design removes), or Packet #7's headline lesson will be filed as an unqualified pacing recipe for a learning product. This is a framing gap for Plan/lessons, not a reason to change the specimen.

## TAXONOMY / CONFUSABLES

**Finding 7 — [TAXONOMY · PLAN-BLOCKING] In the established taxonomy this specimen is arguably NOT kinetic typography — it is serial presentation, and Observe inherits the queue's label uncritically.**
Barbara Brownie's classification of temporal typography (*Transforming Type*, 2015; thesis, Univ. of Hertfordshire) distinguishes **serial presentation** — "the sequential presentation of still typographic compositions," containing "no motion or change" within the composition — from **kinetic typography** proper, which divides into motion typography (scrolling typography · dynamic layout) and fluid typography (letterforms transforming in place). A word that sits still for its dwell and is then *replaced by a cut* exhibits no scrolling, no dynamic layout, and no fluid transformation: its only variable is dwell time. The queue line and Observe both file the specimen under "kinetic-typography brand film (word-animation)" without noting the distinction. **Prices Plan decision:** catalogue naming — Plan must decide the head technique's name and class before Execute drafts the catalogue. Recommended vocabulary for Plan to price: head entry = *serial presentation (RSVP replacement)*, with "kinetic typography" retained only as the loose genre label the queue used, and the distinction printed in the packet; otherwise the library will file a dwell-only device among motion techniques (Packet #5's transitions, dynamic-layout work) where a production consumer searching "how does it move" will be mis-served — nothing here moves.

**Finding 8 — [TAXONOMY / CONFUSABLE · MAJOR] RSVP ≠ Spritz-style RSVP ≠ lane word-animation. Three confusable pairs Plan must keep apart.**
(a) *Plain RSVP vs Spritz-style RSVP:* Spritz's variant is defined by optimal-recognition-point (ORP / optimal viewing position) anchoring — each word positioned so its pivot letter (slightly left of centre; O'Regan & Jacobs 1992, as cited in the RSVP literature) lands on a fixed point, the pivot letter highlighted (red in Spritz) — plus dwell weighting: longer words displayed longer, longer pauses at sentence ends (Benedetto et al. 2015, describing Spritz). Whether "Keep Up" uses ORP anchoring or simple centre anchoring, and whether it highlights any pivot, is **UNVERIFIED** externally (see Finding 5) and is exactly what Execute's frames can settle. The catalogue must record anchor type as a measured field, not assume "fixed screen position" (Observe's phrase) covers it.
(b) *RSVP replacement vs Packet #1 T08 full-screen text slam:* a slam is an emphasis hit — a word/phrase arriving as an event inside a continuing composition; RSVP replacement *erases* the prior word and accumulates nothing. Opposite retention behaviour; do not cross-cite as variants without the retention distinction.
(c) *RSVP replacement vs Packet #1 T03 live-typed lyric text:* live-typing accumulates character-by-character with a reading trail the eye can regress over; RSVP removes the trail entirely (no regressions possible — the mechanism behind Finding 2's comprehension cost). **Prices Plan decision:** the catalogue's contrast/citation lines for T03 and T08, and the onset table's field list (anchor type · dwell per word · punctuation-adjacent dwell).

**Finding 9 — [TAXONOMY / MEASUREMENT · MAJOR] "Words per minute" is used in two incompatible senses, and "monotonic acceleration" is an unmeasured assumption.**
Trade-press wpm (500 TV / 1,000 online) is a campaign-level average claim. A measured onset table yields *instantaneous* rate per word (60 ÷ dwell in seconds), which under Spritz-style dwell weighting (Finding 8a) varies word by word with word length and punctuation — locally non-monotonic even inside a globally accelerating ramp. Observe's lesson-class line asserts a "monotonic acceleration" pacing principle at Observe, before any measurement. **Prices Plan decisions:** (a) the tempo map must state which wpm it prints (section-average including product-flash holds and end-card hold, vs instantaneous onset rate excluding them — the two will differ materially in a 60 s film with intercuts); (b) the pacing-principle lesson must be re-worded from the measured curve's actual shape (see Finding 11's stepwise prediction) or printed as a trade-press claim, not a finding. Plan's own failure-point note already bars binding pacing claims to trade-press figures; this finding extends the same bar to the *shape* word "monotonic."

**Finding 10 — [CONFUSABLE · MINOR] Product-flash intercut vs cutaway: the packet must show whether the word stream's clock pauses or keeps running behind a flash.**
Observe names "product-flash intercut at phrase boundaries" as a technique. The confusable is the ordinary product cutaway, where the main stream's timing is irrelevant during the insert. Here the priced question is rhythmic: does the onset ramp *resume* at its pre-flash rate, *step up* across the flash (flash as ramp accelerator), or *restart* (flash as recovery beat)? Each answer is a different technique with a different lesson for the consumer. **Prices Plan decision:** the onset table must span the flashes (flash onset/offset timestamps recorded as rows in the same table), not treat them as gaps in the word data.

## SEGMENTATION PRECEDENT

**Finding 11 — [SEGMENTATION · MAJOR] Long-form precedent does not apply (60 s = short form, correctly ruled in RUN.md §2), but the applicable precedent is RSVP-stream segmentation, which Observe did not record signals for.**
For a serial-presentation stream the established segmentation units are not musical sections and not generic motion beats; they are, in the RSVP/Spritz literature and in this campaign's own descriptions: (a) the *sentence / phrase unit* — dwell weighting and end-of-sentence pauses make punctuation a timing boundary (Benedetto et al. 2015 on Spritz); (b) the *rate band* — the ramp is usefully segmented where instantaneous wpm crosses bands (e.g. conversational ~120 wpm reference point quoted by Honda UK in LBB, through ~250 wpm normal-reading zone, to the ~500 wpm claimed ceiling); (c) the *interruption* — product flashes as section punctuation (Finding 10). Section signals Observe should have recorded before Plan chose S1–S4: total word count of the manifesto; presence/absence of punctuation-visible dwell holds; the audio ramp's shape (Finding 6); and which execution/version is in hand (Finding 4), since a 1,000 wpm online cut segments differently from a 500 wpm TV cut. **Prices Plan decision:** RUN.md §2's S1–S4 model (challenge set-up · acceleration · intercuts · ceiling+release) is a reasonable hypothesis but currently rests on the trade-press narrative, not on recorded section signals; Plan should re-derive its boundaries from the onset table's rate bands + phrase boundaries at Execute and print any boundary that moved. *Prediction (labelled OPINION, to be settled by the onset table):* the measured curve will prove **stepwise** — plateaus within sentences, steps at phrase/flash boundaries — not the smooth monotonic ramp Observe's wording implies.

**Finding 12 — [SEGMENTATION · MINOR] The end-card / brand release is a segmentation boundary of a different kind and should be timed as a hold, not a beat.**
In RSVP terms the held logo/end card is the stream's only long dwell — the recovery that lets the viewer register that they succeeded/failed. Its duration relative to the fastest dwell (a ratio, plausibly an order of magnitude) is the release mechanic. Plan's clip table already rules the end card NO-CLIP with duration binding to the frame census — sufficient, provided the tempo map prints the hold as a measured duration in the same table as the onsets. **Prices Plan decision:** tempo-map format only; no extraction change.

**Finding 13 — [LESSON-CLASS: technique · MAJOR] Dwell weighting as a technique in its own right.**

If the film does this, "dwell weighting (length + punctuation)" is a separate cataloguable technique from "acceleration curve," and it is the one the DLC consumer most needs (a learner container weights dwell by word difficulty, not length alone). **Prices:** catalogue entry list and onset-table fields (dwell must be analysable against word length — transcript/onset table must carry the word string per row, which Plan already intends; the *analysis* step is what is missing).

**Finding 14 — [LESSON-CLASS: trick · MAJOR] The graded-difficulty version system and designed-failure replay loop.**

For the openings consumer this is a distinct trick from the in-film challenge: *difficulty as a versioning axis*. **Prices:** whether the packet's §3.3(7) idea bank / lessons may claim this — it may, but only labelled as campaign-level evidence (trade press), not as a property of the digested file.

**Finding 15 — [LESSON-CLASS: pacing principle · MAJOR] The comprehension-ceiling principle (the inverse lesson for the named consumer).**
Predicted present and currently unpriced: acceleration *past* comprehension is this film's climax device (Findings 2, 9). The transferable pacing principle for a learning container is the inverse — ramp to just below the comprehension-loss zone, then release — and the packet should file both forms (brand form: exceed-and-release; learning form: approach-and-hold) as one principle with two priced ceilings. **Prices:** §3.3(6) lessons wording; without it the packet exports the brand ceiling as if it were a neutral craft number.

**Finding 16 — [LESSON-CLASS: storytelling pattern · MINOR] Enacted proof, distinct from the manifesto arc Observe already names.**
Observe prices "manifesto arc: challenge → claim → proof flashes → sign-off; the argument is enacted." Prior art sharpens this: W+K's own framing (LBB, Scott Dungate) is that the viewer must *feel* improvement in their own reading — the proof is performed on the viewer's body, not shown in the product flashes; the flashes are punctuation, the reading performance is the proof. The storytelling pattern to catalogue is *experiential proof* (claim demonstrated on the audience) as distinct from *manifesto-with-proof-cuts*. **Prices:** §3.3(6) storytelling lesson's wording and the openings consumer's use (a cold-open challenge the viewer performs).

**Finding 17 — [LESSON-CLASS: transcript device · MINOR] Punctuation and word-shape as timing notation.**
Predicted present: copy written for serial display uses not only short words and no subordinate clauses (Observe's pricing) but punctuation placement and word-length distribution *as the score* for dwell weighting (Findings 8a, 13). If Execute's table shows length/punctuation-correlated dwell, the transcript lesson gains its sharpest form: in RSVP writing, the text *is* the timing track. **Prices:** transcript.txt's required fields — punctuation must be preserved per word row, not normalised away in OCR cleanup.

**Finding 18 — [LESSON-CLASS: texture · OPINION · no gap charged] No additional texture prediction is charged.**
Observe's two-texture pricing (flat colour-field type vs photographic flashes, one rhythm) is consistent with all external descriptions found; no source in this run supports a more specific texture claim (specific palette, typeface, or colour alternation pattern).
