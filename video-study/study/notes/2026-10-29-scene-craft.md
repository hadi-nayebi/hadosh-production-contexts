## Question the session set out to answer

Rule C-1 (scene-plan.md §1 of the soa-01 scenes film) decides cadence for ONE film:
target dwell 2.0–3.5 s, ceiling 5.0 s, floor 1.4 s, every switch on a narration
thought boundary, first switch ≤3.0 s. The curriculum calls C-1 "the seed; refine
it with evidence." Three sub-questions: (1) does our own film's realised cadence
support, qualify, or contradict C-1? (2) what does the research say a "thought
boundary" actually is — is punctuation a good proxy? (3) what decides visual
variety without chaos — a question C-1 does not address at all?

**Findings from our own film:**

- **F1 — Band compliance is real but the distribution is near-uniform.**
  SD 0.50 s on a 2.41 s mean (CV ≈21%) means dwell carries almost no emphasis
  signal: the viewer cannot feel which beats matter from time spent on them.
  The longest dwell (S22, 3.45 s) sits on L13, a setup/list line ("rented
  engines"); the shortest dwells sit on the mantra lines L19/L20 (1.59/1.45 s)
  — the lines the doctrine calls the film's peak get the *least* scene time.
  That inversion is partly redeemed by the word layer (the mantra's events are
  HERO, HOLD, expressive), but C-1 as written cannot even express the question:
  it audits band membership, never dwell *allocation*. Dwell is an emphasis
  budget and is currently spent by line length, not by beat importance.
- **F2 — The anchor taxonomy is implicit and mixes strong and weak joints.**
  Classifying the 36 scored anchors: line starts (majority), single-word
  anchors ("billion", "accumulating", "memory", "distilled", "bring"), dash /
  clause joints (L10, L11, L18), sentence joints (L14, L15), and two verdict
  joints given the sharp 0.25 s fade (S10 "Nothing.", S14 the definition).
  C-1 defines a valid anchor as "a sentence start, a clause joint marked by a
  dash or comma … an inventory strike" — a punctuation definition. See F4:
  punctuation is the wrong test.
- **F3 — Variety is achieved by subject change alone; the pairs that work,
  work by state change.** Composition is deliberately uniform (§2: subject in
  the middle band, dark thirds). The near-duplicate pairs — S6→S7 (same desk,
  tighter), S25→S26 (same layer, matured), S8→S9 (same home, lit→dark) — read
  as intentional because a *state* flips (scale, maturity, light) while the
  subject holds. Conversely the two fidelity failures (S12 sheet-music "notes",
  S28 botanical sketchbook) were variety without story: a new picture that did
  not perform the line's logic. No rule currently names which axis must change
  at a switch, nor which axis must hold for continuity.
- **F4 — Tolerance inconsistency in our own records.** Scene-plan §10 verifies
  switch centres "within ±1 frame" of the scored anchor; the ending revision's
  verification plan uses 0.15 s (≈4.5 frames) and its realised audit reports a
  worst offset of 0.05 s. Two tolerances for the same check, never reconciled.

### 3. Proven research / creator craft (sources, read directly)

- **Magliano & Zacks (2011), "The Impact of Continuity Editing in Narrative
  Film on Event Segmentation" (PMC3208769)** — read in full text. Three grades
  of edit continuity (space/time/action). Key results: discontinuities in
  *action* drive event segmentation most; space/time discontinuities are minor;
  and in Zacks et al. (2009), once situational discontinuity is controlled,
  cuts by themselves are associated with *fewer* perceived boundaries — "the
  reason cuts were positively associated with event boundaries was that they
  tended to be points of situational discontinuity." Event Segmentation Theory:
  a boundary is perceived when predictions from the current event model fail —
  i.e. when the *situation* changes (agent, goal, causality, space, time —
  the event-indexing dimensions), not when the picture merely changes.
- **Segmentation-and-memory evidence** (reviewed in PMC3821069 / PMC6675763):
  memory is better for material near event boundaries; deleting boundary
  intervals impairs film memory more than deleting mid-event intervals
  (Schwan & Garsoffky 2004); breaks/pauses inserted *at* boundaries improve
  memory, breaks inserted *between* boundaries impair it (Boltz 1992; Schwan
  et al. 2000); instructing segmentation at natural boundaries improves recall
  up to a week later vs. arbitrary 15 s segmentation (Flores et al. 2014).
  Direct implication: a mid-thought switch is not a neutral miss — it is the
  impairing condition. C-1's anchor rule is the right rule; its punctuation
  test is the weak part.
- **Mayer's segmenting + coherence principles** (Cambridge Handbook of
  Multimedia Learning; effect sizes as tabulated in the 2021 summaries read):
  segmenting d≈0.67 (0.98 in the original learner-paced tests), coherence
  d≈0.86, temporal contiguity d≈1.31. Segmenting's mechanism is learner
  pacing — which a Short does not offer. In a system-paced Short, the scene
  switch *is* the segment marker: it must land where one thought is complete
  enough to consolidate before the next arrives. Coherence bounds variety:
  interesting-but-irrelevant change taxes the same capacity the lesson needs.
  Together they give the variety rule its shape: **every switch must carry
  new story information (a segment boundary), and no switch may carry only
  novelty (a coherence violation).**
- **Creator pacing guidance** (lower trust, used only where it converges with
  the above): pattern-interrupt practice converges on "an interrupt that
  carries no new information habituates — viewers tune it out" (retentionyt
  pattern-interrupt guide) and on 3–5 s interrupt cadence for Shorts
  (influencers-time hook/pacing/payoff structure). The hyper-kinetic claim
  (visual change every 1.5–2 s, aibrify playbook) is *rejected* for this
  layer: it contradicts both the educational-bracket evidence C-1 already
  cites and the segmenting mechanism — at 1.5 s a thought rarely completes.
  Recorded as a considered-and-rejected source, not silently ignored.

## Synthesis — C-1 refined (what changes)

1. **Anchor = situational discontinuity, graded, not punctuation.** An anchor
   qualifies by what changes in the viewer's event model: STRONG (agent,
   goal, custody, or verdict flips — e.g. L10's dash where custody flips to
   "their side"; L6's "Nothing.") · MEDIUM (new sub-event in the same
   situation: a new object enters the inventory, a state flips) · WEAK (a
   comma/dash with no situational change — not a valid switch point on its
   own; the scene rides through it). Sharp transitions belong to STRONG
   joints only (the film already does this at S10/S14; the rule now says why).
2. **Dwell is an emphasis budget.** Band compliance stays (C-1 numbers are
   supported by our film: 0 ceiling breaches, publishable result). Added:
   the plan must state, per scene, whether its dwell is STANDARD (set by its
   line block), EXTENDED (a beat the film wants felt — verdict, definition,
   mantra, designed silence; extension is taken from the band's top, never
   past the ceiling), or FLOOR (a short line carried by the word layer, with
   the carrying event named). A plan whose longest dwell lands on a setup
   line while a verdict/mantra line sits at the floor is flagged at audit —
   the soa-01 pattern (F1) — unless the word-layer carrying is recorded as
   the reason.
3. **Distribution shape is audited, not just the band.** Report mean, median,
   min, max, and the count below/above band (the numbers this session computed
   for soa-01 become the standing report format). Near-uniform dwell
   (SD < ~0.4 s on a ~60–90 s piece) with no EXTENDED scene is a monotony
   finding — the visual analogue of a monotonic lane.
4. **Variety-without-chaos rule.** Every switch names (a) the story axis it
   changes — subject, scale, location, light-state, or situation-state — and
   (b) the continuity axis it holds — subject identity, location, palette/
   style block, or the persisting layer-object. A switch changing no story
   axis is a coherence violation (novelty only); a switch changing all axes
   at once outside a STRONG joint is a re-orientation cost and must be
   justified. State-flip pairs (same subject, one state changed) are a named,
   legitimate device — S8→S9 is the precedent.
5. **One tolerance.** Switch-centre verification tolerance is ±0.15 s for
   scenes+words (the ending revision's realised worst offset was 0.05 s;
   ±1 frame is unachievable across a 0.40 s crossfade whose centre is a
   blend midpoint, and was never actually measured at that grain). The
   scene-plan §10 figure is superseded by this row until the plan is next
   revised; the discrepancy is recorded here rather than silently harmonised.
