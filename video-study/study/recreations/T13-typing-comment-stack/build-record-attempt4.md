## Construction summary (neutral behavior names)

1. **Opening anchor** — the schedule opens mid-sequence: card 1 pre-landed, card 2 pre-typed to the configured prefix (its length is a configured count). The anchor applies only when at least two cards exist and the prefix is a grapheme prefix of card 2; a one-card configuration schedules its card normally from t=0.
2. **Measured fitting** — titles and chrome rows are fitted before playback. When a canvas 2d context exists, text is measured with the configured font string (weight/size/family from the same tokens the styles consume); the wrap simulation collapses whitespace as normal flow does, moves whole words to the next line when they fit, breaks over-wide words at grapheme boundaries, reserves marked-span padding on the line that carries the mark while wrapping, adds configured letter spacing, rounds widths up, and adds a 1px-per-line epsilon reserved during wrapping. When no canvas exists, a per-character-class ESTIMATE table (its class factors are configured values) is used: it is not a measurement and carries no upper-bound guarantee; on that path an element is accepted only when its estimated width is at most 90% of its available bound; inside the top band the fit is rejected as uncertain (fail-closed).
3. **Unit-faithful geometry** — every layout token is interpreted in its own CSS unit: cqw tokens scale with the stage width, px tokens are pixels, % tokens resolve against their named reference length (stage width for stage-relative tokens, the post card's width for the stamp). The entrance displacement is reconstructed by scaling the configured token's numeric part and re-attaching the token's own unit, so a px-configured displacement renders in px.
4. **Complete joint fitting** — in addition to card titles and card header rows, the following surfaces are fitted jointly, each with a named fail-closed error: the post header row (author + handle + close mark), the comments header row (title + sort label), card footers (mark + label), the post footer (all action spans), the stamp text (against its rotated bounds), and avatar initials (against the avatar disc). The post body is fitted as wrapped state: its line count is modelled at the measured width and, on the estimate path, again at 90% of the bound; a pessimistic wrap needing more than one additional line is rejected as uncertain (estimate error in wrapping text manifests as line count, not as width overflow — see the premise amendment below).
5. **Caret-inclusive title fitting** — every reachable grapheme prefix of every title is fitted in both layout modes with the caret (width + margin) charged on the prefix's last line, and the marked range charged where the prefix reaches it. A prefix whose caret cannot fit on its last line may wrap the caret to a further line only while the title's line bound allows it; a caret that fits nowhere within the bound fails closed. The full-title fit additionally rejects a computed maximum line width above the inner width even when the line count passes.
6. **Whitespace-consistent marked ranges** — marked ranges are computed on the whitespace-normalized grapheme sequence in the fitting path, the same normalized sequence the wrap simulation measures, so fitted mark indices and displayed mark indices denote the same normalized graphemes for titles with leading or repeated whitespace.
7. **Fail-closed validation** — if the effective configuration fails any named check (title unsupported or too wide, fit uncertain, chrome overflow, stack overflow, beat invalid, hold shorter than the landing duration, a landing/blink/ending duration that is not positive, finite, and within the catalogue window, prefix mismatch, card count unsupported, phone supporting type below its floor, or a missing configuration group), playback never starts: no card is constructed into the playing stack, the banner names the errors, the machine-readable record is exposed, and the clock controls hold the error state as no-ops. Malformed configuration groups produce the named configuration error rather than an exception.
8. **Override handling (single policy)** — a malformed or out-of-range override value is discarded before effective-config validation and recorded in a separate machine-readable outcomes list; it never freezes playback by itself. Strict full-string numeric parsing is used. Colour overrides are accepted only as six-digit hex values; shorthand forms, named colours, and other values are discarded and recorded with the same outcome shape as numeric rejections, so colour interpolation only ever receives validated hex. Overrides are applied in dependency order: replacement titles first, then beat expansion for the replaced card list, then an accepted typing-rate override applied to every beat including the added ones.
9. **Window allocation** — the performance is allocated across the whole evidenced window. In-window beats (grapheme counts recomputed from the source titles 31/30/47/49/25/22): the second card's remaining 15 graphemes type at 44 ms each (660 ms) and hold 640 ms; the third card appears over 180 ms, types 47 graphemes at 40 ms (1,880 ms), holds 620 ms; the fourth appears over 180 ms, types 49 at 40 ms (1,960 ms), holds 620 ms; the fifth appears over 200 ms, types 25 at 48 ms (1,200 ms), holds 700 ms; the sixth appears over 220 ms, types 22 at 44 ms (968 ms). The ending hold is 1,972 ms: it is the only beat whose job is reading the finished stack rather than building it — about 2.8 times the largest intermediate hold (700 ms, the pre-finale pause) and about 3 times the ordinary holds, so the hierarchy of pauses reads as closure rather than as another gap; the final title is the shortest (22 graphemes) and the sequence's topper, and the hold gives the viewer time to land that line and scan back up the accumulated six-title stack, which is the sequence's stated payoff; at 16.4% of the window it stays below the share a previous allocation was faulted for, and because every other beat is priced first from its own cadence reason, any future beat change must re-price this hold explicitly or the total-equality check fails. During the hold the final card's landing interpolation (120 ms) completes in its first ~6%, the caret is gone, and the landed stack sits in its settled colours — the hold is displayed state, not dead time. The accounting record exposes catalogue window, performance duration (schedule end measured from t=0), and residual; for the specimen configuration performance equals the catalogue window and the residual prints 0.
10. **Landing as a priced visual property** — on landing, the card's background and border colours interpolate over the configured landing duration from the typing colours to configured landed colours. The interpolation is computed from performance time, so its midpoint is a determinate state. Validation requires every intermediate hold to cover the landing duration, so a landing always completes inside its hold.
11. **Performance-time rendering** — entrance progress, landing interpolation, and caret phase are all pure functions of performance time t computed in the render step; the caret's on/off duty fraction is a configured value. The file contains no CSS transition and no CSS animation. A sampled state at time t fully determines the displayed state, identically after restart and after reverse seeking. The caret is one persistent element per card whose opacity is the t-function; it is never recreated per grapheme. The piece starts under its own startup path (playing from load, first frame rendered without any external call) and reaches its scheduled ending under that path.
12. **Active-card tint** — while a card is typing it carries a configured pale-blue-derived background tint, distinct from both the resting and landed backgrounds; title text contrast over both tint backgrounds is modelled in the displayed-state checks.
13. **Complete geometry tokens** — stamp placement and typography, post-card shadow, marked-span padding and radius, badge geometry, caret dimensions, entrance displacement, card foot spacing, banner geometry and banner colours, handle/sort opacities, line heights and font weights (each consumed by both the stylesheet, through variables set from the active mode's token set, and the geometry model), the stage maximum width (applied from configuration at layout time), the added-card entrance/rate defaults, and the estimate table's class factors all live in configuration and are applied from the ACTIVE mode's set (including the card border width, applied per mode at layout time).
14. **Phone type floor** — a configured minimum size for supporting rows (handles, badges, avatar initials, timestamps, footers, sort label) is enforced at phone width: the narrow token set is sized so the smallest supporting row models strictly above the floor (12.02 px at 360 px stage width, 13.03 px at 390 px), and validation fails closed with a named error if a configuration drops any supporting row below the floor. The narrow stage uses border-box sizing, so the modelled stage width (full width including its padding) is the rendered width in both modes.
15. **Added-card beat rule** — when a content override extends the card list, added cards receive the configured added-card entrance and rate defaults; added intermediate cards receive the configured intermediate landing hold; only the true final card carries a zero per-card hold plus the ending beat. The opening prefix is derived from the replacement second card (its first configured-count graphemes), never retained from the default content.
16. **Cluster-preserving segmentation fallback** — without Intl.Segmenter, segmentation keeps base + combining marks (Mn/Me/Mc), variation selectors, emoji modifiers, ZWJ-joined sequences, regional-indicator pairs, and keycap sequences in one cluster. Boundary (printed, not silently mis-split): clusters requiring full UAX #29 extended rules beyond those classes, e.g. Indic conjunct stacks beyond Mc/Mn attachment.
17. **Phone caret scale** — the narrow-layout caret height equals the narrow title font token (≈14.0px at 360px wide) and its width scales proportionally; the wide caret is proportional to the wide title token.

## Mid-cycle premise amendment (filed at Execute, built to the evidence)

Premise (from the wave plan): the post body's fit uncertainty is priced on its maximum line width against 90% of its bound, the same form as titles.
Contradicting evidence (measured on this build before the change): the post body is wrapping prose; its widest modelled line fills 99.5% of its bound in the wide mode and, counting the per-line epsilon and mark padding that the wrap simulation previously added after wrapping, 101% in the narrow mode — line fill near the bound is the normal outcome of wrapping, not evidence of an uncertain fit, and estimate error in wrapping text manifests as an additional wrapped line (height), never as a line wider than the bound, because the simulation breaks lines at the bound. The same measurement showed the wrap simulation was not reserving marked-span padding while wrapping, which is the actual defect behind the narrow over-fill.
Revised construction (built): the wrap simulation reserves mark padding on the mark's line and reserves its epsilon during wrapping, so no modelled line exceeds its bound; post-body uncertainty is priced on the pessimistic line count (wrap at 90% of the bound may add at most one line, else the fit is rejected as uncertain). The caret-inclusive prefix fit received the same evidence treatment: a caret is charged on the prefix's last line (not its widest line), may wrap within the title's line bound, and is uncertain on the estimate path only when it sits on the final allowed line inside the top band. The plan's remaining construction is unchanged.
