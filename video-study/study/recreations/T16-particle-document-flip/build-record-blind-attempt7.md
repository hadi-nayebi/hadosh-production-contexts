WHAT THE BUILD IS
A single self-contained HTML file. A stack of document pages, rendered as particles sampled from config-rendered text and shapes, flips through its page counter (pages 1 to 14 at counter checkpoints 1, 6 and 14, at 0 ms, 1500 ms and 2200 ms of a 6000 ms cycle); the finished stack disperses, a title condenses out of the particle stream with a ring above it, the title holds, then disperses inside a dedicated seam interval (5700–6000 ms) and the cycle restarts with no borrowed particles carried across the seam. Phases: flip [0,2600), release [2600,3100), form [3100,4600), hold [4600,5700), seam [5700,6000). The nominal hold ends at 5700 ms; the cycle rolls over at 6000 ms. Page intervals: 300 ms early, 87.5 ms rapid, and a 400 ms final-page interval. The build starts its own animation loop on load.

CONSTRUCTION FACTS
- Two distinct faces per page turn, on separate particle sets. The OUTGOING face is the leaving page's own line texture: at a page change those particles stay borrowed and fold around the page's left-edge pivot on an asymmetric profile — deepening over the ramp fraction of the turn window to a horizontal factor of −0.12 (the far edge crosses the pivot), a brief plateau, then returning by the window's readable onset (70% of the effective turn window) — and only then release. The INCOMING face is the new page's own sampled target set (outline + header + line texture), unfolding from the pivot: its factor rises from the configured minimum to 1.0 by the same 70% onset. The pool is sized for both faces at once (the full incoming page plus the outgoing texture held through the handoff, grossed up by the reserve fraction).
- Turn and registration: every page change starts its own turn window at the change's SCHEDULED instant; the effective duration derives from the interval it must fit. Page registration starts at scheduled arrival, and a particle's registration duration is capped against the turn's readable onset. A turning particle's intensity binds to its OWN registration, not to the window's end: a particle that has arrived and registered renders at its settled intensity from that instant, even while the window is still running.
- Readability predicates (two, both per-particle): strict = settled, registered, and at full settled intensity; geometric = settled and registered with the particle's face factor at least 0.98. The build's dwell measurements count strict-readable frames only at presentation boundaries (rendered frame instants), and record pages processed (schedule changes stepped past) separately from pages presented.
- Hold accounting is measured, not scheduled: the runtime records each particle's actual registration-completion instant, and the resolved hold is derived from those measured instants. At the defaults, joint title-and-ring registration completes at 4626 ms (just after the 4600 ms nominal hold entry), and the observed resolved hold — joint completion to hold exit at 5700 ms — is 1074 ms of the 1100 ms nominal hold. The build names both hold events in its diagnostics: nominal hold end 5700 ms, cycle rollover 6000 ms.
- Feasibility is a single gate with exactly two outcomes. A candidate configuration is sanitized, then assessed against a conservative PRE-COMMIT lifecycle bound computed before any execution: per-interval schedule lifecycle (travel + stagger + span-derived registration + the reserved hold), formation lifecycle (delay + travel + stagger + full registration + a detection allowance + the supported tick lag), complete page coverage of the executed schedule (every page in the page count appears in the sampled schedule), positive intervals, the intensity hierarchy WITH a minimum separation between adjacent levels, palette contrast floors for each particle colour against the configured background, magnitude bounds on every numeric value, and non-empty target sets for every required form. FEASIBLE: the resolved configuration is applied whole and execution starts from it. REFUSED ATOMICALLY: the candidate is rejected as a unit, the previously applied configuration remains in force untouched, execution does not proceed on the candidate, and the refusal is written to an observable rejected-configurations record with its reasons. A schedule fallback result is itself a candidate through the same gate. At a cold start the shipped defaults are the standing prior: a refused startup candidate is recorded and the defaults restored whole. There is no configuration state in which an infeasible resolved configuration executes, and no advisory feasibility flag exists in the build.
- Shared resolved values: the turn onset fraction, the fold plateau fraction, the geometric readability threshold and the ring's horizontal centre each live in configuration as a single validated key, and every consumer (registration cap, both face profiles, the geometric predicate, ring sampling) reads the same resolved value.
- Wall-clock pacing: the frame loop accumulates real elapsed time and advances the simulation in bounded sub-steps (each at most the 50 ms frame cap), processing every crossed page/phase boundary in chronological order — segment entries for windows a sub-step crosses entirely still run, in order, including target-set initialization for crossed form windows. Residue above the per-callback sub-step bound is carried forward, never discarded. Driven with 100 callbacks of 120 ms, the simulation advances 12000 ms against 12000 ms of wall time, residue 0.
- Boundary ordering: a release whose scheduled end has passed is evaluated to its endpoint — scatter anchor, displayed offset and reference offset all committed — BEFORE any borrow decision may select the particle, so travel never begins from a stale sampled release position. Capture likewise takes the travel anchor origin from the position the particle occupies at the capture instant, including that tick's stream advance, so the tick's displacement is carried into the travel curve.
- Releases: a release starts from the particle's actual rendered position with its rendered velocity captured, blends that derivative out over a configured fraction of the release window, and eases to rest at a stream point. Per-release randomness, stated exactly: two seeded draws per release — the first displaces the scatter target's x (a +240 px coherent bias plus draw × the configured range), the second its y (a −200 px coherent bias minus draw × range × the configured y-factor); the seed source is the configuration's seed through the run's deterministic stream.
- Formation assignment is assignment-truthful: a formation flag is set only when its borrow assigned every target; a short borrow records its shortfall and retries on later ticks while its window remains. Identity: page targets carry stable keys; line targets key on their page line index and position.
- Counter: the page counter (label + numeral) is fitted as one group against the space right of the page/stack region, with a numeral floor and an above-page fallback position. Text fitting: title and header are fitted in width and height using measured ink bounds; the ink width is the sum of the measure's left and right ink extents. Fitted results are asserted as positions, not only sizes.
- Validation: configuration is validated for finiteness, ranges and text content. Sampling thresholds cannot survive at a value that yields empty target sets (the threshold is range-bounded and the gate additionally refuses an empty required target set). Every policy value the fitting, timing and validation logic consumes is a configuration key.

TECHNICAL QUESTIONS THIS CONSTRUCTION ANSWERS IN CODE
- Is hold accounted from scheduled instants or from observed registration completion? From observed completion, measured per particle (see above).
- Can execution proceed on a configuration its own feasibility computation rejects? No — one gate, two outcomes, applied whole or refused atomically with the refusal on record.
- Does validation cover only previously named cases? The check suite below executes a broader adversarial set: magnitude and non-finite values on EVERY numeric field, null/absent groups, empty required text and target sets, a single oversized token, a checkpoint-fraction grid, page-count extremes, intensity equalization, and a late-failure revalidation whose subsequent behavior is compared against an unchanged control.
