The build has useful reuse mechanisms, but production cannot yet safely supply arbitrary new content. The 74 passing checks cover selected cases; the source exposes gaps beyond that coverage.

- **List replacement is explicit and predictable.** `cardsReplace` and `schedule.replaceCards: true` replace the whole list; ordinary array overrides patch by index. Empty replacement lists are rejected. Replacing with fewer than three cards in default specimen mode also requires updating `specimenCard: 2`; validation names that invalid index.
- **Validation is incomplete.** Specimen reveal, hold, and free timings lack finite-number checks. Sequence exit/free timings and hold settings also lack comprehensive validation. Negative or string values can therefore produce invalid schedule arithmetic without an error. Null configuration groups such as `type`, `stage`, or `geom` can reach property accesses and throw instead of displaying an actionable message. Unknown-key warnings are exposed programmatically but never displayed.
- **The fit contract has a horizontal-overflow hole.** `wrapText` never splits or rejects an individual oversized word. A long unbroken quote, speaker name, or role token can pass the height and line-count checks while overflowing horizontally. Ordinary multiword text receives useful deterministic shrinking and attribution limits, but those checks do not establish safe fit for all supplies.
- **Fit controls themselves are unguarded.** A zero `fit.stepPx` can cause an infinite shrink loop; a negative step can prevent termination. A positive step can overshoot the declared floor. Font sizes, line heights, and floor relationships also lack validation.
- **Per-card timing control works in sequence mode.** Each card supplies its slot and reveal span, and the cycle derives from the list. However, longer replacement content does not receive a reading-duration floor derived from its word count: token count only selects between two fixed post-reveal holds.
- **Narrow geometry is derived at startup.** Width scaling and remeasurement support the checked 360px and 320px cases. There is no resize listener to recompute geometry or fit after a viewport change, no minimum supported width, and no validation that configured regions remain inside the card. The claim that every horizontal geometry value scales also overstates `H_KEYS`, which omits border and attribution-rule widths.

The disclosed browser and Georgia verification limits remain material. More importantly, source-level counterexamples already break the promised rejection and fit guarantees. This is a reusable foundation for controlled inputs, but it needs stronger validation, horizontal fit enforcement, and viewport lifecycle handling before leaving the practice pool.
