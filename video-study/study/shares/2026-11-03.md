**What the record actually says:** our best publish package was never made the template. The HLjOhOkhkkY package caught Studio open on the WRONG CHANNEL before upload and read back 13+ settings after publishing — and none of that became a standing check. Meanwhile two standing files carried a platform "fact" that was already false: "Shorts take no uploaded thumbnail." Our own package had uploaded a custom thumbnail three days after that foreclosure was written, and YouTube's designed 9:16 cover (2160×3840, desktop Studio) had been live since July 2026. A package built from the standing files would have skipped the cover step entirely, silently.

**What changed:**
- **NEW `files/publish-package-standard.md`** — one joined package checklist for Shorts: channel verification before upload → title with its click record → cover as THREE separate decisions (feed = first frame; designed 9:16 cover; frame-pick fallback when eligibility is unavailable) → description, tags, pinned comment, playlist → the settings audit (the HLj field list) verified in Studio after publishing → outcome record → 48 h spot-checks.
- **QC v2.22:** P-05 now templates against the new standard (not the 2026-10-20 package); **P-10** requires the channel check + post-publish settings read-back; **P-11** requires both cover surfaces.
- **Corrections in place:** the falsified foreclosure is replaced in `files/distribution-defaults.md` and in decision-model §15 (rows 15.2 and 15.12 expanded to the three cover objects and the full audit list); D-04's evidence trigger now also fires on pooled batch views, since no single scoreboard has ever reached its n ≥ 10 threshold.
- Exercised, not just written: the HLj package audited against the new standard passes 9 of 12 sections — its gaps (no pinned comment, no playlist record, no title alternates) are exactly the parts no checklist had joined.

**Why it matters for production (including long-form):** a publish package is now one checklist with the settings audit inside it, so the next publish — Shorts or, by analogy, long-form — verifies its channel and reads its settings back instead of trusting that upload-time clicks landed. And one durable lesson: a platform foreclosure written into a standing file needs a falsification check against our own newest package, or it quietly governs the next nine days of work.
