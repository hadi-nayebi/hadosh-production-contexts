# Study share — 2026-11-05 — Topic 7: Transcript craft for Shorts

**What was studied:** how a Short's script is governed today — the
opening (hook + orientation), the one-message rule, and how the
words we write become the words the voice actually speaks
(delivery markup on our speech engine, Shortwave).

**What was found:** the pieces existed but were never joined.
Our markup standard — the rule that the exact text fed to the
voice generator is a decided artifact, with capitals, dashes and
pauses each placed on purpose — had stood for eight days cited by
no checklist and no decision model. One checklist line still gave
the opposite advice ("simplify the punctuation"). And two older
standards disagreed about which version of the script the voice
reads: one said the spoken form, the other said the marked form,
and neither mentioned the other.

**What changed (all in the DIY goal's files/):**
- A new joined standard, `transcript-craft-standard.md`: the
  opening checked as one system (first line quoted, about eight
  words / two seconds, orientation in the same beat), the
  one-message discipline in one place (payoff lands at roughly
  80–90% of the runtime; the last lines carry the return, not the
  first payoff), the script's FOUR forms reconciled in one table
  (content → spoken form → marked synthesis text → on-screen
  display text), and the measurement loop that calibrates each
  mark on real takes.
- The quality checklist advanced to v2.23: the stale punctuation
  line corrected in place, and a new mandatory row (T-20) — a
  script must carry all four forms, with a reason recorded for
  every mark or for leaving a line unmarked on purpose.
- The decision model gained §6A (six rows) so these choices are
  counted, not assumed.

**Why it matters for production:** from the next script on, a
script cannot pass its checks with the voice's text undecided.
What the viewer hears — stress, pauses, energy — is written down
and measured before a take is accepted, instead of being whatever
the generator defaults to. The same four-forms discipline applies
to long-form scripts: the marked text is a deliverable, not a
by-product.
