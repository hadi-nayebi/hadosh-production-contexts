# Study share — 2026-11-08 — Topic 10: scenes+words visual craft skill

What was studied: whether the visual layer of our scenes and words Shorts has a governing skill. The standards themselves are strong and complete: the decision model covers word events, scene cadence, canon style, asset identity, light, motion, and the gates measured on the delivered file, and canon gives the visual style law. The sweep found those standards are loaded by no skill. The two nearest skills do different jobs, one is a preproduction gate and one is a reviewer instruction. So a builder who did not already know the file paths would build without the standards ever reaching the work. That is the same gap the transcript skill closed yesterday, one layer over.

What changed: a new skill, scenes_words_visual_craft, now sits in the custom skills folder. It works as one method in build order. Load the governing sections first. Derive the style from canon, copying the style block word for word and assigning colour roles per plate. Plan scene cadence, with every switch graded by anchor strength and dwell spent as emphasis. Design word events by their cue function, with dosage reported as three separate quantities and a table that states what each motion means. Decide each asset fully, including when it leaves, and hold a frame to a few meaningful elements. Verify on the delivered file itself, through the preproduction gate, the measured gates, a style drift audit on real frames, and the sound-off test where the story must read from pictures and animated words alone.

Why it matters for production: the published scenes film was judged as random images because style prompts were improvised scene by scene. Under this skill that pattern fails at the first step, before any asset is made. For long-form work that reuses these visual standards, the same load list applies: canon style first, cadence and word events planned, motion meaning stated, and verification on the finished file rather than on stills or intentions.

Remaining coverage gaps in this topic, named for later sessions: audio and music arrangement, and community-post craft. The cinematic 3D journey topic waits until this coverage completes.
