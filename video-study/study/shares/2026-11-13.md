# Study share — 2026-11-13 — Topic 1, Scene Craft (second rotation)

**What was studied:** our scene-cadence standard (decision-model §10C,
written 2026-10-29 for the scenes+words films) read side by side with
the owner's Assembler Product Spec (2026-11-12), under which videos
are containers placed one after another — opening, transition,
repeat-after-me, transition, diagram, transition, ending, final —
with transitions as full containers, not glue.

**What changed:** §10C now states its scope and gained two rows.
Inside a piece or inside one container, nothing changed: the dwell
band (2.0–3.5 s, 5.0 s ceiling), graded anchors, and crossfade-only
scene switches stand. Between containers, new row 10C.9 applies the
anchor grading (a container change with no real change in the
situation is a design finding) but not the dwell band — a container's
length is a configuration choice, not a band breach — and row 10C.10
makes clear the crossfade-only rule neither bans nor governs
transition containers.

**Why it matters for production:** before today, §10C read literally
against an assembled video produced false defects everywhere — every
container "too long", every transition block "banned" — while
ignoring §10C for assembled videos would have lost anchor grading at
exactly the joints where the big flips happen. Now one cadence
standard covers both grains, each explicitly scoped. Useful for
long-form planning too: grade the joints between blocks, don't
band-check the blocks themselves.
