# Share note — 2026-11-14 — Word events, second pass

Today's study topic was word-event design — how the words on screen are chosen to move, appear, and act. It was the second pass over this topic, so instead of studying it again from scratch, the session checked the standing rules against everything that has changed since they were written.

Two things had changed. First, our videos are now built as a stack of containers, and words can live inside any of them — including the transitions between parts. The old rules only ever measured a whole video at once, so a transition carrying words could be completely flat and still hide inside a good average. The rules now also report each word-carrying container on its own, and a transition with words gets planned as its own segment.

Second, the video study library has now proven a set of word techniques by rebuilding them — typing that appears letter by letter, a full-screen text slam, a marker highlight swept across a phrase, and a typing stream cut off mid-word into a slam. Four of those proven forms are now on an approved list that a word plan may choose from, each with its name and proof level written beside it. Three more stay closed until their proof is finished. A form joins the list only through a study session like this one — never just because it looks ready.

One honest edge the practice run found: the interrupted-typing form breaks the reading-time rule on purpose, so the rule now says exactly when that is allowed — only when the slam that completes the phrase stays on screen long enough to read.

Production difference: the next stacked video's word plan will show its numbers per container, not just per video, and its palette now includes every proven word form the library has earned since the rules were first written.
