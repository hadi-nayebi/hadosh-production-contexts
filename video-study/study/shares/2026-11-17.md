# Share note — 2026-11-17 — Topic 5: Retention & Studio Evidence (second pass)

Today's study went back to the rules written on 2026-11-02 for reading what YouTube Studio tells us after a video publishes — and checked them against everything the production system has become since.

Three things were wrong, and all three are fixed in the files:

1. **"Complete" meant only one thing.** One rule used the word for a scoreboard round that simply had a file for every published video. But two other rules quietly relied on the same word for two more jobs: naming which old reads are still waiting for their 7-day and 28-day re-reads, and giving every spike on a retention curve a written verdict. Now the checklist names all three jobs separately, so a round can pass one and fail another — and its record has to say which.

2. **The planning book pointed at one rule out of five.** The decision model's post-publish section named only the basic scoreboard rule. The re-read ladder, the pooled-judgment limits, and the rule about films whose quality verdict later changed were in the checklist but not discoverable from the planning section. That section now carries a short note naming all of them. The standing record shows why this matters: our three real scoreboard files were all closed as final at 48 hours, and none names its re-read state, on videos long past 28 days. That open ladder is now a named finding with an owner.

3. **One new rule is written but honestly marked "not yet proven".** Videos are now built as a sequence of containers. When a published container-built video earns a readable retention curve, each dip or spike should be recorded against the container or joint it fell on — with the warning, written into the rule itself, that where a feature fell is a record, not a cause. No container-built video has published with a readable curve yet, so this rule could not be exercised on a real case. It stands in the checklist marked provisional in our records — landed, cited, and waiting for its first real test, which is charged to that video's first scoreboard round. We did not count it as a finished conversion.

Files changed: the QC checklist (now v2.24) and the decision model's post-publish section. The study job's own instructions also gained a rule from today's lesson: a plan must name, for every proposed rule, the real case it will be exercised on — before the work starts, not after a reviewer catches the gap.
