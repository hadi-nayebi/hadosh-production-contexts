## 3. Technique catalogue

Timestamps are seconds from 0. Evidence labels describe what the cited frame itself shows (Amendment A4 clause 2): countdown frames are cited as countdown states of a sequence, and the two motion-defined techniques bind their motion claims to their clips.

### T01 — Logo-mark assembly opening (0–3 s)
Mechanics: three flat triangles (red, green, blue) — the School of Motion mark — slide/settle into their cluster on black, then the wordmark resolves under them. Nothing else competes; the brand geometry is the first moving object. Why it works: the same triangle geometry returns as the match-cut anchor (T09) and the outro (T13), so the opening is also a promise the film keeps. Evidence: ev/1.0-opening-logo.jpg (frame form: the assembled mark on black).

### T02 — Pencil-check task sting (3–7 s)
Mechanics: a drawn pencil enters from top-left and ticks a checkbox on a flat indigo field; small drawn arrows/burst marks punctuate the beat. Why it works: a "quick tip" contract is set physically — one small task, checked off — before any teaching starts. Evidence: ev/5.0-pencil-checkbox.jpg (the pencil at the checkbox).

### T03 — Timeline dive (7–12 s)
Mechanics: the view pushes into a stylized editor timeline (colored clip blocks, easing-curve panels, "QUICK TIPS" title cards riding the timeline), travelling along it until one card fills the frame. Why it works: the tutorial's subject is editing craft, so the camera enters through the editor's own surface; the dive is itself a dynamic-zoom transition (T10) used before it is taught. Evidence: ev/7.5-quick-tips-timeline.jpg (the timeline mid-dive).

### T04 — Definition card (11–14 s)
Mechanics: a full-screen gradient card lands: "TRANSITIONS — Animated changes between two scenes to provide visual continuity" (text verified verbatim in the cited frame). The card's lower motif (a dotted path over a capsule shape) stays as a quiet footer. Why it works: the whole taxonomy is anchored to one sentence the viewer can repeat; every later demo is an instance of this sentence. Evidence: ev/12.0-definition-card.jpg (the card, text legible).

### T05 — Complexity ladder (14–17 s; revisited 29–30 s and 44 s)
Mechanics: a three-row menu card — Easy (Hard cut · Dissolve) / Intermediate (Cut on action · Match cut) / Difficult (Dynamic zoom · Element morph) — with an orange playhead triangle marking the row currently being taught; the card returns between tiers so the viewer's position in the taxonomy is never lost. Why it works: difficulty, not chronology, is the organizing axis, so a beginner can stop after Easy and still hold a complete, usable set. Evidence: ev/17.0-ladder-easy.jpg (Easy row marked) · ev/44.0-ladder-difficult.jpg (Difficult row marked).

### T06 — Hard cut (demonstration ≈17–22 s)
Mechanics: after the countdown (T12), a line-drawn laptop builds on a pink field; at the cut point the frame switches instantly — no blend, no intermediate frames — to the webpage scene. The article's when-to-use: fast-paced action or music, camera changes within a scene, impact, re-timing clips to an audio beat, alternating viewpoints. Why it works as a demo: because both scenes are already familiar (T03–T05), the viewer sees the cut itself, not new content. Evidence: ev/20.0-hardcut-before.jpg (laptop scene, pre-cut state) · ev/22.0-hardcut-after.jpg (webpage scene, post-cut state) — the pair is the evidence form; a hard cut has no mid-frame.

### T07 — Dissolve (≈23–30 s)
Mechanics: the webpage scene cross-fades into the logo/laptop imagery over roughly a second — both scenes coexist at partial opacity at the midpoint — ending on the mark. The article's when-to-use: passage of time, change of location, flashback/retrospection, scene-end fades. Why it works: the overlap duration IS the meaning (lingering = time passing), which is why the demo holds the midpoint instead of rushing it. Evidence: ev/26.0-dissolve-mid.jpg (sequence state: the incoming laptop scene at dissolve) · ev/29.5-dissolve-logo.jpg (the resolved mark) · clips/25.0-dissolve.mp4 (the blend's duration — the timing claim's binding).

### T08 — Cut on action (≈30–36 s)
Mechanics: the laptop lid's opening/closing motion starts in one framing and completes in the next; the cut lands mid-action so the motion vector carries the eye across the edit. The article's when-to-use: angle changes without disrupting the scene, impacts, throws/launches, hide-and-reveal. Why it works: the viewer's attention is on the moving object, and the cut hides inside the movement it is already tracking. Evidence: ev/34.0-cut-on-action.jpg (the laptop mid-action) · clips/33.0-cut-on-action.mp4 (the cut's placement inside the movement — the timing claim's binding).

### T09 — Match cut (≈37–44 s)
Mechanics: elements are pushed into frame and the cut lands at their fastest movement, on a shared anchor shape — in the article's words, Jacob "illustrates this transition technique with the shapes in the School of Motion logo, pushing into them as they come into view and cutting to the next clip at their fastest movement," with "the logo acting as an anchor for the viewer." The countdown frames show the webpage header element highlighted/framed as the anchor candidate before the cut. Why it works: composition, not content, is continuous — the eye lands on the same shape in the same place and reads two scenes as one gesture. Evidence: ev/40.0-matchcut-before.jpg (pre-cut state) · ev/42.5-matchcut-anchor.jpg (countdown state "Match cut in 2": the webpage scene with its header element highlighted — cited as the anchor-selection state, not as the cut itself) · clips/39.5-match-cut.mp4 (the motion claim's binding: the push-in and the cut at peak movement).

### T10 — Dynamic / infinite zoom (≈45–52 s)
Mechanics: the camera follows the cursor into the laptop screen and through it into the webpage scene — a continuous scale move with no cut; the incoming scene is a sub-frame of the outgoing one, so the zoom reveals it as something that was inside all along. The article's when-to-use: any sub-framed element (a window in a room, a screen in a scene) can carry the viewer into the next shot. Why it works: scale replaces the cut as the continuity device, and the cursor gives the viewer a guide to follow. Evidence: ev/48.0-dynamic-zoom.jpg (the zoom in progress) · ev/50.5-zoom-webpage.jpg (the arrived webpage scene) · clips/48.0-dynamic-zoom.mp4 (the continuous scale move — the motion claim's binding).

### T11 — Element morph / scene morph (≈53–59 s)
Mechanics: one scene's elements deform continuously into the next scene's — shapes stretch, re-color and re-settle rather than being cut or faded; the article prices morphing as "the most awe-inspiring, but also the most complicated" of the six, and notes its prevalence in logo animation. Why it works: identity is preserved through change — the viewer watches a thing BECOME the next thing, which is the strongest continuity claim any transition can make. Evidence: ev/55.0-scene-morph.jpg (countdown state "Scene morph in 1" over the webpage scene — the pre-morph state, cited as such) · clips/52-scene-morph.mp4 (the motion claim's binding: the deformation itself).

### T12 — Countdown pre-announcement ("X in 3 · 2 · 1") (throughout, ≈17–59 s)
Mechanics: every demonstration is preceded by a full-width lower-third label naming the technique and counting down — "Hard cut in 3/2/1", "Dissolve in 3/2/1", "Cut on action in 3/2/1", "Match cut in 3/2/1", "Dynamic zoom in 3/2/1", "Scene morph in 3/2/1" — each numeral holding about a second over the outgoing scene. Why it works: the viewer knows exactly what to watch for and when; attention is pre-aimed at the transition instead of spread over the scene. This is the tutorial's signature transcript/visual device: the narration's list structure is made visible as a metronome. Evidence: ev/42.5-matchcut-anchor.jpg ("Match cut in 2") · ev/55.0-scene-morph.jpg ("Scene morph in 1") — both frames show their countdown labels verbatim.

### T13 — Logo outro sting (59–62 s)
Mechanics: the triangle mark re-assembles on black exactly as in T01 and the wordmark resolves; under three seconds. Why it works: bookending — the film's last image is its first, restated after the taxonomy has given the mark six new meanings. Evidence: ev/59.0-logo-outro.jpg (the mark on black).

### T14 — Static CTA end card (62–81 s)
Mechanics: a single static card — "GRAB A FREE STUDENT ACCOUNT AT SCHOOLOFMOTION.COM" with a bullet list and social handles — holds unchanged for the remaining ≈19 s. Catalogued because it is the film's actual ending container, and priced in §5 as its largest defect: it is a still, not a transition, in a film whose subject is transitions. Evidence: ev/70.0-cta-endcard.jpg (the card; sheet5–6 grids show it unchanged across the full span).

## 4. Tempo map

Total 80.99 s. Teaching content occupies 0–62 s; the end card holds 62–81 s.

| Section | Span | Beats | Pacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening (T01–T03) | 0–12 s | logo assembly · pencil sting · timeline dive | fast, one device per ~4 s; no narration-dependent pauses |
| Frame (T04–T05) | 11–17 s | definition card · ladder card | the film's slowest teaching beats — two held cards, reading time given |
| Easy demos (T06–T07) | 17–30 s | countdown + demo, ×2 | metronome: ~3 s countdown, ~3–4 s demo per technique |
| Intermediate demos (T08–T09) | 30–44 s | countdown + demo, ×2 | same metronome; demos slightly longer (match cut needs its push-in) |
| Difficult demos (T10–T11) | 44–59 s | countdown + demo, ×2 | same metronome; zoom demo is the longest single demo (~7 s) |
| Outro (T13) | 59–62 s | mark re-assembly | brisk bookend |
| End card (T14) | 62–81 s | none | full stall — 19 s, 23% of runtime, zero motion |

Pacing curve: a strict countdown metronome across all six demos (accelerates nothing, stalls nowhere inside the teaching span), bookended by a brisk opening and a complete stall at the end. The uniformity is the design: with pacing held constant, the only variable between demos is the transition itself.

## 5. Goods and bads

### Goods
- **G1 (0–62 s) — one variable per demo.** All six demonstrations alternate between the same two scenes (laptop, webpage), so the transition is the only thing that changes; a viewer comparing hard cut vs dissolve is comparing transitions, not scenes.
- **G2 (11–14 s) — definition before taxonomy.** The one-sentence definition card (T04) precedes the ladder, so every demo is read as an instance of a stated claim.
- **G3 (14–17 s) — difficulty as the organizing axis.** The ladder (T05) lets a beginner leave after Easy with a complete working set; the returning card re-anchors position at 29–30 s and 44 s.
- **G4 (≈17–59 s) — countdown pre-announcement.** T12 pre-aims attention before every demo; nothing the film teaches happens unannounced.
- **G5 (37–44 s) — the match-cut demo teaches its own mechanic.** The anchor is the brand mark the opening already taught (T01), so the demo's key idea — cut at fastest movement on a shared shape — is visible without explanation.
- **G6 (companion article) — every transition carries a when-to-use list.** The video shows; the article decides. Together they form a choice-by-job reference, which is exactly the form a production consumer needs.

### Bads
- **B1 (62–81 s) — the end card eats 23% of the film.** A static CTA holds ~19 s of an 81 s tutorial whose subject is motion between scenes; it demonstrates none of the six techniques and teaches nothing. Cut test: removing it loses only the offer text, which the article carries anyway.
- **B2 (≈17–59 s) — the countdown metronome becomes predictable cost.** Six identical ~3 s countdowns spend ~18 s of the 42 s demo span on announcement; by the fourth demo the device no longer adds information, it only delays. (Priced against G4: the first two countdowns earn their time; the repetition is the defect.)
- **B3 (≈53–59 s) — the morph demo is the least legible.** The hardest technique gets a demo whose pre-state (ev/55.0) reads as an ordinary webpage frame; the deformation is fast and dark, and a viewer who blinks misses the one moment the film exists to show. The clip in this packet (clips/52-scene-morph.mp4) carries more of the technique than any frame can.
- **B4 (whole film) — no real-project footage.** Every demo uses the same two synthetic scenes; the film never shows a transition inside a finished piece of work (the article's Psyop "Endless Perfection" example is a separate embed, not part of the video), so transfer to real scenes is asserted, not demonstrated.
- **B5 (720p frames) — small-text resolution limit.** Ladder sub-lines and timeline panel text do not resolve at phone size in the 1280×720 source; claims in this packet that rest on sub-line text are paraphrase-marked as article-grounded rather than frame-grounded (T05's tier membership is headline-legible; the per-tier member lists are confirmed against the article).
- **B6 (whole packet) — no verbatim transcript.** The caption fetch failed (HTTP 429, not retried); narration wording is therefore not quoted anywhere in this packet, and delivery claims are limited to the on-screen devices (T12) and the article text.

## 6. Lessons mapped to our containers

| # | Lesson | Class | Container | Change it implies | Build cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Choose the transition by narrative job, from this tutorial's six-row table (hard cut = impact/beat · dissolve = time/location change · cut on action = angle change inside motion · match cut = compositional rhyme · dynamic zoom = enter a sub-frame · morph = identity-through-change) | technique | transition | The assembler's transition container gains a `job` field whose value selects the transition type; no transition is chosen by habit or decoration | cheap — a configuration table + the six mechanics in §3 |
| L2 | Cut at fastest movement on a shared anchor shape (T09) | trick | transition | Match-cut containers require a named anchor element present in both adjacent scenes, and the cut point is the anchor's peak-velocity frame | moderate — needs per-scene anchor metadata + peak-frame timing |
| L3 | Follow the cursor (or the learner's focus object) through a sub-frame to enter the next scene (T10) | trick | transition · diagram | Zoom containers take a `subframe` target (screen, window, card) instead of a generic scale animation | moderate — nested-scene composition |
| L4 | Pre-announce every demonstration with a named countdown (T12) | transcript device | transition · word animation | A `countdown` lead-in block (name + 3·2·1) becomes a reusable container prefix for teaching sequences — and is capped: after two uses it shortens (B2's cure) | cheap — one parameterized block |
| L5 | Definition before taxonomy: one sentence, held on a card, before any list is taught (T04) | storytelling pattern | opening · diagram | Opening containers for explainer pieces state the piece's one-sentence definition as a held card before the first content beat | cheap — a card template + a hold-duration rule |
| L6 | Organize a technique set by difficulty ladder, and re-show the ladder between tiers (T05) | storytelling pattern | diagram · opening | Multi-technique pieces carry a ladder card that returns with the current tier marked; position is never left to memory | cheap — one card, re-rendered per tier |
| L7 | Hold the scenes constant and vary only the device when teaching a device (G1) | pacing principle | transition · diagram | Comparison sequences in our lanes reuse one scene pair across variants instead of re-skinning per variant | cheap — a production rule, no new machinery |
| L8 | The countdown metronome: constant announcement rhythm across a demo series (T12, §4) | pacing principle | transition | Series of demonstrations share one lead-in rhythm so tempo itself carries no false signal about importance | cheap — timing constants in the container config |
| L9 | Two-scene flat system as a teaching stage: one object scene + one interface scene, one gradient card family, one geometric motif (T01/T04/T05, whole film) | texture | opening · transition · diagram | Our tutorial-style pieces can stage demos on a fixed two-scene stage with the motif carried through opening, cards and outro, instead of designing a new world per piece | moderate — a stage template + motif asset set |
| L10 | Never end on a static card: the ending container must itself move (B1's negative lesson) | pacing principle | ending | Ending containers are barred from holding a still for more than a beat; the CTA rides a final demonstration or the bookend sting (T13) | cheap — a rule + the T13 bookend pattern |
| L11 | Morph only where identity-through-change is the claim (T11) | technique | transition | Morph is reserved for logo/identity moments and concept-becoming-concept beats; it is not a default scene change (cost + legibility, B3) | expensive — path deformation per element pair; reserve it |
| L12 | Dissolve duration is meaning: hold the midpoint when time passing is the claim (T07) | technique | transition | Dissolve containers expose duration as a semantic parameter (short = soft cut, long = time passing), never a fixed default | cheap — one parameter with named bands |
