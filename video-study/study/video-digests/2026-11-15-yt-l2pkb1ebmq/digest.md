### [ARC] Architecture

**P6-T01 — Script-semantic filename convention (the metadata layer is the filename)** · 02:56–03:35
Mechanics: after downloading the image set, the creator renames every file to match the script's language for that image — his worked example: an Archduke portrait becomes "Franz Ferdinand assassination" (transcript 03:11–03:24). The rename is prescribed BEFORE upload, priced on camera at "a few extra minutes" that make "the whole editing process a lot easier" (03:24–03:35). The filename is the only scene-matching metadata the system ever receives: no manifest, no timing, no order field. Why it works: the assembler's matching problem is reduced to string/semantic affinity between script sentences and filenames — the cheapest possible metadata layer, and the one a non-technical user can actually produce. Evidence: transcript 02:56–03:35 (verbal claim, transcript-bound); its payoff is visualised at ev/t205.jpg (image → scene-list match card, §P6-T19).

## 5. Goods and bads

### Goods (priced, timestamp-bound)
- **G1 — The claim is verified inside the video (00:00–01:47).** The absolutist title is treated as a hypothesis the first two sections test: product shown before process, at length, uninterrupted. Most tool tutorials assert; this one exhibits.
- **G2 — The one load-bearing step is given the longest dwell (02:56–03:35).** The rename step — the only step whose omission degrades the output — gets ~39 s, a worked example, and a consequence statement. Instruction time is allocated by failure cost, not by step count.
- **G3 — The revision round teaches the transferable skill (07:38–09:32).** Specify the parts, paste the script excerpt, name the style — a reusable prompting pattern for any conversational editor, demonstrated end-to-end with its result.
- **G4 — The designed shell never lets screen time carry the argument (§P6-T10, throughout).** Every screen insert is preceded by a card stating what it proves; a viewer who reads only the cards still gets the whole method.
- **G5 — Rights hygiene is placed at the moment of risk (02:52).** The usage-rights card interrupts the download step, not the description — the caution stands where the behaviour happens.
- **G6 — The input contract is auditable (04:20–04:30, 09:37–09:42).** Three items, named identically at assembly and at the close; a viewer can check their own folder against the film.
- **G7 — The bargain is honest about its currency (00:52).** "All I ask: watch until the end" prices the free plugin in attention, openly — no fake scarcity on the artefact itself.

### Bads (priced, timestamp-bound)
- **B1 — "No Editing!" is contradicted by the video's own best section (07:38–09:32).** The revision round is editing by instruction (§P6-T05); the title's absolutism survives only because the packet — like the video — must name the operation precisely to see it. A viewer who believes the title misunderstands where their labour went: into specification and judgment, which the video elsewhere (G3) teaches well.
- **B2 — The hardest creative step is the one skipped (01:47–01:52).** "I'm not going to show you how to create the video script" — the script (written with ChatGPT) is the input everything else matches to; its quality determines the film's, and it is the only input the tutorial does not teach. The method's floor is set by an untaught step.
- **B3 — Success-only demonstration (05:31–06:00, §P6-T14).** No failure is ever shown: no mismatched image, no bad generation, no repair. The "low effort" caveat (05:42) and the ~15-minute generation are reported, never examined — the viewer cannot price the workflow's failure rate or iteration cost from this specimen.
- **B4 — The "free" frame omits the running costs (00:41–00:52).** Five hours of the creator's training time, a Claude subscription capable of the work, and ElevenLabs are all real costs the bargain frame (G7) does not price; the method is free to watch, not free to run.
- **B5 — Repeated narration pads the runtime (01:06–01:47 vs 06:09–06:50).** The documentary's opening passage is heard twice near-verbatim (~40 s of duplication, §4 sag map) — the second playback's new material starts only at ~06:46.
- **B6 — Load-bearing screens are illegible at watch size (05:31–06:00).** The Claude Code session screens — the only direct evidence of the assembly act — are shown small and dense; their text cannot be read in the frame (this packet's own evidence frames show the same limit). The proof of the central act is the least legible material in the video.
