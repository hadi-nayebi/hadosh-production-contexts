### T01 — Fade-from-black colonnade reveal (opening)
- Timestamps: 0.00–3.04 s (span bounded by census boundaries at 0.56 s and 3.04 s, @0.35 ledger).
- Mechanics: the film opens on black; the first image is a shaded colonnade with a hanging red textile, revealed as exposure/motion settles. No title, no logo — the place itself is the first statement.
- Why it works: a dark-to-place reveal makes the viewer lean in before any cutting starts; the journey's contract (watch, don't listen for plot) is set in the first three seconds.
- Evidence: ev/000-open-black.jpg (opening black, 0.16 s) · ev/001-open-colonnade.jpg (colonnade revealed, hanging red textile centre, 0.96 s).

### T02 — Single voiceover passage (transcript device)
- Timestamps: 11.5–32.2 s (the film's only speech segment; ASR census, small model, VAD — one segment in 212.68 s).
- Mechanics: a single poetic spoken passage (credited "voice off meryem aboulouafa") rides over the opening images, then never returns; the remaining ~180 s are instrumental music only. ASR wording is garbled and is NOT quoted as transcript — the passage's exact words are UNVERIFIED at this packet's evidence level (paraphrase marking, Amendment A1 candidate form).
- Why it works: one voice passage frames the montage as a spoken invocation, then withdraws so the images carry the argument; its scarcity is the device.
- Evidence: audio record (ASR census in the production record — the evidence form is the named data record, per Amendment A4 clause 3) · ev/020-poster-graphic.jpg (the poster insert the passage spans, 20.5 s).

### T03 — Graphic poster insert (icon as shot)
- Timestamps: ~19.0–22.2 s (census boundaries 19.04 / 21.36 / 22.24).
- Mechanics: a printed portrait poster on a red flag fills the whole frame — a flat graphic treated as a full shot inside a photographic montage, held long enough to read as an icon, not a detail.
- Why it works: the montage's grammar admits any rectangle as a shot; a national icon lands as a chapter marker without a title card.
- Evidence: ev/020-poster-graphic.jpg (poster portrait filling frame, red field right, 20.5 s).

### T04 — Portrait holds (rest beats)
- Timestamps: throughout; exemplar 28.8–36.4 s (the elder portrait; census boundaries 28.84 / 36.44 give a ~7.6 s shot — the film's longest class).
- Mechanics: a face to camera, held well past information delivery, at intervals inside fast montage. The subject does not act; the hold itself is the beat.
- Why it works: holds are the montage's rests — they reset the eye, give the music a face, and make the surrounding density feel faster by contrast.
- Evidence: ev/031-portrait-elder.jpg (elder portrait held, sunlit, 30.5 s) · ev/191-portrait-hold.jpg (elder woman portrait inside the 189 s rapid sequence — the same device at montage speed, 189.48 s).

### T05 — Detail-to-wide match on action
- Timestamps: cut at 81.8 s (census cluster 81.16 / 81.80 / 82.44).
- Mechanics: an extreme close-up of hands rolling dough (motion-blurred, frame-filling) cuts to the wide shot of the same activity — a woman making flatbread at her griddle. The action and subject are continuous; only the scale jumps. Observable: hand working dough → wide kitchen. Inferred construction: cut on the shared action, not on a cover.
- Why it works: the viewer completes the scale change themselves because the action never breaks; it is the film's clearest teachable transition — detail first, context second, no cover needed.
- Evidence: ev/081-hand-detail.jpg (hands rolling dough, motion-blurred close-up, before the cut, 81.72 s) · ev/082-gozleme-wide.jpg (the same activity wide, after the cut, 81.88 s). Clip: clips/081-match-action-1.4s.mp4 (81.2 s + 1.4 s).

### T06 — Foreground-arch framing (panorama through a silhouette)
- Timestamps: ~98.0–102.4 s section; exemplar frame at 100.5 s.
- Mechanics: a city panorama (rooftops, harbour, Bosphorus) is viewed through the dark silhouette of a tower's arch openings — the foreground is near-black, static in frame, and occupies both edges while the lit city sits in the aperture.
- Why it works: the silhouette gives the wide shot a frame inside the frame and a viewing position (you are somewhere, looking) — the difference between a postcard and a witness shot.
- Evidence: ev/100-tower-arch-panorama.jpg (panorama through dark arch silhouettes, 100.5 s).

### T07 — Occlusion-framed cut landing
- Timestamps: cut at 102.40 s (census boundary).
- Mechanics: a panning townscape with minaret cuts to a dark interior where a guitarist is seen between passing foreground silhouettes — the new shot lands already occluded, so the eye accepts the scene change while it is still reading the foreground. Observable: the landing shot's first frames are majority-dark foreground. Clip: clips/100-occlusion-pass-3.5s.mp4 (100.0 s + 3.5 s) carries the pan → cut → occluded landing as one span.
- Why it works: the cut's discontinuity is spent on the dark foreground, not on the subject; by the time the guitarist is legible the new scene is already established.
- Evidence: ev/102-minaret-pan.jpg (townscape pan, before the cut, 102.0 s) · ev/103-guitar-occluded.jpg (guitarist between foreground silhouettes, after the cut, 102.8 s).

### T08 — Same-event multi-angle performance cutting
- Timestamps: ~132.8–138.8 s cluster (boundaries 132.84 / 135.00 / 135.44 / 135.72 / 136.68 / 137.16 / 137.60 / 138.84).
- Mechanics: one musical performance (singer with frame drum, violin beside him) is cut across angles inside the event — wide singer → violin close → singer profile — at sub-second to ~1 s intervals, all inside one room and one song.
- Why it works: the event's own rhythm licenses the cut rate; the viewer reads acceleration as excitement, not as scene change, because place and sound never change.
- Evidence: ev/135-drummer.jpg (singer with frame drum, before the 135.44 s cut) · ev/136-violin.jpg (violin close, after the cut, 135.6 s). Clip: clips/135-sound-bridge-2.5s.mp4 also carries this cutting under the continuous track.

### T09 — Foreground pass, mid-coverage
- Timestamps: ~189.3 s, inside the 188.5–193.9 s rapid cluster.
- Mechanics: a bright foreground body/object passes so close to the lens that the frame's right half is a white blur while the street scene continues legible at left — a photographed wipe caught mid-pass. Whether the edit hides inside this pass is NOT established at this evidence level (the census boundary nearest is 189.20/189.40); the entry catalogues the pass itself, with the hidden-cut claim explicitly unmade (P-A1/P-A2 discipline).
- Why it works: a full-coverage pass is the strongest cover a photographed transition can have — anything can change behind it; even without a cut, it resets the frame's composition.
- Evidence: ev/190-foreground-pass.jpg (white blur covering the frame's right half mid-pass, street scene at left, 189.32 s).

### T10 — Rhythmic insert montage (sub-second inserts)
- Timestamps: 188.48–192.96 s cluster (boundaries at 188.48 / 189.20 / 189.40 / 189.60 / 189.64 / 191.44 / 191.84 / 192.96).
- Mechanics: four unlike inserts in about a second — a hand holding a BB pistol (insert) → street man with a foreground pass → an elder woman's portrait → a night tram — each ~0.2–0.5 s, none returning. Density here is ~4× the film's median.
- Why it works: at this speed the inserts stop being scenes and become percussion; their only shared property is Turkey, which is the film's whole argument in miniature.
- Evidence: ev/189-gun-insert.jpg (pistol insert, 189.0 s) · ev/191-portrait-hold.jpg (portrait insert, 189.48 s) · ev/192-tram-night.jpg (tram insert, 189.8 s). Clip: clips/189-rhythmic-montage-1.6s.mp4 (188.8 s + 1.6 s).

### T11 — Continuous-music sound bridge (whole-film glue)
- Timestamps: 0–212.68 s (structural).
- Mechanics: one instrumental track (credited ludovico einaudi) runs unbroken under every cut — loudness census: mean −11.5 dB, max 0.0 dB, ZERO silence gaps ≥1.5 s at −35 dB in the whole film. No cut is ever exposed to silence, so no cut needs visual cover to survive.
- Why it works: this is the precondition for the film's cut rate — the ear experiences one continuous gesture while the eye is cut 100+ times; remove the bridge and the same edit reads as a slideshow.
- Evidence: the loudness/silence record (named data record, production record) · clips/135-sound-bridge-2.5s.mp4 (audiovisual excerpt spanning the 135 s cuts, music continuous).

### T12 — End card over final shot, then black hold
- Timestamps: card over the dawn-balloon shot from ~209 s; cut to black at 210.32 s; black hold to 212.68 s.
- Mechanics: the credits are overlaid in small tracked-out caps on the film's most beautiful shot (hot-air balloons at dawn), then the same card is held on black for the final ~2.4 s. The film ends twice: once as image, once as text.
- Why it works: the overlay borrows the shot's emotion for the credits; the black hold gives the music a clean room to end in.
- Evidence: ev/205-balloons-dawn.jpg (the dawn-balloon shot before the card, 205.0 s) · ev/210-endcard-shot.jpg (card overlaid on the shot, 209.9 s) · ev/211-endcard-black.jpg (the same card on black, 210.7 s).

## 5. Goods and bads
### 5.1 Goods (timestamp-bound)
- G1 (81.8 s): the detail-to-wide match on action (T05) — the single most transferable transition in the film; scale change with zero cover and zero confusion.
- G2 (102.4 s): the occlusion-framed landing (T07) — a scene change the viewer never has to process as an edit.
- G3 (11.5–32.2 s): the single voiceover (T02) — one spoken passage, then restraint for 180 s; the scarcity is disciplined.
- G4 (150–180 s): the settling section — the film slows down (18 candidates/30 s) before its climax instead of monotonically accelerating; the climax at 180–210 s is earned by the rest.
- G5 (209–212.68 s): the double ending (T12) — card over the best shot, then black; credits get emotion and clarity in sequence, not either/or.
- G6 (0–212.68 s): the unbroken music bridge (T11) — zero silence gaps; the montage's licence.
- G7 (100.5 s): foreground-arch framing (T06) — witness-position wide shots instead of postcards.

### 5.2 Bads (timestamp-bound)
- B1 (189.0 s): the BB-pistol insert (ev/189-gun-insert.jpg) is the montage's one sour note — a weapon close-up with no setup or payoff inside a warm portrait of a country; at insert speed it reads as provocation, not information.
- B2 (180–210 s): the climax density (76 candidates/30 s, median shot ~0.44 s in the @0.20 ledger) sacrifices legibility — several inserts (the 189 s cluster) are gone before they can be read; the argument survives on rhythm alone.
- B3 (11.5–32.2 s): the voiceover's mix sits low under the music at its start — at this packet's ASR evidence level the passage is not reliably intelligible (small-model ASR garbles it), and a first-time viewer gets tone, not words. (Priced as an intelligibility risk, grounded in the ASR record, not as a transcript claim.)
- B4 (209.9 s): the end-card overlay's small lines (voice/music credits) are low-contrast tracked caps over a bright dawn sky — the directed-by line reads, the smaller credits strain (ev/210-endcard-shot.jpg); they only become fully legible on the black hold.
- B5 (20.5 s): the poster insert (T03) assumes the icon — a viewer who does not know the face gets a graphic beat with no information; the film never prices the outsider.
- B6 (36–102 s): S2's landscape/craft run is the sag risk — density is mid-range and the subjects (terraces, chandelier, market) are the film's most postcard-like; the cut at 81.8 s (T05) is what rescues the section's back half. Compression floor for the whole film: the same journey survives at ~90 s by keeping S1's voiceover + poster, T05, T07, one portrait hold, the S4 rest, and the S5 climax at reduced count — below ~90 s the rests (T04) go, and with them the film's distinguishing patience.
