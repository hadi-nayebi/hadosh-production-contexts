### T01 — Scale-cascade cold open (galaxy web → one small observer)
- **Timestamps:** 0:00–0:20 · **Mechanics:** the film opens on a dense animated galaxy-network field, then pulls to a lone astronaut figure in empty space as the narration narrows from "hundreds of billions of galaxies" to "we see nothing." The cascade is a continuous zoom/dissolve flow, not cuts (0 hard cuts in S1 at the 0.35 census).
- **Why it works:** the paradox is staged as a visual contraction — abundance drains into emptiness inside one unbroken move, so the question "Where is everyone else?" (0:12) lands as a picture the viewer has already seen.
- **Evidence:** ev/8-opening-galaxy-web.jpg (the abundance state) · ev/18-emptiness-where-is-everyone.jpg (the contracted state) — a before/after pair; the move between them is continuous (scene census: no boundary in 0–60 s).

### T02 — Thesis-as-spoiler (the answer is previewed before the questions)
- **Timestamps:** 0:20–0:50 (transcript) · **Mechanics:** before any argument, the narration states the conclusion in compressed form: "we are early… but very soon this may change… An irreversible competition for the universe might be about to begin" (0:20, 0:27, 0:35). The essay then spends 9 minutes earning a claim the viewer already holds.
- **Why it works:** orientation replaces suspense — a speculative chain this long cannot run on withheld conclusions; the preview converts every later section into evidence for a known verdict.
- **Evidence:** transcript.txt [00:20.23], [00:27.79], [00:35.77] (verbal claim, transcript-bound).

### T03 — Disclaimer with literalised visual gag
- **Timestamps:** 0:50–1:00 · **Mechanics:** the speculation disclaimer ("based on scientific papers… little data and lots of extrapolation, so take them with a grain of salt") is delivered AFTER the cold-open thesis and BEFORE the argument — and staged literally: the duck pours from a bottle labelled "Imagination" as the salt line lands. Placement, not just presence, is the device: hook first, contract second, argument third.
- **Why it works:** the contract is signed at the last moment it can still govern everything after it, and the gag makes the abstract instruction ("discount what follows") a physical act the viewer watches.
- **Evidence:** ev/55-disclaimer-duck-imagination.jpg (the pour, mid-disclaimer) · transcript.txt [00:50.65].

## 5. Goods and bads

### 5.1 Priced analyses the goods/bads stand on

- **Structure (P-A1 claims/dependency map):** the argument is a CUMULATIVE DEPENDENCY CHAIN, not a branching tree — hard steps (S2) → timing window (S3) → loud-civilisation deadline (S4) → quiet-civilisation dismissal (S5) → race conclusion (S6). It carries exactly TWO branch points: the reserve-respecting loud variant (7:26 — even polite expansion traps latecomers) and the closing decision fork (9:01 — stay quiet or expand). The queue line's "branching-scenario escalation" therefore reads, on the evidence, as cumulative escalation with two priced branches; the packet files it in that corrected form.
- **Deadline arithmetic (P-A5 reconstruction):** inputs as narrated — appearance window ~1 trillion years (4:32) · civilisations appear ~at random inside it · hard steps make early success rare · the Sun's remaining habitable span ~1 billion years (3:57). The narrated result follows: few if any before today, a cohort bunching inside "this period of a billion years that we are in" (4:42), then all remaining starfarers close together — the bunching scale is ~0.1% of the window, and the "deadline" is NOT a date: it is the moment the first loud front arrives, which the model leaves undated. Inputs, assumptions, and result agree internally; the packet prints the deadline as a distribution argument, never as a schedule.
- **Epistemic inventory (P-A2):** the specimen does BOTH honesty forms. (a) The front disclaimer (T03, 0:50). (b) Continuous local hedging through the argument — transcript counts: "if" ×12 · "might" ×6 · "could" ×6 · "probably" ×4 · "likely" ×3 · "maybe" ×2 · "we don't really know how hard these steps are" (1:46) · "consequence of all these assumptions and ideas" (8:29). The contrast with Packet #2 is therefore one of DEGREE and PLACEMENT, not kind: Packet #2 marks uncertainty inside its artwork per-claim; this specimen signs one contract up front AND hedges locally — but its strongest sentences (T02's thesis, the title) are exactly the ones that escape hedging (§5-B1/B2).

### Goods
- **G1 — The disclaimer is placed where it can still govern (0:50–1:00).** After the hook has earned attention, before a single argumentative claim is built — and literalised as a gag (T03) so it is watched, not skipped.
- **G2 — Orientation never lapses across 592 s of essay.** Announced question ladder (T04), two chapter cards at the two irreversible turns (T11), and a thesis preview (T02): a cold viewer can say where they are at any minute mark (net-viewed across both contact sheets).
- **G3 — Every abstract quantity gets a physical staging.** Hard steps → board-game path (T05) · multicellularity ×25 → animal cast (T06) · stellar lifespan → rows (T09) · bunching → game-lobby chart (T10) · deadline → approaching border (T16). No load-bearing number is left as a numeral.
- **G4 — The analogy is produced at full weight (6:10–6:56).** The forest-clearing sequence (T14) is shot like a primary scene; the squirrel line lands the deadline's moral content before the galaxy version asks to be believed.
- **G5 — The ending pays the argument off emotionally, in both directions.** Threat personified (T19, 9:10) then greeting staged (T20, 9:46): the call to action is powered by a face to fear and a table to earn.
- **G6 — Sources are part of the artefact.** A dedicated sources page is linked in the description and the model is named as a paper-based idea in the disclaimer itself (0:50) — a viewer can leave the video and check the claim's home.
- **G7 — The merch pitch is labelled as advertising on its face** (small "Advertisement" print on the poster-grid frame, ev/605-merch-grid-advertisement.jpg) — the outro's commercial nature is disclosed inside the work, not only in platform metadata.

### Bads
- **B1 — The strongest claims precede the disclaimer (0:20–0:50).** "An irreversible competition for the universe might be about to begin" is delivered as the cold-open thesis BEFORE the 0:50 contract tells the viewer it rests on "little data and lots of extrapolation." The hedge exists — but a viewer who stops at 0:45 (the retention-critical window) carries away the claim without its discount.
- **B2 — The title overstates the model ("already on their way").** The essay argues civilisations will bunch in the future and that fronts will eventually move; no civilisation is shown or claimed to be en route now — the deadline is explicitly undated (§5.1). The title converts a distribution argument into a present-tense arrival the content does not support.
- **B3 — 65 s of outro for a 592 s essay (9:52–10:57; 9.9% of runtime).** The argument ends at 9:52; what follows is a four-state merch sequence plus a ~27 s static end card. As pacing, the film's last tenth carries no argument and its delivery rate collapses to 90 wpm (§4).
- **B4 — The quiet option is under-argued before the fork (S5: 47 s vs S4's 144 s).** Quiet aliens are dismissed in the shortest movement of the essay ("not really a concern for us," 8:04), largely on a single-sample argument (humanity is going loud, 8:11) — then S6 prices staying quiet as a fate to fear. The fork at 9:01 is real, but one prong was never given its best case.
- **B5 — The race frame outruns its own caveat (9:12 vs 8:30–9:52).** "Meeting others does not necessarily mean war or conflict" is conceded in one sentence, then the section's card, staging, and close all price meeting as border competition. The caveat is present; the framing overrides it.
- **B6 — Diagram micro-labels fail at phone size (throughout, e.g. T10's join notifications, T08's axis ticks).** The load-bearing shapes read fine, but the small text layer is decorative at delivery size — the packet paraphrases it and rests no claim on it; a production borrowing T10 must re-size that layer or drop it.

### 5.2 Length priced as craft (Amendment A3, specimen >5 min)
- **What the length BUYS:** the three-question architecture (each question needs its own staging grammar: path, timelines, analogy); the forest analogy at full weight (G4); the two-step emotional close (T19→T20). None of these survive a 3-minute cut.
- **SAG MAP:** (a) S3's red-dwarf passage (3:51–4:32) states the window point three times (Sun short-lived · red dwarfs long-lived · random-appearance inference) — cut test: merging the 4:15 inference into 4:07 loses a restatement, not a step. (b) S7's held end card (~10:30–10:57) — cut test: nothing is lost at 5 s. (c) S2's waiting-astronaut beat (2:40–2:52) repeats S3's "long times" point early — cut test: the gag's laugh, not information.
- **COMPRESSION FLOOR:** ~6 minutes. A 6-minute version keeps: hook + disclaimer (1:00) · hard steps as the path visual only (1:00) · window + deadline chart (1:30) · loud front + forest analogy compressed (1:30) · fork + close (1:00). It drops: the quiet-alien movement's nuance, the menagerie and candles beats, the second chapter card — i.e., it keeps the chain and loses the breathing room that makes the chain feel inevitable rather than asserted.
