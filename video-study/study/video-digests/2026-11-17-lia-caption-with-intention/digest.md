## 3. Technique catalogue

### T01 — Pixel-icon cold open with a stat line (0–13 s)
- **Mechanics:** on black, a white pixel-art icon builds at center in sequence — globe (2 s), ear (4 s), CC badge (6 s), film camera (8 s), couch-and-screen (10 s), seated figure with a question mark (12 s) — each held under a single small-caps caption line at the bottom ("MORE THAN 430 MILLION PEOPLE WITH DISABLING HEARING LOSS" · "RELY ON CAPTIONS TO WATCH MOVIES AND SHOWS" · "HOWEVER, THE CURRENT CAPTIONING SYSTEM HAS SEVERAL SHORTCOMINGS"). One icon at a time; no competing element; the argument advances by icon substitution at a steady ~2 s beat.
- **Why it works:** the pixel register reads as "caption-era technology" before a word of argument lands, and the single-icon discipline makes the stat sequence legible at glance speed.
- **Evidence:** ev/t002-globe-430m.jpg (the globe icon over the 430-million stat line).

### T02 — Numbered shortcomings, signed over conventional captions (13–22 s)
- **Mechanics:** three ASL signers appear in turn against a grey studio ground, each carrying one numbered display line in large white type — "#1 NOT TIMED" (~13–15 s), "#2 DON'T CONVEY EMOTIONS" (~16–19 s), "#3 DON'T IDENTIFY SPEAKER" (~20–22 s) — while a CONVENTIONAL caption bar at the bottom states the same fault in plain form ("CAPTIONS ARE NOT TIMED WITH THE SPOKEN WORD." / "THEY DON'T CONVEY TONE OR EMOTION." / "AND THEY DON'T IDENTIFY WHO IS SPEAKING."). The defect list is enumerated, not narrated.
- **Why it works:** the packet's whole argument is priced in three numbered, timestampable claims — and the film exhibits the old system's flat bar underneath each claim, so the "before" state is on screen the entire time.
- **Evidence:** ev/t014-problem1-not-timed.jpg · ev/t017-problem2-emotions.jpg · ev/t020-problem3-speaker.jpg (each shows its numbered line over its signer, conventional bar below).

### T03 — "1971": the giant pixel numeral as a stagnation stake (22–27 s)
- **Mechanics:** the caption line "DESPITE THIS, CAPTIONS HAVE REMAINED UNCHANGED, SINCE 1971" first appears lettered in dissolving particles (~22 s), then the year itself lands as a screen-filling pixel numeral "1971" (~24 s) and holds. One number carries the whole stagnation claim; nothing else moves.
- **Why it works:** a date at that scale is a stake, not a fact — the viewer feels the 50-plus years as size. The particle dissolve into the numeral also bridges the pixel-icon register of T01 into the title.
- **Evidence:** ev/t024-since-1971.jpg (the numeral at full frame over the "remained unchanged" line).

### T04 — Title lockup reveal (27–29 s)
- **Mechanics:** after a near-black beat with the word "INTRODUCING" entering at small size (~26 s), the system's name lands as a clean lockup — a CC-derived mark + "Caption with Intention" — centered on black (~28 s) and holds ~1 s before the system montage. The reveal is a state change (dark → lockup), not a build.
- **Why it works:** the problem section's noise (particles, pixel type) resolves into one quiet, modern lockup — the register change itself announces "the new system."
- **Evidence:** ev/t028-title-lockup.jpg (the lockup centered on black).

### T06 — Word-by-word spoken-state highlight sweep (38–48 s)
- **Mechanics:** on black, a full caption line sits centered in white; as the voice speaks, each word flips from white to yellow AT ITS SPOKEN ONSET, left to right, so the line's spoken prefix is yellow and its unspoken remainder stays white: "Caption with Intention uses" → "so captions are synchronized" → "precisely as each word is spoken." (at 44 s "each" has just flipped; by 46 s the line is fully yellow). The flip is a color-state change per word; the line does not move. The change is not instantaneous: the 40–47 s clip shows a mid-word state at ~42 s ("dynamic" fully yellow, "text" split yellow/white mid-word, "animation" still white) — the spoken state sweeps through the word as it is spoken, at letter scale. Synchronization here is DEMONSTRATED as speech-associated state flips on frames; the film's grid (T05) names the design — Roboto Flex variable axes — while automatic real-time operation remains UNVERIFIED (P-A1).
- **Why it works:** progress through the line is visible at every instant — the reader always knows where the voice is, which is the exact failure T02's #1 named.
- **Evidence:** ev/t044-sync-highlight-mid.jpg (mid-sweep: spoken prefix yellow, remainder white) · ev/t046-sync-highlight-complete.jpg (the completed line, fully yellow). Clip: clips/040-sync-highlight-7s.mp4 (40–47 s).

### T07 — Pre-lighting: the whole line visible before it is spoken (38–67 s, the demonstration sections throughout)
- **Mechanics (the trick):** caption lines in the demonstration sections are FULLY VISIBLE from the line's first frame — future words are already on screen in white (the unspoken state), and each word's spoken onset changes its state (color flip in T06; size/weight in T08). At 55 s the line "so you can feel when my voice gets louder or softer" stands complete on screen with its spoken prefix yellow and "louder or softer" still white, BEFORE the size states those words will take. Anticipation is therefore a visibility state, not a reveal: nothing appears at onset; something CHANGES at onset. (The queue's learn-reason described this as words "pre-light in white before turning the speaker's color" — the frames confirm the white pre-state; what turns at onset in the narration demos is the spoken-state yellow, and in scene demos the speaker's color, T09.)
- **Why it works:** the reader can pre-read the whole line (no word ever ambushes), while the state change still marks the voice's position — anticipation and synchronization in one device.
- **Evidence:** ev/t055-line-before-ramp.jpg (the complete line visible, spoken prefix yellow, unspoken remainder white, before the ramp states) · ev/t044-sync-highlight-mid.jpg (the same state in the sync demo). Clip: clips/040-sync-highlight-7s.mp4 (the state changes are the clip's content).

### T08 — Volume ramp: size and weight track vocal emphasis (48–58 s)
- **Mechanics:** the narration line "types sizes, weights and animation," sets the demo's own terms (~50 s: "sizes" stands larger and bolder than its neighbours). Then the line "so you can feel when my voice gets louder or softer" performs the mapping: the channel the film's own grid names "Font Size Range (percentage of the screen height)" (T05) is performed: "louder" grows transiently at its onset (~55.3 s, ev/t055-louder-onset.jpg, bound by the clip), and the persistent frame-verifiable end state is the shrink — at 56 s "or softer" sits visibly smaller and lighter while the line's earlier words hold full size (ev/t056-ramp-softer.jpg); at 55 s the ramp words are still white and unscaled (ev/t055-line-before-ramp.jpg). Size is a continuous-feeling ramp tied to the phrase's dynamics, not a per-word pop. Easing curve: UNMEASURED (no frame-level size series was extracted; the claim is the before/after states + the clip).
- **Why it works:** loudness becomes a spatial fact a Deaf viewer can see — the sentence about volume is itself set at two volumes.
- **Evidence:** ev/t050-types-sizes.jpg ("sizes" enlarged in its line) · ev/t055-line-before-ramp.jpg (the ramp line before its size states) · ev/t055-louder-onset.jpg ("louder" at its onset) · ev/t056-ramp-softer.jpg ("or softer" shrunk, the end state). ### T09 — Speaker-color coding: persistent color as identity (58–75 s and throughout the scene demos)
- **Mechanics:** color identifies the speaker and persists across that speaker's lines, independent of the transient size/weight emphasis: the narration demo announces it in a green line ("Now, colors will distinguish characters," 58.32–60.60 s; "so Deaf people instantly know who's speaking," 60.96–62.92 s — at 62 s its spoken prefix is green and its last word still white, ev/t062-whos-speaking.jpg). A combination card follows (63.40–66.18 s: "This is what it looks like when we bring all those features together") before the scene demos combine all three devices. In the Forrest Gump demonstration (header on frame: FORREST GUMP · CHICAGO HEARING SOCIETY · CAPTION WITH INTENTION · TEST #22) the drill sergeant's lines are GREEN (ev/t070-gump-this.jpg) and Gump's reply is ORANGE (ev/t073-gump-drill-line.jpg, "Drill" enlarged at its stress). In the Toy Story demonstration (TEST #11) Buzz's lines are MAGENTA (ev/t084-buzz-rex.jpg · ev/t098-my-ship.jpg) and Rex's are green. The color is assigned per character, not per line.
- **Why it works:** identity survives cuts and off-screen lines — the viewer knows who is speaking before reading a word, which is T02's #3 answered as a system property.
- **Evidence:** ev/t060-colors-distinguish.jpg (the green announcement line) · ev/t062-whos-speaking.jpg · ev/t070-gump-this.jpg (sergeant, green) · ev/t073-gump-drill-line.jpg (Gump, orange — the color change IS the speaker change) · ev/t084-buzz-rex.jpg (Buzz, magenta). ## 5. Goods and bads

### 5.1 Goods (timestamp-bound)
- **G1 — The film is its own demonstration (38–67 s).** The mechanics section does not describe the system; it performs it on the narration itself, so every claim is checkable against the line that states it.
- **G2 — The defect list is enumerated and falsifiable (13–22 s).** Three numbered shortcomings, each with a timestampable exhibit (the conventional bar running underneath) — the "before" state never leaves the screen during the problem section.
- **G3 — Famous-scene transfer proof (67–75 s · 82–99 s).** The system is shown on Forrest Gump and Toy Story — scenes the audience already knows — so the delta the system adds is visible against memory, and the test headers (TEST #22 · TEST #11) frame them as tests, not decoration.
- **G4 — Testimonials run on the product (76–109 s).** The viewers' own lines are captioned by the system with speaker chips, so the reaction footage doubles as demonstration (T12).
- **G5 — One stake, held (24–27 s).** The "1971" numeral holds ~3 s with nothing competing — the film's argument has a single memorable number and it is given the film's biggest type.
- **G6 — The close spends its stall on the address (110–113.7 s).** The end card holds the name + URL for ~3.7 s of black — the only call to action, given the film's only dwell.

### 5.2 Bads (timestamp-bound)
- **B1 — No legend for the color code (58–75 s).** Speaker colors are announced ("colors will distinguish characters," ~60 s) but never keyed: that the sergeant is green and Gump orange is inferable only from the scene itself. A viewer meeting the system cold must reverse-engineer the mapping the film claims is instant.
- **B2 — The tool montage outruns reading (29–37 s).** Waveform, color wheel, timeline, and layout diagram each hold under ~2 s; the color wheel — the one element that would have answered B1 — is gone before it can be read.
- **B3 — Long demo lines exit the frame (52–56 s).** The volume-ramp line is wider than the frame and slides: its first words leave the left edge while later words are still unspoken, so early words' dwell ends by exit, not by line completion — a readability cost the system's own demo incurs (the lane's provisional readability figures, 12–15 chars/s and 0.8–1.2 s minimum display, are inspection questions here per P-A4, not a verdict — but the exit-by-slide is visible on the frames regardless of any figure).
- **B4 — The adoption claim is not in the film (99–113.7 s).** The entry text claims Oscars rule-book adoption and 196-language support; the film's entire second half is testimonials and it closes on the URL — the strongest claim the campaign makes gets no second of screen evidence.
- **B5 — Provenance: the canonical film is a trade-press upload (whole film).** The bound case film survives as ReelChicago Reel360's "PR FILM" upload, not on the entrant's or client's own channel; the entrant's own posting was not locatable this cycle, so the packet's specimen of record is a re-upload whose version fidelity to the LIA submission is UNVERIFIED.
- **B6 — Emphasis peaks are unkeyed too (70 s · 74.5 s).** Size peaks land on stressed words ("this", "Goddamnit"), but nothing distinguishes a stress peak from a volume peak — the same channel (size) carries two meanings, and the film never separates them.
