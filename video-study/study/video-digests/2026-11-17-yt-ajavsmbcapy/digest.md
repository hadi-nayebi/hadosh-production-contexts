## 5. Goods and bads

### Goods
- **G1 — One substance, every job (0:28–8:08).** The dot grid is title (T03), tally (T09), and outro (T22); tiles serve modern data (T16) and founding history (T17). The viewer learns two representations in eight minutes and reads everything with them.
- **G2 — The margin is shown before it is erased (2:37–2:52).** T11 draws every state's losing share as dotted matter before T13 states the rule that discards it — the film's central unfairness is demonstrated, not asserted.
- **G3 — Sourced graphics at the surprising points (0:54, 3:59, 6:17).** The Gallup trend is named on its chart (T05); the influence grid credits Mixon, Duquette, Cebula 2017 on its face (T16); the Allen quote card prints "Source: Alexander Keyssar" (T20). The three least believable exhibits carry their provenance.
- **G4 — The worked pair is staged fairly before it is indicted (1:24–2:19).** TX/VT is shown near-proportional at the House stage (T07) before the +2 step creates the distortion (T08) — the film isolates the exact operation it criticises.
- **G5 — History is load-bearing, not decorative (4:21–6:30).** S4's dated sequence (three-fifths scoreboard T18 → reform timeline T19 → defenders' quotes T20) is what entitles the coda's generalisation at 7:46; cut S4 and the ending is an opinion.
- **G6 — Numbers arrive as verdicts (1:51, 2:51, 3:58).** The figures that matter (210,000 ringed; 1% → 100%; 51×) land after their diagrams are built, each as the last element of its scene — the numeral is the punchline of a constructed proof.

### Bads
- **B1 — Sponsor frame around an explainer (throughout; 7:58–8:08).** The "Presented by ABSOLUT" watermark sits top-right in frame after frame (visible in ev/ frames) and the film closes on a presenter/sponsor card — a spirits brand's frame around a civics lesson is a credibility cost the film never acknowledges.
- **B2 — The smallest influence tiles are illegible (3:59–4:12).** Priced from a native-resolution crop inspection of ev/t242-influence-tiles.jpg (Amendment A12 cl.1 form): mid-size tiles resolve at native scale (Kentucky, Idaho, New York, Wyoming all readable in crop), but the smallest handful of tiles — e.g. the micro-tile beside Nevada — cannot be read at any available scale, so those states' identities rest on the chart's design, not on a read. The chart's tail is carried by tile size alone.
- **B3 — The threshold grid-map is hard to parse mid-formation (2:19–2:27).** In ev/t142-threshold-270.jpg the state outlines overlap the dot field and the 270 marker is partly covered by the accumulating regions — the film's most important diagram is least legible at the moment it forms (it resolves as the draw completes, but the formation beat itself is cluttered).
- **B4 — The total is never given (2:19).** The narration names the threshold ("270 or more", transcript 2:19) but neither the narration nor any frame states the 538 total it is a majority of — a viewer cannot reconstruct the fraction the whole film is about.
- **B5 — The two exceptions are never named (2:43).** Winner-take-all is stated for "almost every state" (transcript 2:43); Maine and Nebraska, the exceptions a curious viewer will immediately ask about, are never mentioned in 488 s.
- **B6 — S5's restatement sag (6:30–7:10).** T21's beneficiary tiles + demographic bars re-price S2's ratio conclusion in new charts for ~40 s before the 1948 callback (T22) restarts the argument — the only stretch where the film repeats itself instead of advancing.
