## 3. Technique catalogue

**T01 — Question-first cold open.** *Timestamps: 0:00–0:11.7 (the film's first sentence, at 0.48 s).* Mechanics: before any title, branding sequence or scene-setting, the narration asks the film's whole question — "When AI handles everything, what remains for humans?" — over a dark, near-still visual (a face in shadow, a question-mark of smoke). Nothing else competes with the sentence for ~11 s. Why it works: the viewer is given the interpretive frame before a single spectacle image arrives, so every later scene is watched as evidence for or against an answer. Evidence: `ev/t005-opening-question-smoke.jpg` (the dark opening visual, question-mark smoke at right) · transcript [00:00.48].

**T02 — Kinetic title assembly.** *Timestamps: ~0:07–0:12, immediately after the opening question.* Mechanics: the title text assembles in motion over the first bedroom scene — words fly in with light streaks and particle glow ("The F…" and "24" caught mid-assembly at 10 s), then the settled card reads "The Future of AI in 2050 / 24 Hours in 2050" over a city-window scene. The channel's hourglass logo animates in the top-left during the same beat, and its watermark form (bottom-right) persists for the whole film. Why it works: the title is an event, not a card — its assembly energy carries the viewer from the dark question into the bright scenario without a cut to black. Evidence: `ev/t010-title-card.jpg` (mid-assembly state, labelled as such) · `frames/net010.jpg` (the settled card over the city window) · `clips/title-assembly-000-013.mp4` (0–13 s, the assembly in motion). Source text — specimen property.

**T03 — One-protagonist continuity casting (the continuity trick).** *Timestamps: throughout the domestic and work sections (first at ~0:20, still carrying the sunset walk at ~7:40).* Mechanics: the same generated man — same face, build and styling family — is cast in every home, wardrobe, commute, office and social scene, although every scene is an independently generated image. The film never cuts away to a narrator or a host; the day is lived by one continuous person. Why it works: it converts a slideshow of generated stills into a film about someone — the viewer's attachment and orientation survive every scene change because the face is the constant. (Its failure at the climax is priced in §5, B2.) Evidence: `ev/t075-smart-mirror-schedule-ui.jpg` (the protagonist at the mirror) · `frames/net020.jpg` · `frames/net190.jpg` · `frames/net460.jpg` (the same man across morning, office and evening).

**T04 — Beat-per-scene turnover at a metronome rate.** *Timestamps: throughout; measured in §4.* Mechanics: nearly every narration sentence receives its own new generated scene; scenes turn over at a near-constant rate (frame-difference census: a new high-change onset every ~3.0–3.5 s in every section) and join by dissolve (T05), under a slow push-in or drift inside most scenes. Why it works: the visual never lags the narration — each claim is pictured as it is spoken — while the constant rate keeps the turnover from feeling frantic. Evidence: §4 census table · `clips/dissolve-park-to-store-219-237.mp4` (two turnovers in 18 s).

**T05 — Dissolve stitching of generated clips.** *Timestamps: throughout; densest measurable clusters at 221–234 s and 451–455 s (scene-detector ramp clusters).* Mechanics: scene joins are cross-dissolves, not hard cuts — the outgoing generated scene fades as the incoming one resolves, so the join has no single cut frame. The standard scene detector consequently under-fires severely (3 candidate boundaries @0.35 across 610 s; §4). Why it works: a dissolve hides the two things generated footage cannot match — exact lighting and exact geometry — inside a blend the eye reads as a time-passage convention. Evidence: `clips/dissolve-park-to-store-219-237.mp4` (park → store dissolve, in motion) · `scene0.35.json` / `scene0.20.json` (the detector record).

**T06 — Diegetic holographic UI as exposition.** *Timestamps: 0:31 (mirror health scan) · 1:09–1:27 (smart-mirror schedule) · 1:40 (wristband health hologram) · 2:42 (hologram meeting) · 4:37 (clinic body scan) · 5:30 (city control wall).* Mechanics: the film's claims about AI are shown as interfaces inside the scenes — a mirror that tiles the day's schedule, weather and meetings around the protagonist's reflection; a wristband projecting health rings; a meeting table whose far side is a hologram; a clinic scan rendering organs in a glass cylinder. The narration names the function while the interface performs it on screen. Why it works: exposition becomes set dressing — the viewer absorbs "AI manages this" as a visible property of the world instead of a narrated assertion. (The interfaces' text does not survive close reading — §5, B3.) Evidence: `ev/t035-mirror-health-scan.jpg` · `ev/t075-smart-mirror-schedule-ui.jpg` · `ev/t100-wristband-health-hologram.jpg` · `ev/t165-hologram-meeting.jpg` · `ev/t285-clinic-body-scan.jpg` · `ev/t330-city-control-wall.jpg`.

**T07 — Verbal clock-stamps, never displayed.** *Timestamps: 11.72 s ("6:00 a.m. 2050") · 191.96 s ("It's only 10:00 a.m.") · 341.28 s ("As evening falls…") · 490.88 s ("Late at night…").* Mechanics: the day's time is told only in narration, at four sparse points. No clock graphic, HUD or timestamp appears in the frame at those moments (net frames at 10/190/340/490 s viewed: title card, office, sunset city, dark bedroom — no time display in any). Why it works — and its limit: the day is paced by life-domain (T08), not by the clock, so four verbal stamps are enough to keep the viewer oriented; but the device is weaker than Packet #4's on-screen clock register for a viewer who looks away — the stamp exists only in the audio. This is the measured contrast the A2 pairing was queued for. Evidence: transcript [00:11.72] · [03:11.96] · [05:41.28] · [08:10.88] · the net frames at all four stamp moments, viewed: `frames/net010.jpg` (title card, no time display) · `frames/net190.jpg` (office) · `frames/net340.jpg` (sunset city) · `frames/net490.jpg` (dark bedroom).

**T08 — Domain-episode segmentation.** *Timestamps: the nine sections in §4 (11.7 s – 582 s).* Mechanics: the day is sliced by life-domain — morning home · work · lunch + shopping · city systems · body + creativity · evening social — not into equal dayparts, and not into the queue's four named dayparts (Morning · Midday · Afternoon · Late night). Section lengths run 20–134 s (§4); a section changes when the domain changes, signalled by a narration pivot sentence ("The 2050 classroom…", "Afternoons are for the body…") plus a dissolve to a new location family. Why it works: each episode is a complete miniature claim (one domain, fully pictured), so the film can be entered, left and re-entered at any episode without losing the thread — the question (T01) is the only connective tissue required. Evidence: §4 section table (boundary cues logged per P-C1) · `frames/net260.jpg` (classroom, S5 onset) · `frames/net350.jpg` (sport, S6 onset).

**T09 — Enumerated-answer ending.** *Timestamps: 521.24 s ("First answer, time") · 531.28 s ("Second answer, affection") · 541.72 s ("Third answer, dreams") · thesis restated 551.44–561.44 s.* Mechanics: after the question returns (T12's first move), the film answers it as a spoken numbered list — three answers, each given one emblem visual: an hourglass for time (T10), held hands for affection, a stargazing figure for dreams — then a one-line thesis ("2050 isn't the year machines replaced humans. It's the year humans finally got to be fully human."). Why it works: enumeration makes the ending memorable and quotable; the list form promises completeness, and three is short enough to hold. Evidence: transcript [08:41.24] · [08:51.28] · [09:01.72] · `ev/t520-hourglass-time-answer.jpg` · `ev/t532-hands-affection-answer.jpg` · `ev/t545-stargazing-dreams-answer.jpg`.

**T10 — Brand motif literalised as an answer visual.** *Timestamps: watermark throughout; the literal hourglass at ~515–530 s.* Mechanics: the channel's mark is an hourglass (top-left logo at the title, bottom-right watermark on every frame sampled). When the first answer is "time", the film cuts to a hero shot of an ornate golden hourglass — the brand symbol appears inside the argument as its own illustration. Why it works: the motif has been on screen, peripheral, for eight minutes; its promotion to subject makes the answer feel discovered rather than inserted, and brands the film's most quotable beat. Evidence: `ev/t520-hourglass-time-answer.jpg` (the hourglass) · the watermark legible in `ev/t075-smart-mirror-schedule-ui.jpg` (bottom-right).

**T11 — Systems-ring recap tableau.** *Timestamps: 498–512 s, under "Yet right now, the biggest question appears" (504.88 s).* Mechanics: a slow zoom-out reveals a reclining figure encircled by a ring of glowing icon panels — robot arms, gears, circuits, a brain, a cloud — the day's whole machine world drawn as one ring around a resting human, resolving to the settled tableau at ~508 s. It is the film's only diagram-shaped shot, and it lands exactly where the narration sums the day up. Why it works: after 8 minutes of episodes, one composed frame re-presents the system as a single object — the recap is visual, not verbal. (The figure in the ring is not the protagonist — §5, B2.) Evidence: `ev/t508-day-recap-ring.jpg` (settled tableau) · `ev/t500-reclining-woman-night.jpg` (the same figure seconds earlier, ring not yet drawn) · `clips/ring-reveal-498-512.mp4` (the reveal in motion).

**T12 — Re-opened question coda (after the answers).** *Timestamps: 600.88–609.88 s — the film's last two sentences.* Mechanics: the answers (T09), the thanks and the subscribe ask (582–594 s) all complete — and then the film speaks again: "A day in 2050 comes to a close, but its question remains open. When machines handle everything, what will you do with your time?" — the opening question re-asked in second person, over a nebula field. The question's three placements are therefore: opened (0.48 s, third person) · returned (511.48 s, verbatim) · re-opened to the viewer (605.48 s, second person). Why it works: the viewer leaves holding the question personally, which is the channel's subscription argument. (Its placement after the subscribe ask is priced in §5, B5.) Evidence: transcript [10:00.88] · [10:05.48] · `ev/t605-final-nebula-question.jpg`.

**T13 — Neon-hologram texture system.** *Timestamps: throughout; densest in S2 and S5.* Mechanics: one texture grammar covers every domain — photoreal warm-lit generated scenes carrying cyan/magenta holographic overlays (rings, waveforms, icon tiles, scan lines) wherever AI is present in the scene. The overlay is the film's visual signature for "AI is here": it appears on the mirror, the wrist, the car, the classroom, the clinic and the city wall, in the same palette and glow treatment each time. Why it works: a single repeatable texture lets the viewer read a new scene instantly — glow means machine agency, warm light means human life — without any caption or narration cue. Evidence: `ev/t100-wristband-health-hologram.jpg` · `ev/t285-clinic-body-scan.jpg` · `ev/t330-city-control-wall.jpg`.

## 5. Goods and bads

### Goods
- **G1 — The question architecture is load-bearing, not decorative** (event: question at 0.48 s · echo 205.28 s · return 511.48 s · re-open 605.48 s). Every section is watched as evidence; the ending's answers land because the question was posed before any spectacle. Consequence: a viewer can state the film's point in one sentence after one viewing.
- **G2 — Metronome pacing keeps a 610 s catalogue legible** (measured: 99–116 wpm and ~1 beat onset/3.1 s in all sections, §4). Nothing is rushed; a viewer can leave and re-enter at any episode (T08) without losing the thread. Consequence: the film tolerates distracted viewing, which is its platform reality.
- **G3 — Exposition is shown as set dressing** (event: smart-mirror tiles at 75 s, `ev/t075…`). Claims arrive as visible properties of the world (T06/T13), so the film teaches its future without pausing to lecture. Consequence: the visual channel carries information the narration never has to spend words on.
- **G4 — The ending answers before it re-opens** (events: enumerated answers 521–551 s · thesis 551–561 s · re-open 605 s). The viewer is paid for the full runtime with a position, then handed the question personally. Consequence: the coda reads as an invitation, not a withholding.
- **G5 — One-protagonist casting makes generated footage feel filmed** (events: the same man at 20 s, 75 s, 190 s, 460 s). Continuity of person substitutes for continuity of set, which generated imagery cannot provide. Consequence: the day reads as one life, not a stock reel.

### Bads
- **B1 — The catalogue sprawls where the day-frame stops organising it** (span: S5, 261–351 s). Classroom, clinic, traffic and city systems are not dayparts and not a sequence; they are a second catalogue inside the day, and S6 (body + creativity) gets 20.1 s against S2's 134.2 s for no stated reason. Consequence: the middle third feels like a different, list-shaped film; the "24 hours" frame is doing no work there.
- **B2 — The continuity trick breaks at the climax** (events: ring tableau, 498–512 s, `ev/t508-day-recap-ring.jpg`; family bench, ~580 s, `frames/net580.jpg` — a man, woman and two children who are none of them the protagonist). The recap's central figure is a woman who has not appeared before, and the closing family is a third cast — at exactly the two beats where the film sums itself up, the protagonist the viewer has followed for eight minutes is absent. Consequence: the ending's emotional claim ("your day, your time") is illustrated by strangers.
- **B3 — Diegetic UI text is gibberish at native resolution** (event: mirror tiles at 75 s, `ev/t075-smart-mirror-schedule-ui.jpg`, read at 1080p). Tile labels read "Scheder", "Schedskar", "Meeting" under a weather icon, "Appointment" under a location pin — the interfaces are texture, not information, and dissolve under the close reading the film's own lingering shots invite. Consequence: T06's exposition works only at viewing distance; any freeze-frame exposes the device.
- **B4 — The climax is paced identically to the catalogue** (measured: S8 at 99 wpm, the film's slowest section; beat rate unchanged, §4). The three answers — the film's whole payoff — arrive at the same metronome rate as the lunch section, with no rate, pause or density change to mark them as the point. Consequence: a viewer can miss that the answers have started; the enumeration's numbering (T09) carries the entire signalling load.
- **B5 — The subscribe ask splits the ending in two** (events: thesis closes at 561 s · thanks + "Subscribe so you won't miss what's next" at 582–594 s · the final question then re-opens at 600–610 s). The film ends, sells, and then ends again. Consequence: the strongest line in the film (T12) plays after the channel's commercial beat, to whoever is still watching — the coda's audience is the remainder after the CTA's exit point.
- **B6 — Decorative numerals pose as facts** (events: car "arrives in 40 seconds" 136.60 s · assistant "summarizes a week's work in 10 seconds" 171.52 s · clinic scan "in a minute" 277.74 s · "accidents near zero" 316.76 s). Specific numbers are asserted throughout with no source, method or hedge — in a film whose subject is a speculative year, the numerals borrow the authority of measurement for what is set design. Consequence: the film teaches a habit — precision as decoration — that a critical viewer learns to discount, discounting the real structure (T01/T09) with it.
